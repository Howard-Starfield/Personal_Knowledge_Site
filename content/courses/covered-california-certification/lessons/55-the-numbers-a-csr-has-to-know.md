# The Numbers a CSR Has to Know

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

## What you will be able to do

- Sort a figure into one of three kinds - a rule you may quote, a figure the training hedged, or a figure that belongs to one consumer's case - and say which kind it is before you say it out loud
- Name the figures a representative may quote, and say where two different rules share the same number
- Show both sides of a hedged figure, with the body or the session that said each, and say which one governs
- Run the three clocks - the resend wait, the document deadline and the reinstatement - step by step
- Hold a money conversation without quoting a flat penalty figure
- Tell a case figure apart from a rule, and say what a representative who has memorised the case will get wrong next time

## Stage 1: Three kinds of number, and why the kind decides the answer

The hard part of handling figures on the phone is not remembering them. It is
picking the right kind of figure to say.

A representative has three kinds of number in front of them, and the three
behave differently:

| the kind                                         | what it is                                                             | what you do with it                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A rule you may quote**                         | a figure with a rule behind it - a deadline, a window, a count of days | quote it, and say what it governs                                         |
| **A figure the training hedged**                 | the training gave a number and then told you not to rely on it         | name both sources, say which one governs, and never present it as settled |
| **A figure that belongs to one consumer's case** | a date or an amount out of a worked example                            | do not quote it at all; work out the rule that produced it                |

The three kinds are not decoration. They decide what you say out loud. A rule is
something you can state flatly. A hedged figure has to arrive with its sources
attached, because the training and the official record do not agree and the
consumer deserves to know which one you are working from. A case figure is
something you should not say at all, because it belongs to somebody else's case
and the next consumer's numbers will be different.

There is a fourth thing that catches people, and it is not a fourth kind. It is
a **rule that shares its number with a different rule**. Sixty days is one such
number. Ninety days is another. Thirty days is a third. The number is not the
trap; the rule behind the number is. When a consumer quotes a figure back at
you, the useful question is not "is that right" but "which rule is that".

The test that does the work, and it is worth memorising because it does the work
on its own:

> **Before you say a number, ask which of the three kinds it is.**

## Stage 2: The figures you may quote, and where they collide

Here is the set a representative may quote, with what each one governs and where
it comes from. The third column matters as much as the first two: some of these
are published and some are the training's own, and that changes how firmly you
may say them.

| Figure               | What it governs                                                                                                       | Where it comes from                                                          |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **10 business days** | how long to wait after the last transaction feed before a resend can be escalated                                     | the training; the official record's own 10 business days is a different rule |
| **60 days**          | the general special-enrolment window from a qualifying life event                                                     | the official record                                                          |
| **90 days**          | the window to file an appeal, counted from the date the eligibility decision was mailed                               | the official record                                                          |
| **90 days**          | the special-enrolment window for losing Medi-Cal coverage                                                             | the official record                                                          |
| **90 days**          | the appeal hearing and the decision that follows it                                                                   | the official record                                                          |
| **95 days**          | the reasonable opportunity period for most documents                                                                  | the training; the official record publishes no number of days                |
| **155 days**         | the same period for proof of income                                                                                   | the training; the official record publishes no number of days                |
| **60 days**          | the extension you can give on a document request, through manual verifications, and only before the item goes overdue | the training; the official record publishes no number of days                |
| **30 calendar days** | the window to get a termination for a missing document reinstated, counted from the date the cover ended              | the training; the official record publishes no number of days                |

Now the collisions. They are taught as collisions on purpose, because a list of
numbers teaches the numbers and not the trap.

### Sixty days does two jobs

Sixty days is the window to apply after a qualifying life event, and it is also
the extension you can give on a document request. Two rules, one number. A
consumer with a document request is not waiting out an enrolment window, and a
consumer with a life event is not asking for an extension. The trap is carrying
one meaning into the other, and it is easy to do, because the two routes are
both live in the same week.

### Ninety days does four jobs

Ninety days is the appeal filing window, counted from the date the eligibility
decision was mailed; the time the hearing and the decision can take; the
special-enrolment window after losing Medi-Cal coverage; and the written
notice an employer has to give at least 90 days before an HRA plan year starts.
None of the four is the same rule as any of the others.

### Ten business days is not the behavioural-health promise

The training's 10 business days is the wait after the last transaction feed
before a resend can be escalated. The official record carries a 10 business
days too, and it is about something else entirely: a health plan has to offer
the member a return behavioural-health appointment within 10 business days of a
referral or a first visit. The two rules meet only in the number. The same
session uses 10 business days a third time, for how long a carrier may take to
return the payment for a plan that was cancelled as never effective
(07-56-48 00:51:55-00:52:06).

### Thirty days does three jobs

Thirty calendar days from the date the cover ended is the window for getting a
termination for a missing document reinstated. Thirty business days is how long
a complaint takes to be answered. Thirty days is how long a consumer has to
report a change of income. One is a clock a consumer is trying to beat, one is
a service standard the marketplace is held to, and the third is the consumer's
own duty.

## Stage 3: The figures the training hedged, and the sources that disagree

These are the figures where two sources give two answers. The rule for this
stage is simple: **name both figures, say which body or session said each, say
which one governs and why, and then say what you actually tell the consumer.**
A representative who has been shown one number is being set up to mislead
somebody; a representative who has been shown both, with the sources attached,
can answer.

### The California penalty

Three sets of numbers, from three places, and the body that decides is none of
the three that quote it.

| Figure                 | What the training said                                                                                           | What the official record says                                                                                                   | What you say to the consumer                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A single adult         | up to \$900, and it can be more than \$900 (10-41-17 00:48:21-00:49:17), which is the training's own rough range | the penalty page publishes a minimum of \$950 per adult and \$450 per child under 18, and attaches no tax year to those amounts | name the Franchise Tax Board, name the tax year, and point at the FTB's Penalty Estimator                                                               |
| A family of four       | up to \$2,700 for a family of four, and it could be more than that (10-41-17 00:48:57-00:49:08)                  | the December 2025 release publishes at least \$900 per adult, \$450 per dependent child, and \$2,700 for a family of four       | the training is clear that the \$2,700 is not a flat sum for any household - it is a 2026-cycle figure built out of the per-adult and per-child amounts |
| The affordability test | not covered in these two sessions                                                                                | the penalty page carries an 8.05% threshold, and that threshold is the only figure on the page labelled tax year 2026           | name the year and the body every time you mention it                                                                                                    |
| Who computes it        | the training says the Franchise Tax Board computes and collects the penalty, not Covered California              | the penalty page sends consumers to the FTB for their own figure                                                                | the FTB's Penalty Estimator is where the household's own number is                                                                                      |

The training's own instruction about the flat figure is blunt: you never want to
say \$900 (10-41-17 00:48:21-00:48:36). Two official bodies publish different
amounts, and only the 8.05% affordability threshold carries the tax year 2026
label - the penalty amounts on the penalty page carry no tax year at all. That
is why the answer to a consumer is not a number but a route.

### The document period: one session or the other

Two of the owner's own sessions describe the same period differently, and a
representative trained on the earlier one will tell a consumer the wrong
number.

| Session         | What it says                                                                                      | Which one governs                                                                                         |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 15 October 2026 | a flat 95 days to submit documentation, with no carve-out for income (10-56-52 00:52:43-00:53:10) | no - it is superseded                                                                                     |
| 22 October 2026 | 95 days for most documents, 155 days for proof of income (07-56-48 01:36:12-01:38:16)             | yes - it is the later statement, and it is the one the training repeats in the recap at 02:00:09-02:00:30 |

The later statement governs because it is the later one, and because it is the
version the training repeats when it summarises the period. Neither session's
figure is published: the official record's pages on documents, income and
citizenship say what may be asked for and what the document has to show, and
they say nothing at all about how long the consumer has.

### The length of an extension

Three statements, and the session contradicts itself.

| Source                                              | What it says                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 22 October, 01:39:49-01:40:17                       | a reasonable timeframe might be 14 days or 30 days, and the training adds that there is nothing definitive to stand by      |
| 22 October, 01:41:40 and 01:43:15                   | go to manual verifications, and you will give them 60 days                                                                  |
| 15 October, 00:52:48-00:53:10 and 02:35:00-02:35:49 | extensions up to 60 days, and two extensions of 30 days each, both of which have to be requested before the 95 days run out |

The training does not settle this, and says so: the length lives in the
knowledge article, and the people in the room are told they do not have to
memorise it (07-56-48 01:36:31-01:36:39). So the representative's job is not to
pick a number out of the three. It is to read the article, give the extension
through manual verifications, and be honest that the length is settled by the
article rather than by anybody's memory on a call.

What is settled, and is not in doubt, is the shape of the route. It is offered
when the consumer asks for it or the deadline is close, never automatically. It
is arranged before the item goes overdue; once the item is overdue the route
is shut. And it is recorded for each applicable person separately, not once for
the household.

### The provable error

One more thing the training hedged, and this one is a door rather than a
number. A reinstatement past the 30 days is available where the document was
uploaded in time and nobody passed it. The training's own reason is that this
is a provable error, and it says the door is open even when it has been past
30 days (07-56-48 02:05:39-02:05:52 and 02:12:06).

And an approved reinstatement restores the cover from the original end date, as
though the termination had never happened. It does not erase the bill: the
consumer still owes the premiums for the months that are reinstated
(10-41-17 00:50:55-00:52:43).

### The 145 that became 155

Once, in the middle of the explanation, the figure came out as 145, and it was
corrected in the same breath to 155 (07-56-48 01:36:12-01:36:31). It is worth
knowing about for two reasons. It shows what a self-correction looks like in
practice, which is worth copying. And it is a warning: a figure you heard once
may have been heard wrong, and a representative who heard 145 once and 155
twice is entitled to be unsure.

## Stage 4: The figures that belong to one consumer's case

The third kind. These are the numbers that are in the training because the
training walked through a case, and they are the easiest to quote wrongly,
because they sound exactly like rules.

| Figure           | What it actually was                                                                                                        | Where it came from                      |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| 14 December      | one consumer's last day to add health and dental cover                                                                      | 07-56-48 01:08:38-01:09:03 and 02:18:05 |
| 1 January        | that consumer's start date                                                                                                  | 07-56-48 01:09:03                       |
| 18 January       | that consumer's proof-of-income deadline                                                                                    | 07-56-48 02:18:20                       |
| 30 October       | the call-back date once ten business days were up from that consumer's feed date                                            | 07-56-48 01:05:05 and 02:17:58          |
| 7/10, 7/21, 7/31 | the dates in the other reinstatement case - termination, documents uploaded, cover ended                                    | 10-41-17 00:21:45-00:31:52              |
| \$1,400          | that consumer's binder payment                                                                                              | 07-56-48 00:52:28-00:52:58              |
| \$100,000        | the minimum-essential-coverage example: a person earning that is single, so the Medi-Cal item reads pending or not verified | 07-56-48 01:56:51                       |
| over \$5,000     | the duplicate case's claims                                                                                                 | 10-41-17 01:02:45                       |
| \$1,200          | one consumer's actual penalty, quoted to show that the penalty varies                                                       | 10-41-17 00:48:36-00:48:48              |
| \$20,000         | a hypothetical penalty, quoted for the same reason                                                                          | 10-41-17 00:49:17-00:49:20              |

The point to make is this: the training session contains exactly one instance
of the 60-day rule, and 14 December is that rule applied to one event on 15
October. A representative who remembers "December 14" instead of "60 days from
the event" has memorised a case, and will get the next one wrong. The same goes
for 18 January, for 30 October, and for the rest of the column.

### The 30, 10 and 45 minutes

One more set of numbers, and the easiest of the lot to get wrong, because they
sound like a rule and they are not one at all. A consumer complains about how
long the call took: changing a preferred method of contact became a 30-minute
process, and a 10-minute call turned into a 45-minute one
(07-56-48 00:47:57-00:48:16).

The trainer's reply is the explanation. To change a preferred method of contact
you have to complete an entire report a change, which made the call awkward for
the consumer and for the representative, and the state built the mini report a
change and the case overview hub to stop it happening. So there is no rule of 30
minutes, no rule of 10 minutes and no rule of 45 minutes anywhere. They are one
consumer's complaint, and the right response to the complaint is the route -
the mini report a change, and the case overview hub - not a number.

The same stretch explains why the clocks in this lesson matter. The state
implemented the required case review and the report a change for its own staff
too, not only for the service centre (07-56-48 00:46:51-00:47:10). Lessons 15,
16, 22 and 37 cover the routes and the review.

## Stage 5: The three clocks, step by step

Each clock below is a sequence a representative can actually follow. The order
matters more than the numbers, because in each one the number only means
something at the right point in the sequence.

### The resend clock

1. The trigger is a disagreement between the carrier's record and yours, or the
   carrier cannot see the consumer as active at all. A disagreement is necessary
   and it is not sufficient.
2. Find the date of the **last transaction feed**.
3. Count ten business days from it.
4. The tenth business day has to be **completely over**. If it is not, the
   escalation is not available today.
5. Tell the consumer to **call back** on the next working day. You do not call
   them, and you do not raise the escalation early.
6. When they call back and it is still wrong, escalate for the resend.

The training is blunt about step 5: if the consumer calls back and there is a
carrier representative on the line, that is when the escalation happens, and
until then they wait (07-56-48 00:59:00-01:00:29 and 01:05:05-01:07:44).

### The document clock

1. A pending status means the application could not verify something by itself.
2. Read the deadline **off the record**: 95 days for most documents, 155 days
   for proof of income.
3. Offer an extension only if the consumer asks for one or the deadline is
   close. Never automatically.
4. Give it by going to **manual verifications**, where the training says you
   will give them 60 days.
5. Before the deadline, never after. Once the item shows overdue the route is
   shut, even for a consumer who never asked.
6. If the deadline passes with nothing acceptable uploaded, the system can end
   the cover by itself.

Steps 3 to 5 are the part of this clock the training hedges, and Stage 3 says
what to do about that: read the article for the length, and never quote the
length as though it were settled.

### The reinstatement clock

1. Find the date the cover actually **ended** - the end date on the case.
2. Count 30 calendar days from it.
3. Get the documents uploaded **and reviewed** inside those 30 days.
4. If a document was uploaded in time and nobody passed it, that is a **provable
   error**, and reinstatement is available past the 30 days.
5. You ask the lead two questions: is the document acceptable, and if it is,
   may the case be escalated for reinstatement. The lead pulls the document up
   and decides. You do not judge it.
6. An approved reinstatement puts the cover back as though the termination had
   never happened, and the consumer still owes the premiums for the months that
   are reinstated.

Step 1 is the one that gets skipped. The clock starts at the end date on the
case, not at the date of the call (07-56-48 02:01:40-02:02:45).

## Stage 6: The money conversation, and what you actually say

This is the conversation where a wrong number does the most damage, because the
consumer writes it down and plans around it.

1. **You do not give out tax advice, and the training said so.** The rule behind
   everything else in this list: no flat number, no promise, and if the
   consumer's own figure comes back different from the rough one, the answer is
   an apology and the body that owns the calculation, not an explanation of the
   gap (10-41-17 00:48:51-00:48:54). The penalty page says the same thing in
   its own words: for a penalty amount, contact the Franchise Tax Board or a
   tax professional, because Covered California cannot provide tax advice.
2. **Never quote a flat number.** The training's own instruction is that you
   never want to say \$900.
3. **Name the body.** The Franchise Tax Board computes and collects the
   penalty. Covered California does not.
4. **Name the tax year.** The penalty depends on the year it is for, and only
   the 8.05% affordability threshold on the penalty page carries a tax year
   label at all.
5. **Point the consumer at the FTB's Penalty Estimator** for their own figure.
   That is the number they should plan around, because it is their number.
6. **If you have to say something rough, attach the hedge.** The training's own
   rough range is up to \$900 for a single person, up to \$2,700 for a family of
   four, and it can be more or less than that.

Step 6 is the only thing you may say if the consumer insists on a number, and
the hedge is not optional. A representative who says "up to \$2,700" and stops
there has given the consumer a promise the training told them not to give.

## Stage 7: How to hold the numbers

These are aids, not claims. Each one is a handle for a figure, and none of them
is something you say to a consumer.

- **Business ten, calendar the rest.** Ten business days is the only business-day
  figure in the set. The 30-day reinstatement, the 60-day window and the
  90-day windows all count calendar days. The one exception is the complaint
  answer time, 30 business days, which belongs to a different route.
- **Ninety-five for papers, one-fifty-five for pay.** The document you cannot
  afford to be late on is the one with the long clock.
- **Thirty from the end, not from the call.** The reinstatement clock starts at
  the end date on the case.
- **Sixty wears two hats.** Sixty days is both the window to apply after a
  qualifying event and the extension you can give. The trap is carrying one
  meaning into the other.
- **The case dates are not rules.** 14 December, 18 January and 30 October are
  one consumer's. A representative who can recite them has memorised a case.
- **Nine hundred is a ceiling, not a quote.**

And the test that closes the lesson, because every one of the traps above is a
version of it:

> **Before you say a number, ask which of the three kinds it is.**

Three notes on the sources behind this lesson, so that you do not over-read it.

First, the figures. The 95 days, the 155 days, the 60-day extension and the
30-day reinstatement are the training's own, and the official record publishes no
number of days for any of them. The 60-day and 90-day enrolment and appeal
windows are published. The penalty amounts are published, but two bodies
publish different amounts and the body that computes and collects is neither
the marketplace nor the training.

Second, the disagreement. Where two sources give two answers - the document
period, the length of an extension, the penalty - this lesson shows both and
says which one governs. It does not smooth them over, because a representative
who has been shown one number is being set up to mislead somebody.

Third, the recording. The dates and amounts in Stage 4 are one consumer's, and
no question in this lesson depends on them. The 30, 10 and 45 minutes are one
consumer's complaint, and no rule anywhere attaches to them.
