# Onboarding Help and Technical Troubleshooting

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

## What you will be able to do

- Sort a blocked request into the shape it really is: cannot get through, cannot get in, or cannot finish.
- Work the ladder of fixes in order, and tell a device problem from an account problem.
- Recognise the point where a fix stops being a setting and becomes a ticket, and hand it over properly.
- Keep a blocked task from costing the person something while the real fix is worked on.
- Say only what you actually know, and give a time for the part you do not.

## Step 1: Sort the symptom before you touch anything

This session was an onboarding help session: new people on a team, one morning, a training lead working down a list of people who were stuck. Nothing about it was a consumer call, and the middle of it was a person doing a knowledge check. What makes it worth studying is the shape of the help, because the same three shapes arrive on a service centre line.

**Cannot get through** — the audio and video. A camera greyed out in the meeting. A microphone that is not working, so a person sounds muffled and thin.

**Cannot get in** — the system itself. A person could not reach the internal system, and the guide they had been sent did not match what their screen showed. Their own suspicion was the name on the account.

**Cannot finish** — the task that the first two are blocking. A payment setup with a deadline the next day, and a benefits choice with a deadline the week after, where neither page would open.

Three questions sort almost anything of this kind, and they are worth asking before you suggest anything: what exactly happens, what has already been tried, and when did it last work.

## Step 2: The ladder of fixes you can hand over

For the cannot-get-through shape, the session worked down a ladder, in this order.

1. **The computer's own settings.** Allow the camera and the microphone, and check the user name on the account.
2. **The join options.** When you click to join, does it offer to show your camera? Use the new link you were sent.
3. **Unplug the device and plug it back in.** The person in the session had already done this, and it was accepted as done.
4. **Clear the cookies.**
5. **Restart the whole machine** — after the cookies, not before.

**The input check is not the permissions check.** Two people in that session had their microphone attached to their camera rather than to their microphone. That is why the sound was muffled and why a person heard themselves as far away. Permissions being switched on did not fix it; changing which device was actually feeding the microphone did.

**One more thing about the cookies.** The machine was struggling because too many sites were signed in at once — the internal ones and coveredca.gov among them. After clearing the cookies, bring up the application that was failing first. That is the one being diagnosed, and bringing it up first is how you find out whether the fix worked.

**The ladder is a ladder.** Start at the bottom and work up. Do not open at the restart.

## Step 3: When the fix is a ticket, not a setting

The cannot-get-in shape is different. When the access itself is wrong — permissions that have not been granted, or an identifier on the account that does not match — there is no setting on the person's screen that will fix it. The help you can give is the hand-off.

In the session, the answer to the question "is there someone I can talk to, do I call tech support" was to submit the ticket for the person while they were still on the call. Not to point at a department. Not to hand over a number to ring themselves.

**The trap in the inbox.** That person had reported the problem, and the only thing that had come back was a message about the deadline. A reminder and an answer look the same in an inbox, and they are not the same thing: the reminder was about the task, and the person still had no answer about the problem they had reported.

So when someone says they have already been in touch and nothing happened, the useful question is not what else they could try. It is whether what came back was an answer, or only another reminder.

And when the person says they have tried everything, do not walk them down the ladder again. You have heard the ladder. The fix has moved past it.

## Step 4: Do not let a blocked task cost the person something

The cannot-finish shape is where a good outcome gets lost. In the session, a payment setup had a deadline the next day and a benefits choice had a deadline the week after, and neither page would open.

Two separate things were said, and both were needed.

**The deadline was a reminder, not a requirement.** Nothing was lost by missing it. That removes the cost.

**There was a fallback.** The person was still going to be paid by cheque whether or not the direct payment was ever set up. That removes the gap.

**And the real fix still went on the ticket.** The workaround and the fix are not alternatives; the session did both.

The transferable shape: when someone is blocked, sort the deadline into one of two kinds — a requirement, or a reminder — and say which one it is out loud. Then find the route that leaves them whole while the blocker is worked on. A person who is told a deadline is a requirement and that you will sort it out later has been handed two problems instead of one.

## Step 5: Say only what you actually know

Two answers in that session were honest about a gap, and both were useful.

**"I don't know yet."** A person wanted to request days off in advance and needed to know who their supervisor was. The answer was that the supervisor was not known yet, with an expectation of knowing by the following week. That is a real answer: it says what is not known and when it should be.

**The time card.** It was the end of the pay period. The instruction was to check that the time card looked right and needed no changes, by the end of the shift, and to clock out at 4:30 before approving it. The recording is not clear about who gives the final approval — the speaker said both that each person approves their own and that they would approve it for them — so the safe reading is that the card is checked at the end of the shift and then approved, and that you confirm which of the two applies rather than assuming it.

**The resources are usually already there.** The people in that session had every note and every resource they needed. A large part of the help was pointing at what they already had, and telling them there was no need to rush.

## Step 6: Where a consumer's blocked problem goes

The same three shapes turn up on a consumer's call, and the routing for each is set by what the service centre is there to do, not by what you can fix on the call.

**Cannot get in — the account.** The official record groups a self-service guide under creating or accessing an account, resetting a password and managing personal information. That is the route a consumer is pointed to, and a representative can walk a consumer through it rather than doing it for them.

**Cannot get through to the carrier.** The official record carries a topic guide for finding the phone numbers, websites and billing contacts of a health, dental or vision plan. The split the service centre works to is that appointments, claim status, payment, and picking a doctor or a specialist belong to the carrier. Its own lane is helping a consumer learn about and buy insurance, deciding whether they qualify for financial assistance, submitting the application, helping them enrol, and sending them on their way to the carrier.

**Cannot get in to the payment.** The service centre neither takes payment nor handles billing, and it never holds a consumer's card details. The one money-adjacent thing it does is escalate, and escalation is reserved for narrow cases where a consumer gets money back from their insurer.

**The rule underneath all three.** Every route has to end with the consumer somewhere they can act, and with you having told them where that is. A blocked consumer who has a clear next step and a point of contact is not a blocked consumer.

2. The ladder of fixes, in order, and the point at which cookies and a restart come in.
3. Why a microphone attached to the camera is a different problem from a permission that is switched off.
4. Why the application that was failing is the one to bring up first after clearing cookies.
