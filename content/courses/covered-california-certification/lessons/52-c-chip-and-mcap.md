# C-CHIP and MCAP

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

The session these two presentations came from is the longest in the course, but the teaching in it is short and sits at the two ends: the children's programme first, and the pregnancy programme much later.

## What you will be able to do

- Say what the children's programme is, where it runs, and every condition a child has to meet for it.
- Explain what happens when a consumer declines the children's programme, and what a change to their application needs.
- Name the pregnancy programme, and work out the household size for a pregnant consumer.
- State how long the pregnancy cover runs, and what does not bring it to an end.
- Say what has to be said before a consumer opts out of the pregnancy programme.

## Step 1: Neither programme is Medi-Cal

The training's headline for both presentations was the same: **do not describe either programme to a consumer as Medi-Cal.** It may look like Medi-Cal and work the same way, but each is its own scheme with its own eligibility rules and its own path in.

Two names to keep apart:

- **C-CHIP** — the children's programme. The training called it the county children's scholarship programme; the official record calls it the County Children's Health Initiative Program, which it writes as CCHIP. The course uses C-CHIP.
- **MCAP** — the pregnancy programme, which the training expanded as the Medical Access Program.

**Where the recording does not settle a question.** In one stretch of the session the training said that MCAP, despite the Medi-Cal in its name, should be treated as a third-party scheme rather than a county one — and in the same breath told enrollers to think of it as tied to the county. The recording never names a body that administers MCAP, so this lesson does not name one either `[00:05:30]`. The children's programme is different: there the training does name the route, and puts the consumer onto their county's C-CHIP office.

## Step 2: The children's programme: who qualifies

It is a health insurance scheme giving children affordable and comprehensive medical, mental and vision cover `[00:00:40]`. The conditions the training listed are these:

- The child lives in one of the **three counties of San Mateo, San Francisco and Santa Clara**. No other county `[00:00:33]`.
- Age: **from birth up to the 19th birthday** — the training's wording. The official record puts the same limit as children under 19.
- Household income in the band **above 266% of the federal poverty level, and no more than 322%** of it `[00:01:12]`.
- The child must be **a U.S. citizen, a U.S. national, or lawfully present** `[00:01:03]`.
- The child has a **social security number** — except for newborns, and except for children whose social security number is still pending `[00:01:29]`.
- The child does **not** already have minimum essential coverage in place, and is **neither incarcerated nor deceased** `[00:01:38]`.

Two more things about it:

- **Duration.** Eligibility lasts **12 months**, and it still lasts 12 months where the family's percentage of the poverty level goes up and the child would then be eligible for financial help with a monthly premium `[00:01:55]`.
- **Household treatment.** A household with a child who is eligible for the programme counts as a **Covered California only household** for service centre purposes `[00:00:51]`.

And its shape on the federal poverty level chart: in those three counties the Medi-Cal threshold for children sits higher than it does elsewhere. The training's way of putting it was to think of the threshold as a little higher for anyone in those counties, while the cover itself is the children's programme and not Medi-Cal `[00:05:14]`.

## Step 3: Declining the children's programme, and changes

**If the consumer declines it** `[00:03:06]`:

- They may enrol in a Covered California health plan, but **with no subsidy at all**.
- The decline **does not change the eligibility result** for the child, and it **does not create premium assistance** for them.
- If the family's percentage of the poverty level later goes **down**, the child may qualify for Medi-Cal instead.
- If the family stops the enrolment and comes back at a later date, the **original eligibility may still be in effect** for the child, provided the child still qualifies.

**If the consumer wants to move into it** from an unsubsidised Covered California plan, you must tell the consumer that they need to **contact their county's C-CHIP office** `[00:02:47]`.

**Changes to the application.** The training was explicit that a change to the household's application is something the service centre does itself, **without passing the case to the county** `[00:02:33]`.

**Where the consumer gets help.** The programme's own line, which the training gives as 833-912-2447, and two knowledge base articles, 1696 and 1697, or the Covered California website `[00:03:50]`.

## Step 4: The pregnancy programme, and the household count

MCAP provides health coverage for pregnant individuals who are eligible and who meet the programme's requirements `[00:44:52]`.

The household rule is the one to hold on to: **when the household size for Medi-Cal for pregnant individuals is worked out, it is done by counting the pregnant consumer as one and then adding the number of children expected.** So a consumer who is pregnant with twins counts as **three** `[00:45:03]`.

## Step 5: How long the pregnancy cover runs

Consumers stay covered **throughout their pregnancy and through the 12-month extended postpartum coverage period**, and it goes on **however their income, citizenship or immigration status changes**. It applies to individuals enrolled in Medi-Cal for pregnant individuals and in MCAP `[00:45:18]`.

Two details around the end of it:

- A consumer is free to **bring their postpartum Medi-Cal cover to an end at any time**.
- But bringing minimum essential coverage **to an end of your own accord is not a qualifying life event**, so it cannot open a special enrolment period `[00:45:38]`.

The official record puts the same period the same way: full-scope Medi-Cal, Medi-Cal for Pregnant Individuals and MCAP each last the pregnancy plus 12 months after the pregnancy ends.

## Step 6: Opting out, and the programmes next door

**The opt-out rule** `[00:46:20]`: if a consumer opts out of the pregnancy programme, **they cannot return to MCAP during that pregnancy for any reason.**

Before the opt-out goes through, two things have to be said out loud:

1. The consumer needs to understand that they cannot return to MCAP.
2. The consumer needs to understand that a Covered California plan is open to them only during open enrolment or a qualifying life event, and that without one they will be uninsured.

And pregnancy itself is **not** a qualifying life event.

**Declaring the pregnancy is optional** `[00:47:56]`. A consumer who does not want to find out whether they qualify for MCAP, or for the Medi-Cal for Pregnant Individuals Program, simply does not declare the pregnancy in the application.

**The programmes next door** `[00:45:51]`:

- **Presumptive Eligibility**, abbreviated PE, and **Temporary Immediate Pregnancy Related Coverage**, which applies while the consumer's Medi-Cal application is being processed.
- The **Medi-Cal Access Infant Program**, which gives eligible babies born to MCAP members their own route to cover.

The official record adds what Presumptive Eligibility covers and how long it runs: prenatal care, care related to pregnancy loss and pregnancy-related prescriptions, up to 60 days, and it does not cover labour and delivery or other hospitalisation.
