# Ending Coverage: Voluntary and Involuntary Termination

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

## What you will be able to do

- Name the four parties who can bring coverage to an end, and say what each of them decides.
- Explain how a household's status decides which of those parties you may act for.
- Distinguish cancelling a plan from reporting a change to the household, and say how you know which one the consumer needs.
- Describe the voluntary route in full, including why it cannot be undone.
- Describe the involuntary route in full, including what the consumer can do about it.

## Step 1: Who can end cover

Four parties can bring a consumer's coverage to an end, and each of them decides something different. The distinction between them is what the rest of this lesson turns on.

**The consumer.** A consumer holds a legal right to bring their own health and dental cover to an end, for any reason and at any time (00:01:43–00:01:52). The training put this at the head of its list of things to keep in mind, and it sets the frame: the marketplace is not a gatekeeper of a consumer's decision to leave, and nothing about their reason changes what you do.

**The county.** Medi-Cal enrolment and household membership are county decisions. The service centre has no access to end a Medi-Cal enrolment (00:06:41–00:06:46), and a member leaving the household is a county action as well (00:08:30–00:08:39).

**The carrier.** The health plan holds the policy, so it decides the things the marketplace cannot decide for it: whether to approve a mid-month termination, and whether to hand back part of the premium if one is approved (00:16:51–00:19:00). The training was explicit that the carrier's agreement is not the same as the consumer getting their money back, and that a carrier representative who promises a refund has set the consumer up to fail (00:18:37–00:19:34).

**The escalation queue.** Four things go to the escalation queue rather than being handled in the call: a mid-month termination, a change to or removal of the primary, a retroactive end date, and a request to reinstate cover (00:20:49–00:21:38). None of those are your decision to make, and the queue is not bound to agree with you (00:19:24–00:19:35).

## Step 2: Household status decides who may act

Household status is what determines who can process a termination request, and you have to check it whenever anyone asks to end cover (00:06:19–00:06:38). There are three possible shapes:

- A household where every member is on Medi-Cal.
- A mixed-program family — the official record's word for it; the training called it a mixed household.
- A household of Covered California members.

**Where to look.** Open the enrollment dashboard on the consumer's case and go to the eligibility results listed further down that screen. That is where the programme eligibility for each person in the household is shown (00:07:12–00:07:27). The training pointed out that you are looking at the same eligibility results during a required case review, so the two jobs share a screen (00:07:29–00:07:36).

**What each status allows.**

| household status                | what the service centre may end | who handles the rest                                                                       |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| everyone on Medi-Cal            | nothing at all                  | the county ends the Medi-Cal enrolment                                                     |
| mixed-program family            | the Covered California members  | the county handles the Medi-Cal members, and any change to who is in the household         |
| Covered California members only | every member                    | the carrier acts where the policy itself has to, such as approving a mid-month termination |

**The mixed-program family in detail.** Three things were said about it, and each one is a different instruction:

1. If the Covered California members all want the same date, that is one process (00:08:06–00:08:23).
2. If they want different end dates, each member is processed separately (00:08:23–00:08:30).
3. The Medi-Cal members are not yours to end, and neither is the membership of the household (00:08:30–00:09:16).

**A Medi-Cal-only household** gets a referral, not a cancellation: point the consumer at their local county office and give them the number if they need it (00:07:44–00:07:55).

## Step 3: Cancel the plan, or report a change

Two different actions sit behind the word terminate, and which one applies is decided by a single question: is the member still in the household?

- **Cancel the plan.** The member's cover ends and they stay on the application, still a tax household member. The confirmation wording on the screen says exactly that: the process ends the health cover on the date chosen, while the consumer stays on the application, still a tax household member. The training added that the cancellation has to be documented in Salesforce afterwards (00:10:45–00:11:08).
- **Report a change.** The member leaves the household. That is a county action, because household membership is not yours to change (00:08:59–00:09:13).

**Worked example.** A consumer calls because they have moved out of state and are no longer a tax dependent of the primary. They are not asking for a cancellation — they are asking to come out of the household. You report the change with the county, and you do not cancel the plan.

**The steps on the screen.** The training walked through the whole path (00:09:47–00:11:08):

1. Open the enrollment dashboard on the consumer's case, reached by scrolling down the home page.
2. Find the group the plan sits in, and open the three dots at the corner of it.
3. Choose cancel plan. Health plans and dental plans sit in separate tabs, and each one has to be cancelled on its own (official record, Cancel Your Plan, retrieved 2026-10-13).
4. Select the member or members.
5. Select the end date.
6. Give a reason if the consumer gave you one. A reason is optional (00:03:09–00:03:11).
7. Continue, and then confirm.

**Nothing happens until you confirm.** Clicking cancel does not end anybody's cover on its own; the request has to be worked through the pages before the confirmation step, so no consumer is cancelled by accident (00:11:14–00:11:31).

## Step 4: The voluntary way

This is the consumer asking to leave. The renewal lesson covers the end dates and the notice period; what belongs here is who acts and what it means afterwards.

**The request.** It is the consumer's right and you simply process it, for a Covered California household, without escalation. There is no reason you have to accept and no reason you have to question.

**The end date.** The screen offers three choices, all of them month ends: the last day of the month you are in, then the end of the month that follows, and then the end of the month after that (00:03:39–00:03:50). There is no booking ahead of that window — to have cover end in May, the consumer rings in March (00:05:27–00:05:42).

**The member stays in the household.** Cancelling ends the cover and leaves the person on the application as a tax household member, with no cover attached to them.

**It cannot be undone.** This is the part that catches people. Once a consumer has voluntarily ended their cover, that cover cannot be reinstated. The request still goes to the escalation queue, and the expected outcome there is a refusal (00:36:37–00:37:14, 00:38:26–00:38:30). What you must not do is turn the consumer away yourself: denying a reinstatement without escalating is the mistake that is penalised (00:38:38–00:38:45).

What you say afterwards is that the cover cannot be reinstated because the consumer cancelled it, and that what you can do is enrol them again if they have a qualifying life event; otherwise they wait for the open enrolment period (00:39:32–00:39:47). A voluntary cancellation is not itself a qualifying life event, so ending one cover does not open a special enrolment period for another (trap register K19, training session 2026-10-09 10-46-31).

## Step 5: The involuntary way

Here the cover ends and the consumer did not ask for it. This stage is about who acts, and about what the consumer can do afterwards.

**The marketplace acts** when the rules say the cover must end. The renewal lesson sets out the four reasons it can happen and the notice of termination that goes out, which tells the consumer why the cover ended, when it ended, and how to appeal.

**The county acts** when Medi-Cal eligibility ends, because household size or income has changed. Here the official record adds something the training did not say: California law requires Covered California to enrol the consumer in a new plan so that there is no gap in cover. The consumer has one month from the date their Medi-Cal ends to choose a plan without a gap, and can still enrol for up to 90 days, though waiting may leave them without insurance. If they qualify for financial help, a plan is chosen for them — the one offering the most financial help — but they do not have to keep it (official record, Losing Medi-Cal Coverage, retrieved 2026-10-13).

**The carrier acts** where the policy itself has to be dealt with: it can approve a mid-month termination and it decides about a prorated premium (00:16:51–00:19:00). The renewal lesson also covers the case where the carrier rescinds a policy for suspected fraud, and where a carrier tells the marketplace that cover should end for non-payment.

**What the consumer can do.** After an involuntary ending, the consumer has a route to challenge it: a complaint or an appeal. The official record sets the deadlines — an appeal generally has to be filed within 90 days of the mailing of the eligibility decision, and a complaint is answered within 30 business days. The Ombuds Office comes after the service centre, and only if the issue is still unresolved (official record, File an Appeal or a Complaint, retrieved 2026-10-13).

## Step 6: The two ways side by side

|                                | voluntary                                      | involuntary                                                         |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| who ends it                    | the consumer                                   | the marketplace, the county or the carrier                          |
| whose decision                 | the consumer's alone                           | a rule, a redetermination, or a decision the plan made              |
| who acts at the service centre | you process it                                 | you explain it and hand the rest on                                 |
| the end date                   | one of three month ends the consumer chooses   | a date set by whatever ended the cover                              |
| household membership           | unchanged                                      | may change, if the member also left the household                   |
| can it be undone               | no; a voluntary cancellation is not reinstated | yes, if the ending was a mistake, and that is what an appeal is for |
| what the consumer can do       | nothing to contest about the decision itself   | file a complaint or an appeal                                       |

**The single sentence to carry.** If the consumer asked for it, it is theirs, you process it, and it stands. If nobody asked for it, somebody else's rule or decision ended it, and the consumer has a route to challenge that.

2. The three household statuses, and what the service centre may do in each of them.
3. Where on the case you look to find the household status.
4. The difference between cancelling a plan and reporting a change, and the question that tells them apart.
5. The end dates the screen offers, and how far ahead a consumer has to ring.

7. Who ends a Medi-Cal enrolment, and what the official record says happens next.
