# Open Enrollment and Special Enrollment

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

## What you will be able to do

- Lay out the enrolment calendar and say what a consumer needs on a given day.
- Name the events that open a special enrolment period, and say what the official record calls them.
- State how long the special-enrolment window stays open, and what happens when it closes.
- Explain the one exception to the calendar for American Indian and Alaska Native consumers.
- Tell the difference between what the training covered and what only the official record says.

## Stage 1: The calendar as the training described it

The session was recorded on 6 October 2026, and the framing the training used was the present tense: right now, as we speak, a consumer who called needing coverage would need a special enrolment period, and they would need a qualifying life event to go with it. There was no open window on that date.

Then the calendar turns.

| Date                               | What is open                     | What the consumer needs   |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| 6 October 2026 — the session date  | Special enrolment                | A qualifying life event   |
| 1 November 2026                    | Open enrolment begins            | Nothing; anyone can enrol |
| 1 November 2026 to 31 January 2027 | Open enrolment for 2027 coverage | Nothing                   |
| From 1 February 2027               | Special enrolment again          | A qualifying life event   |

The training put it plainly. Once 1 November arrives the state is no longer in special enrolment but in open enrolment, and a consumer calling on that date who needs 2027 coverage does not need any qualifying life event at all. On 31 January the window closes. From 1 February, everyone is back to needing a qualifying life event.

**Two date cautions.** First, the training said open enrolment begins on 1 November without attaching a year. The official record dates the 2027 cycle as 1 November 2026 through 31 January 2027, so the year has to be supplied from there — and it matters, because 1 November is a different year each time. Second, the training's framing was written against the day the session was recorded. The shape repeats; the dates move. It was a description of where the calendar stood on 6 October 2026, not a standing statement about the state of enrolment today.

The official record also notes something worth knowing: California runs its own window through 31 January. A June 2026 court ruling vacated a shortened open-enrolment period that would otherwise have applied, so the full period stands. Check the current window before you quote a date to a consumer.

**The analogy.** The calendar is a shop with a door that is normally locked. Open enrolment is the day the door is propped open and anyone may walk in. A qualifying life event is the key that unlocks the door for one person on one occasion — and like a real key, it opens the door for a limited time only.

## Stage 2: The events that open a special enrolment period

The official site calls these **major life changes**. The training called them **qualifying life events**. Same set of circumstances, two names, and you will hear both from consumers.

| Qualifying life event                                                     | What it looks like in practice                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Losing coverage through an employer, or losing health insurance elsewhere | Job-based coverage ended, or other insurance ended |
| Marriage or a domestic partnership                                        | The household changed                              |
| A birth or an adoption                                                    | A new member of the household                      |
| Divorce or legal separation                                               | The household changed                              |
| A death in the household                                                  | The household changed                              |
| A recent move to California, or within California                         | The consumer's options changed with the move       |

Two things about that list that are worth carrying beyond the training.

The official record publishes a **wider set** of qualifying events than the training covered — among them losing Medi-Cal coverage, COBRA coverage ending, being released from jail, gaining citizenship or lawful presence, experiencing domestic abuse or spousal abandonment, and losing financial help after an income or household change. So a consumer describing something that is not on the training's list is not automatically out of luck. The official position is that a consumer who is unsure can still apply, and the application is reviewed to decide what they are eligible for.

And a move inside California is not quite the same as a move to California: the official record ties a move within the state to a change in plan options. The training's phrasing was simply a recent move to California or within California.

## Stage 3: How long the window stays open

The general rule is **60 days** from the life event. When the window closes, that route to enrolment closes.

Two honest qualifications. The 60-day figure comes from the official record, not from the training — the session did not state it. And the official record hedges it: a special enrolment period _usually_ lasts 60 days from the date of the event. So 60 days is the general rule, not an absolute, and the official record publishes per-event deadlines that in some cases differ.

The case that matters most in practice is the one that can be opened early. For losing job-based insurance, the official record allows you to apply 60 days _before_ your coverage ends as well as after, and applying early is what avoids a gap in coverage. Losing Medi-Cal coverage gives 90 days rather than 60.

A missed window does not reopen. It closes that particular route — but the consumer is not stranded: open enrolment comes round again, and a later qualifying life event opens a fresh window with a fresh 60 days.

## Stage 4: The one exception to the calendar

This one was not in the training. It is in the official record, and it is exactly the sort of exception worth knowing.

American Indian and Alaska Native consumers may enrol in a Covered California plan **at any time**. They do not have to wait for open enrolment, and they do not need a qualifying life event. On the portal they choose federally recognized American Indian or Alaska Native as their life change. They can also change their health plan once each month, and members of federally recognized tribes are exempt from the California penalty for not having insurance.

Note what this exception is and is not. It is not a shorter version of the open window — it is a standing entitlement to enrol, on any date, without producing a life event. That is why it matters to a consumer calling on an ordinary day in February: the answer is not that they must wait, and it is not that they must find a qualifying event.

**The analogy.** Everyone else is standing outside a shop that opens once a year, or needs to produce a key after an event that changes their life. For this group the door is simply unlocked.
