# Regaining Account Access

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

A consumer who cannot sign in is a consumer who is already frustrated. The work is short and mechanical, and the training for it is short and precise.

## What you will be able to do

- Apply the four rules that decide whether a password may be reset at all, and put authentication before all of them.
- Say who may ask for a reset, and who must be on the line, and what an authorised representative gets instead.
- Tell a locked account from a blocked one, and explain what a consumer can do for themselves.
- Walk the service centre through the CalHEERS procedure, including the field that silences the search if you get it wrong.
- Explain what unblocking resets, and when you should not unblock at all.
- Recognise the four calls you cannot fix, and hand each one to the right place.

## Step 1: Authentication first, and the right account

**The four rules the training laid down.**

1. Service centre representatives can help consumers with login errors caused by invalid usernames, passwords, or responses to security questions.
2. Passwords may only be reset or unblocked for the primary member, and only after authentication.
3. Only the primary member, their spouse, or a registered domestic partner may have the primary member's password reset.
4. An authorised representative has to hold an account of their own for the case they act on.

**Why rules 1 and 2 look like they clash, and do not.** Rule 1 tells you what you are allowed to help with. Rule 2 tells you when you may start. Nothing at all happens to a consumer's username, password or account before they have been authenticated — not a reset, not an unlock. It is a general reminder, and it is the one that is quoted back at you most often.

**Rule 3 in practice.** A dependent calling on behalf of a parent does not get a reset. The training's example was a 23-year-old dependent asking for a parent's password reset, and the answer was that the primary, the spouse or a registered domestic partner has to be on the line. You apologise, and you explain that you need one of those three people present.

**Rule 4 in practice.** An authorised representative does not get the primary's username, and the primary's password is not reset for them. What they get instead is a **delegation access code**, which lets them register their own username and password and then reach the primary's information from their own account. This is not the same code as the one you generate when a consumer links an existing application to a new username; the delegation code is what makes a representative's own account work.

**Before you touch anything: which account?** Confirm the consumer has a username at all, because you cannot reset a password to something that does not exist. Then confirm the username is the one that gives them the case they need. People are signed in to accounts from the past, sitting on an inactive case, looking at a page that says apply now while you are looking at a completely different set of information. So ask which username they are signed in with, and if it is an old one, the work is creating the right username on the case that matters.

## Step 2: Who may ask, and who may not

**You authenticate the primary, not the caller.** When a representative or an agent rings, you authenticate the primary. Once the primary's details check out, the representative gives you their own first and last name and that is enough. Their own telephone number, email address and mailing address are optional and are not part of authentication — the training pushed back on a representative who tried to make all of it match.

**You still have to ask.** Most callers volunteer the fact that they are an authorised representative or an agent; the training put that at about 80% of the time. The rest are waiting for you to work it out. So ask, and then look: the delegates sit in the application under account information, and the insurance agents sit under the managed delegates.

**What the login rules change.** The wider set of things a spouse, a representative or a delegate may ask for on a case is not what this session is about. What this session adds is the login itself:

| They may                                                    | They may not                                     |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Register their own account with a delegation access code    | Be handed the primary's username or password     |
| Have a password reset on their own account                  | Have the primary's password reset for them       |
| Add a delegate, where the primary or the spouse asks for it | Receive an access code for the primary's account |

Only the primary or the spouse can add another representative, which is why the choice of representative matters: this is someone the consumer genuinely trusts with their health information.

> **A note on two sessions.** An earlier session described a representative's rights more broadly, and listed resetting passwords among them. The rule taught here is the narrower one and it governs the online login: the primary's own username and password never change hands, and the representative works through an account of their own.

**One agent at a time.** A case can carry more than one authorised representative, but only one insurance agent can be delegated to it. CalHEERS will not let a second agent be added until the original one is removed. So a consumer who wants a different agent has the first one taken off before the new one goes on, and if they would rather keep the first one, nothing changes.

**The two calls you cannot help with.**

- _An insurance agent about their own password._ The service centre does not reset agent passwords, does not create agent accounts, and does not sort out an agent's system problems for their clients. That belongs to the agent service centre, and the number sits in a knowledge article. When you search an agent's username in CalHEERS, nothing comes back — which is expected, not a fault.
- _A county eligibility worker._ Refer them back to their supervisor. You do not reset their password, and the service centre has no access to the Medi-Cal system at all.

## Step 3: Locked or blocked, and the road the consumer can take alone

**Two states, one question.** A consumer who cannot get in may have an account that is blocked or one that is locked. The training's diagnostic question was whether they had entered their one-time passcode incorrectly — the same slide appeared a second time with security questions in place of the passcode — and the rule was the same either way: **yes means the account is blocked, no means it is locked.** On the consumer's screen the two states arrive as different messages, one stating that the account cannot be confirmed and the other that the maximum number of attempts has been reached. Either way the consumer is pushed to the contact-us page, and the account has to be cleared by the service centre before a reset will work.

**What the consumer can do alone.** A consumer who has registered an email address or a cell phone number against their account can reset their own password or PIN, retrieve their own username and answer their own security questions. The route is coveredca.com, the sign-in button, then the forgot password or forgot username link, and then a page asking for their username and date of birth.

**Why registration is the hinge.** Registering an email address or a cell phone for the one-time passcode is what makes the self-serve route possible at all. The register-your-account page comes right after a consumer creates their username and password, before they reach their account home, so registration is part of setting up an account rather than an afterthought. The training's advice was to register both, and at least one of the two.

**When the consumer comes back to you.** Two things send them to the counter. If they never registered a one-time passcode against an email address or a text number, the self-serve reset redirects them to the contact-us page. And if they reach the maximum number of failed login attempts, they are redirected there as well. In both cases the service centre does the work.

**How far you can guide them.** You are not sitting next to them and cannot watch their screen. The training's own walkthrough went as far as the page that asks for the username and date of birth, and stopped there, because from that point the consumer has to do it. If they get stuck at that page, the fallback is the simplest thing in the job: reset the password for them.

**The password they end up with.** A new password has to combine upper and lower case letters, numbers and a special character, and the training put the length at between eight and twelve characters. If the criteria are not met the system will not let you continue.

## Step 4: The service centre procedure in CalHEERS

The tool is the **user accounts administration** screen, reached from the CalHEERS admin home page: hover over **management**, then choose **user accounts administration**. From there:

1. On the search users page, look for the person you need and then press the search button.
2. Select the edit button on the user's row.
3. The pop-up offering a password reset or an unblock appears.
4. Follow the steps to reset a password, to unlock the account, or to unblock the account.

**The search.** You can search by username, or by first and last name, and you can add the email address. A common name brings back a lot of results, which is a good reason to use the username instead. There is a like-search box that matches partial words. If the data you entered returns nothing, the results area says no accounts found.

> ★ **The organisation field is the trap in this screen.** The organisation must be set to **exchange external** for a consumer account. Set it to **SC4** — the internal organisation — and the search returns nothing at all. SC4 is the one you use for your own staff and agent passwords, not for a consumer's.

**The status field.** You may leave the status alone or set it to disabled. The training's habit was to assume disabled on the grounds that a consumer ringing about online account help is probably not active, but it is only a habit, and accounts turn out to be active often enough.

**Resetting the password.** The pop-up offers two routes:

- _Manual change the password._ Type the new password, confirm it in the confirmation field, then select reset.
- _Auto-generate._ This is the default radio button, with a checkbox to email the new password to the user. Auto-generate is inactive for accounts whose preferred method of contact is mail.

So a consumer with no email address, or one whose preference is mail, gets a password you set by hand.

**The temporary password.** Either way what the consumer receives is temporary. It does not expire on its own, and with the auto-generate route an email goes out immediately with the new password. The instruction you give is to sign in straight away and change the password: on that first sign-in the consumer is asked to create a new one before reaching the account home, and the temporary one stops working there.

**Verify before you reset.** The search results show the username, first and last name, date of birth, the email address if there is one, the account status and the organisation. Follow the usual policy and procedure for verifying the identity of the user before you go on to reset anything.

## Step 5: Unblocking, and what it resets

Sometimes a consumer is not sure whether they have blocked their own account. The training's answer was blunt: when in doubt, unblock the account after resetting the password.

**How.** In the unblock account section of the pop-up, tick the disclaimers box. That box acknowledges that the one-time passcode and the security question are reset by the unblocking, and ticking it activates the unblock button. Selecting the button produces a message saying the account has been unblocked.

**What it wipes.** The one-time passcode and the security questions are reset. The trainer also described it as resetting the email address and phone number the consumer first registered, and the consumer then meets a re-register page once they sign in, to update their email address, phone number and security questions. That is the answer to the consumer who cannot reach the address they used ten years ago.

**One thing that does not happen.** No email is sent to the consumer when an account is unblocked.

**Do not unblock by reflex.** Unblocking resets the verification methods. If a consumer's verification methods are fine and all they need is a password reset, they do not need an unblock. The training's exception was a consumer who wanted to re-register their phone number, email address and security questions.

## Step 6: The callers you cannot fix

**Medi-Cal only households.** No password resets — that one belongs to the county. It sits alongside everything else you cannot do on a Medi-Cal case: you cannot report a change, add a Medi-Cal plan, or terminate a Medi-Cal plan. If the consumer can self-serve online, that is fine; if they need your help, they go to the county.

**Mixed-program families.** Here you can reset, with one large caveat. The county sometimes leaves a note in CalHEERS telling Covered California not to reset that consumer's password. The route to it is account information, then case history, then the case summary, then the documents and correspondence notes. The wording is plain when you meet it: the case is one the county treats as sensitive, no password reset is to be done, and the consumer should be pointed at the county for help with their online access. If the note is there, you stop and refer the consumer to the county or to self-serve. Missing that note is something you get docked for. A household that is entirely Covered California does not need this check at all. The training's own phrase for this kind of household is a mixed household; mixed-program family is the official record's term, and that is the one to use.

**County eligibility workers.** Refer them to their supervisor.

**Insurance agents.** Refer them to the agent service centre, and give them the number from the knowledge article.
