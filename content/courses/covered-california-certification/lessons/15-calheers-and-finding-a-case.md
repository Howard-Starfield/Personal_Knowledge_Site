# CalHEERS and Finding a Case

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

## What you will be able to do

- Explain what CalHEERS is, and say why calling it only "the application" understates what it does.
- Describe how one system serves both Covered California and Medi-Cal, and who uses it.
- Choose between the two case-search methods, and say what each one needs from you.
- Predict which search method will be selected when you open the case search, and check it before you type.
- Take a consumer's case from a search result to the screens you can work on once it is open.

## Stage 1: CalHEERS is the whole system, not just the form

Most people meet CalHEERS as a web page with boxes on it. The training is
emphatic that the boxes are the smallest part of it.

CalHEERS is the programme Covered California uses to connect consumers to
coverage. People often call it "the application", but it is an entire system.
It gathers the consumer's data, works out household eligibility, supports plan
selection and refers the consumer onward to the health plan's own external
site, takes payments, carries out ongoing system updates, sends messages to
consumers, and issues the forms the programme is required to send.

The name is **California Health Care Eligibility, Enrollment and Retention
System**. The training singled this out as a fact worth knowing by heart.

> **The analogy.** The application is the counter where a consumer fills in a
> form. CalHEERS is the whole office behind that counter — the records, the
> payments, the letters that go out, and the people who work there.

> **Why it matters.** If you think of CalHEERS as only the web page, you will
> look for things on the screen that the system does somewhere else entirely,
> and you will not look for things it does that are not on the screen.

## Stage 2: One system, two programmes, many users

CalHEERS is the consolidated system behind eligibility, enrollment and
retention for **both** Covered California and Medi-Cal. A consumer uses a
single streamlined application to find out which of the two programmes their
household may qualify for, and to enrol in health insurance plans. The system
also keeps hold of what came before: earlier application information and the
documents a household has already supplied.

Many different groups work in this system:

| Group                                                                                                                     | What they use CalHEERS for                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Service centre staff — representatives, leads, supervisors, and units such as appeals, business operations and escalation | Daily case work, enrolling consumers, answering calls                                            |
| Consumers                                                                                                                 | Applying on their own, checking the status of their own case, seeing their documents and letters |
| County eligibility workers                                                                                                | Medi-Cal determinations and case work                                                            |
| Licensed insurance agents and enrollment counselors                                                                       | Helping consumers apply, including by telephone and in person                                    |

Every one of these users reaches the system the same way: through the Covered
California website. And a consumer has three routes to an application —
telephone the service centre and complete it with help, enrol on the site by
themselves, or get help from an enroller in person or on the telephone.

> **Why it matters.** A consumer who asks "can I do this myself on the site" is
> asking a question with three possible answers, and the honest one is yes —
> the same system the person on your call is using is open to them.

The system also changes. The training expects **a few major updates over a
year, with smaller ones in between**, and says that representatives pick them
up through assigned training in the learning management system rather than by
noticing. A consumer sees the changes when they log in to their account.

## Stage 3: Two ways to search for a case

The administration home page has two tabs you can toggle between, and one of
them is the case search. Inside it there is a **Search by** menu holding **two
radio buttons**, and what each one needs is fixed.

| Search method            | What you put in                                                                                   |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Consumer information** | The consumer's name, date of birth, social security number, or the email address on their account |
| **Unique identifier**    | The CalHEERS case ID, the application ID, or the username attached to the case                    |

The username option is the newer of the two additions the training mentions,
and it is worth knowing because it is the one people forget.

> **The analogy.** Finding a case is finding a parcel. You can look it up by
> the recipient's personal details, or by the number printed on the label. You
> pick one set of details or the other — you do not mix them.

## Stage 4: The search method you used last time is the one you get

Here is the detail that catches people out. The radio button does not reset.
**It defaults to whichever search type was used most recently.** Search by case
ID on one call, and the unique identifier button is still sitting there when
you open the search on the next one.

This is a deliberate convenience, not a fault. It means a representative who
works mostly from case IDs does not have to re-select the same method on every
call. The cost is that anyone who assumes the search always opens on consumer
details is wrong roughly half the time, depending on the last call they took.

> **What to do.** Look at the selected button before you type. It takes a
> second and it is the difference between a search that works and a search that
> returns nothing.

## Stage 5: From a search result to the right case

The everyday search is the **combo search**: last name, first name and date of
birth together. It returns **every case attached to that consumer's contact**,
which may be more than one. That is the point of it — the cases may belong to
different household members, or to different years of coverage.

The training's procedure for handling that:

1. **Bookmark every case that comes back**, even when there is only one, so it
   is easy to return to.
2. If there is more than one, bookmark them all and then narrow down by
   process of elimination until the right case is identified.
3. Work the case: assist the consumer, discuss what is on it, and authenticate
   it.
4. **Clear every bookmark before the next call.**

Two things about bookmarks are easy to get wrong:

- **They live on your own account**, not on the consumer's case. That is
  exactly why they have to be cleared — you are the one carrying them into the
  next conversation.
- **You can hold no more than ten of them at once.** Reach the limit and you
  must remove the ones you are not using before you can add another.

Before this feature existed, representatives copied case notes into a
document, or ran the same search again from scratch every time.

> **Why it matters.** A stale bookmark from the previous caller is a quiet way
> to open the wrong case. The training treats clearing them as standard
> practice after each call, not as tidying.

## Stage 6: What you can do once the case is open

Finding the case is the start. The consumer homepage — the welcome page a
consumer lands on when they log in, and one you can reach from the
administration home page or from anywhere inside the application — is where the
work happens.

| Area of the homepage              | What it gives you                                                                              |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Return to administration homepage | Your way back out to where you do case searches                                                |
| Household summary                 | Every household member on the application, and a place to start an application                 |
| Year toggle                       | Switch between benefit years — the current and previous one, and on the homepage 2026 and 2027 |
| Manage your application           | The links that appear once an application is complete                                          |
| Account information               | Authorized representatives, case history, cost applications, documents and correspondence      |
| Account alerts                    | Messages waiting for the consumer, including notices about documents that are due              |

**The household summary** shows, once an application has gone in, the household
members by name, their home and postal addresses, their email address, their
phone number, how the consumer wants to be contacted, any plan they hold, and
any documents still outstanding, such as proof of income or proof of
citizenship.

**Manage your application** fills with links once the application is finished:
review the submitted application, review eligibility results, the enrollment
dashboard, a shopping comparison, terminate the application, cancel a plan.
The training's advice is not to go near termination casually.

**Account information** is where you check whether an authorized
representative — a family member, a friend, an agent — is attached to the
case. A representative can do almost everything the consumer and their spouse
can, so this is worth checking on every contact.

It also holds **case history**, **cost applications** (the original submission
with its results, and the record of every application since) and **documents
and correspondence**.

**Documents and correspondence** is the one that settles arguments. You can
open the actual letter the consumer is holding in their hand and go through it
with them. The letters are produced in the consumer's preferred language, and
where you are not authorised to read it in that language there is an option to
view it in English.

**Case history** shows who made a change. The entry carries a label: staff
appear as a service centre representative, a county appears under its own
name, a delegated agent appears as an agent, and a username means the consumer
made the change themselves from their own online account. That label is what
settles a disputed income entry.

> **The technique the training recommends.** When a consumer will not take your
> word for what their record says, have them log in to their own account and
> look at the same screens alongside you. The two of you are looking at the
> same record, which is usually more persuasive than anything you can say.

> **The other thing you can do.** A change a consumer reports can be corrected
> on the spot, whether it was their typo or someone else's. Remember the
> timing, though: a reported change normally takes effect on the first day of
> the following month, so a correction made on 7 April lands on 1 May and
> leaves the April premium as it stands.
