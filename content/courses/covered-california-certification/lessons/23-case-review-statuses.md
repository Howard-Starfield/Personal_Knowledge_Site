# Carry Forward, Soft Pause, and Negative Action

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

## What you will be able to do

- Name the three review outcomes a case can land in after a change is reported, and say who decides each one
- Explain what a consumer keeps while a carry forward or a soft pause is still running
- Work out the end date a carry forward produces, and why the county's backdating does not change it
- Say what a representative can do about a soft pause and about a negative action, and what only the county can do
- Find all three statuses on the budget worksheet and read the reason lines underneath them
- Explain how a Medi-Cal case that was approved can show as pending once the county changes the application, and what the county then asks the consumer to do

## Stage 1: A reported change starts a review, and the county owns the answer

Reporting a change does not hand the decision to the service centre. It starts a
review, and the county makes the decision that comes out of it. Three outcomes can
follow, and each one shows up on the case under its own name.

Two of the three are review states: the consumer's existing coverage keeps running
while the county works. The third is a decision the county has already made. The
rule that governs all three is the same. **The county owns these statuses.** A
county eligibility worker — the county staff who handle Medi-Cal determinations —
is the only role that can lift them, and the Department of Health Care Services can
remove a negative action. A representative at the service centre has no way to
alter a Medi-Cal case directly, and the training is explicit that representatives
never remove a negative action.

| Status          | Whose eligibility is under review                           | Who is waiting | What the consumer keeps meanwhile                                  |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Carry forward   | A Covered California case that may now qualify for Medi-Cal | The county     | The Covered California plan continues, at the same monthly premium |
| Soft pause      | A Medi-Cal case that may now qualify for Covered California | The county     | Medi-Cal coverage continues                                        |
| Negative action | Nothing — the county has already decided                    | —              | Medi-Cal ends, and financial help can be affected                  |

The training groups all three as statuses that belong to consumers moving to or
from MAGI Medi-Cal, and it points out that only county or Department of Health
Care Services staff can change them. That is why every answer you give a consumer
about these three eventually comes back to the same place: here is what is
happening, and here is the office that decides it.

## Stage 2: Carry forward keeps the plan running while the county checks for Medi-Cal

Carry forward is the state a Covered California case moves into when a consumer
reports a change that could make them eligible for Medi-Cal. The consumer holds a
plan with financial help and pays a monthly premium for it. The change puts the
case into a pending Medi-Cal position, and the county is asked to make the final
determination.

**Nothing about the consumer's coverage changes while the county works.** The plan
runs on without a break, and the monthly premium goes unchanged — it does not rise,
and it does not fall either. If the consumer can afford it, they keep paying it.
They can also keep going to the doctor and keep picking up their prescriptions.
There is no need to cancel an appointment because of a status change, and no need
to wonder whether a doctor takes Medi-Cal: the plan they hold continues
uninterrupted until the final determination is made.

The review is not quick. The figure the training gives the consumer is 45 days at
most, and sometimes longer than that. What the representative says is roughly
this: your eligibility with Covered California stays the same and unchanged until
the county steps in, and you need to contact the county to get the decision made.

Two consequences follow once the county decides:

- If the county finds the consumer eligible for Medi-Cal, they have to take it. If
  they refuse, they can stay on Covered California, but the financial assistance
  stops.
- If the consumer simply ignores the county, the county takes the financial
  assistance away and puts a negative action on the account. That is the bridge
  from this stage to Stage 4.

### The end date: the county's clock, not the backdated one

When the county finds the consumer eligible for Medi-Cal, the Covered California
plan is scheduled to end automatically. The date it lands on is set by the month
the county reached its decision — not by the month the county chose to backdate the
Medi-Cal to.

The backdating is the county's gift, not its obligation. Medi-Cal can be awarded
from the month in which the application was made, or from an earlier date that the
consumer asked for. In the training's worked example a consumer reports a change
on 12 October, the county decides on 12 November and backdates the Medi-Cal to
1 October. The Covered California end date is 30 November, because the decision
was made in November. The consumer has paid a premium for October that they did
not need to pay, and no retroactive end date can be given: the financial
assistance has already been paid to the carrier for that month, and the law does
not allow the plan to be backdated to recover it. A consumer who wants that money
back has to appeal.

The 14-day rule decides which month the system schedules by default. It exists
because the consumer is entitled to notice before coverage ends, and that notice
has to fit inside the calendar.

- **Decided early in the month.** If the county finalises in the first half of the
  month — the training's example is the 10th — the notice period still fits inside
  that month, so the system schedules the termination for the last day of the same
  month.
- **Decided late in the month.** If the county finalises closer to the month's end
  — the training's example is the 18th — the 14-day window falls in the following
  month, so the system schedules the termination for the last day of the following
  month.

In the second case the consumer is still entitled to an end date of the last day
of the month in which the county decided. They just have to ask for it: they
call, and the representative escalates it so that the date is corrected. A
consumer on auto-pay will notice anyway, because the carrier bills them for the
extra month.

> **An analogy that holds.** The county runs two clocks. One is the Medi-Cal
> clock, and the county is allowed to wind it back to an earlier date. The other is
> the decision clock, and the end date always follows it. Winding back the first
> clock does not touch the second.

## Stage 3: Soft pause is the same review running the other way

Soft pause is that review in the opposite direction. The consumer is Medi-Cal
eligible and reports a change that might make them Covered California eligible —
an increase in income, for example, or a change that means they no longer qualify
for Medi-Cal, or might qualify for a different Medi-Cal programme instead. The
soft pause flag then sits on the account while the county works out whether the
consumer could qualify for another Medi-Cal programme.

**Medi-Cal coverage continues uninterrupted** through all of it. The consumer is
under review for Covered California eligibility, not off Medi-Cal.

What you can and cannot do:

- You cannot lift it. Only a county eligibility worker can, because the soft pause
  is part of the Medi-Cal case. So you send the consumer to their county office
  for anything to do with their current Medi-Cal coverage, because Covered
  California cannot make that determination.
- You should verify the consumer's programme eligibility first, before you say
  anything else.
- Once the determination is finalised the soft pause is lifted. If the consumer is
  moving to Covered California and qualifies for a subsidised plan, the automatic
  plan selection rules choose the plan, and the representative can then help the
  consumer confirm it or make changes.

### The same-month trap

Here is the trap that catches people. If a change is reported during the month in
which the soft pause came off, the redetermination can put a soft pause straight
back on, and the county has to work through it again before it can be removed for
the affected months. The training's example: the soft pause is lifted on 14 April,
and on 28 April the consumer reports a new address. The change may trigger
another soft pause.

Three things can bring a soft pause back in that same month:

- the state automated welfare system running eligibility again in the month the
  soft pause was lifted
- the consumer updating a spouse's work information
- the consumer updating their consent for verification, which can redetermine
  eligibility

So the advice to the consumer is one of two things: report the change directly to
the county, or wait until the first day of the following month and report it then.
The training's reasoning is blunt — if the service centre reports the change for
them, they will most likely go straight back into a soft pause.

One thing that is _not_ a reason for a soft pause: a consumer who is found
Medi-Cal eligible. If they are Medi-Cal eligible, there is no soft pause on the
case at all.

## Stage 4: Negative action is the county stopping or denying Medi-Cal

Negative action is the third status, and it is the only one of the three that is
not a review in progress.

**What it is.** When somebody who was previously eligible is stopped from
Medi-Cal, or when an application that is still pending is refused Medi-Cal, the
county eligibility worker changes the status of the application. The case is then
flagged as a negative action, which means the consumer does not qualify for
Medi-Cal. The flag is applied to individual enrollees — it is not a blanket mark
on the whole household. Only a county eligibility worker or the Department of
Health Care Services can remove it.

**What usually causes one.** Non-compliance. The county mails a packet to the
household asking them to fill it out and send it back. If the packet is not
returned, the county places the negative action, because the household did not
respond to the county's request. The training is careful here: the consumer may
have ignored the county on purpose, or ignored it by accident, because people are
often confused by a Medi-Cal packet that arrives in the post.

**What is not a negative action.** A household that does return the packet and is
found ineligible on the merits. That is an ordinary denial based on the facts, not
a negative action for failing to respond. The training draws that line explicitly,
and it is the line a representative needs.

**What it does not affect.** The flag has no bearing on whether the consumer can
enrol in a subsidised Covered California plan if they are otherwise qualified. It
is not a Covered California function at all; it exists to signal that Medi-Cal has
been denied or discontinued. Representatives never remove it.

**What you can do about it.** You can help try to lift it, and the usual way is to
report a change. That can restore the financial help going forward, from the first
of the following month. Financial help for the months that have already passed has
to be given back by the county, and the consumer must go to the county for that.
The training's example: the county puts the negative action in place in
September, so the financial assistance stops from 1 October and the consumer pays
full price for October. If they call on 12 October, you can work with them to get
that financial help restored from 1 November; for October itself, they have to
contact the county.

A consumer who is convinced they should still qualify for Medi-Cal should be sent
to their county office, and you can help them with the reapplication process.
Offering that help is the action the training calls the right one, as opposed to
telling the consumer that you cannot help them.

**How to say it.** Use neutral language. The training's own wording for it is a
temporary hold, and that is the phrase to reach for, because the internal term
sounds harsh and can inflame a conversation that does not need to be inflamed. If
the consumer uses the word negative action first, that is fine — you are not
required to correct them, and you can still use it yourself. Two further details:
a consumer discontinued from Medi-Cal is entitled to receive ten days' notice
before the day the discontinuance takes effect, and that is worth mentioning when
it comes up; and the long-term negative action information held in the case system
carries step-by-step instructions for sending a Medi-Cal referral, which is what
a consumer is sometimes asked to request.

## Stage 5: Finding the three statuses on the budget worksheet

On every call, check the plan eligibility for each household member. There are
several places to count, and the three statuses do not all live in the same one.

The **budget worksheet** is the one-stop shop. You reach it through a link in the
footer of the eligibility results page, and it gives the information for each
household member.

| Status          | Where to look on the budget worksheet                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Soft pause      | The top of the list, on the field just beneath the household member's name. It shows one of several values — child, parent or caretaker, relative, pregnant woman, aged, blind or disabled, or not applicable. A value of not applicable means the soft pause is not in effect for that member; any other value means it is |
| Carry forward   | A yes or no indicator on the line directly under the soft pause entry, near the top of the worksheet                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Negative action | In the MAGI Medi-Cal individual household income budget section. The row is the first line under the federal poverty level figure shown for that individual, and it is not directly under the section title                                                                                                                 |

The negative action row holds two entries: a negative action reason and a
non-compliance reason. If the case has no negative action, both of them read not
applicable. If there is one, the reason field is populated on the appropriate
line — when the denial rests on the applicant receiving SSI, the negative action
reason says so, and if the discontinuance was for non-compliance, the reason is
on the second line.

The same indicators are available on the personal verifications page and on the
case transaction pages.

## Stage 6: When the county's own change makes an approved case pending

The three review outcomes above all begin with a change the consumer reports. This
one begins with a change the **county** makes, and it produces a status that looks
like a contradiction until you know the sequence. A Medi-Cal case that was already
approved can go back to pending, because the county itself altered the application
afterwards.

The session this stage comes from is a screen-share walkthrough of a real
consumer's case, so the dates below are the dates on that case and nothing more.

| When              | What happened                                                                                                                   | Where               |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| 17 September      | The county issued a notice of action approving Medi-Cal                                                                         | (00:07:28–00:07:37) |
| 3 October         | The county changed the application itself — a household member had been added to the shared application                         | (00:03:31–00:03:44) |
| after that change | Eligibility for the household showed as pending Medi-Cal                                                                        | (00:03:44–00:04:00) |
| 4 October         | A further notice was dated — and the presenter found no Medi-Cal termination notice of action after the county made its changes | (00:06:33–00:07:06) |

**Approved, then pending, is not a contradiction.** A case can carry an earlier
favourable decision and a current review at the same time. The earlier decision
was made on the application as it then stood; the later review follows a change
to it. The presenter put it plainly: the eligibility had shown as Medi-Cal
eligible up until the month the county changed the application, and that was when
it changed (00:06:16–00:06:19).

### Why it is pending

The county is re-determining eligibility on the changed application
(00:07:42–00:07:47), and it has asked the consumer to review and update the
income information it holds for Medi-Cal eligibility (00:06:06–00:06:16). The
presenter gave that as the reason in one short sentence: the request for the
income information is why the case shows as pending (00:06:16–00:06:19).

Another notice of action follows the income verification. Once the income is
verified, the county sends another notice of action (00:07:47–00:07:55). Until
that notice arrives the pending status is the live result, and the September
approval is the earlier decision rather than the current one.

### Where the boundary sits

The representative cannot verify the income, and cannot make or reverse the change
that brought the review on. The presenter said Medi-Cal would help the consumer,
that the county had to work through its own change of eligibility, and plainly
that the service centre cannot verify the income or do it for the consumer
(00:08:07–00:08:26). This is Stage 1's rule in a new place: **the county owns
these statuses**, and the representative's part is to explain the sequence and
point the consumer at the office that decides.

### After the approval: choosing the carrier

A Medi-Cal approval brings a separate step with it. The consumer is sent a
**health care options packet** so that they can choose which insurance carrier is
enabled underneath their Medi-Cal coverage (00:04:21–00:04:30). The alternative
is to contact Health Care Options by phone, or on the Department of Health Care
Services' health care options website, to move into a Medi-Cal health plan
(00:04:30–00:04:48).

The official record draws the same line from the other side. Covered California
cannot update the county systems that determine Medi-Cal eligibility, and
Medi-Cal matters belong to the Department of Health Care Services (ombuds office,
retrieved 13 October 2026). A consumer who needs help with Medi-Cal goes to the
**Medi-Cal Helpline, (800) 541-5555**, or to their local county office (filing an
appeal, retrieved 13 October 2026).

### The dates are this case's dates

The presenter worked out from the case record that the last eligibility
termination would have been 3 October and that the notice was dated 4 October,
and then said that in the documents and correspondence there was **not** a
Medi-Cal termination notice of action after the county made those changes
(00:06:33–00:07:06). So no termination appears in this story, and there is no
timing rule to carry away from the gap between the dates. The part worth keeping
is the pending status, what the county has asked the consumer to do about the
income, and the notice that follows the verification.

Two notes on the sources behind this lesson, so that you do not over-read it.
The recording renders the name of the budget section that carries the negative
action row in a garbled way; the wording used in Stage 5 is the clearest reading
of it. And the recording is also unclear about how long the county waits before it

depends on that number.

rather than guessing at an official one. The dates in Stage 6 are that one case's
