# Special Eligibility Groups

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

Points marked ★ carry the emphasis the training placed on them.

## What you will be able to do

- Say when a young adult's dependent coverage ends, and what keeps it going past 26.
- Separate the zero cost-sharing and limited cost-sharing options for tribal members.
- Explain why a student plan can stop a student getting tax credits, and how to check.
- State what Veterans Affairs cover does and does not let a veteran do.
- Tell a consumer with Medicare what they can and cannot buy through Covered California.

## Step 1: Young adults and the age-26 rule

A person may stay on a parent's plan as a dependent until 26. What catches people out is the ending, not the starting.

> ★ **L20-1 — Turning 26 does not cancel the cover on the birthday.** The training was clear that the person is not cut off on the day, and not even in that month. The remainder of the benefit year is still theirs. A dependent whose 26th birthday falls during **2026** stays on the plan through **31 December 2026**. The training's worked example was a 26th birthday on 9 October 2026, with cover running to the end of the year and the person needing a new arrangement from 1 January 2027.

**What does not end it before 26.** Marriage, having a child, leaving home, starting a job, filing taxes on your own, and moving out are all irrelevant to the rule. The training's illustration was a 23-year-old who gets married and can still be on the parent's policy until 26, or can separate out and go on their own application instead. A person aged 19, 20 or 21 has the same choice.

**What keeps it going past 26.** A plan contract that provides dependent-child cover says that reaching the limiting age does not end the cover while the child continues to meet **both** of two conditions:

- the person is incapable of self-sustaining employment because of a physical or mental disability, injury, illness or condition, and
- the person is chiefly dependent on the subscriber for support and maintenance.

Both, not either. A person who is 27 and still wholly supported by a parent but has no qualifying condition does not keep the cover; a person with a qualifying condition who is not chiefly dependent does not either.

**The employer offer still applies.** If the young adult is offered affordable employer cover that meets minimum value, the same rule as any other adult applies: the employer cover wins. Covered California encourages tax-independent individuals to enrol in their own plan, and the choice between staying on a parent's plan and going it alone is the consumer's.

## Step 2: American Indian and Alaska Native members

The training's own abbreviation for this group was AI/AN. Membership here means a member of a tribe recognized by the Bureau of Indian Affairs of the U.S. Department of the Interior. The official record adds Alaska Native status as a basis in its own right, and notes that some of these benefits reach anyone eligible for services through the Indian Health Service, a tribal programme, or an urban Indian health programme, even where that person is not a tribal member.

> ★ **L20-2 — The definition of Indian in this programme is narrower than the one used in other federally supported health services.** The training's warning was that a person may be deemed an American Indian by one programme and not by another, which produces different eligibility outcomes. Two programmes disagreeing is not an error; it is a reason to ask the application rather than to reason it out.

**Zero cost sharing.** A member of a federally recognized tribe whose household income is between **100% and 300%** of the federal poverty level, inclusive, can take a zero cost-sharing plan, on condition that they are not eligible for full-scope Medi-Cal, do not have free Medicare, and do not have affordable minimum-value employer cover. On that plan there are no deductibles, copayments or coinsurance for the covered services. Only one zero cost-sharing plan is offered per carrier and product, so there is no metal tier to choose and no comparison to make.

**Limited cost sharing.** A member of a federally recognized tribe with income **above 300%** of the poverty line can take a limited cost-sharing plan, even on an unsubsidised application, and at **any metal tier**. The limits are: no out-of-pocket cost for care received through an Indian health care provider, and they need an Indian health care provider referral in order to avoid copayments, deductibles and coinsurance for essential health benefits taken through the Covered California plan. The training also noted that income does not have to be verified in order to enrol on this plan.

**Both plans, always.** Members of federally recognized tribes do not have to pay the individual tax penalty. They can enrol at any time rather than waiting for the annual window, and they may change their plan once a month. The official record adds that the life-change reason to choose is federally recognized American Indian or Alaska Native status, and that cover starts on the first day of the month after the choice or change.

**A household is not a tribal household.** If one member of the household is eligible and the others are not, only the eligible member can take the AI/AN plans; everyone else takes their ordinary plans and the two are grouped separately. The official record is stricter still: a group holding eligible and non-eligible members together cannot choose an AI/AN zero cost-sharing or limited cost-sharing plan at all, and the household can instead choose a standard plan together with financial help where they are eligible.

## Step 3: Students and school health plans

Students with cover through their school may opt out of the student health plan and buy cover through Covered California. Depending on income, a student may get tax credits that help with the cost of a private plan through Covered California, or low or no cost cover through Medi-Cal.

**The blocking rule.** A student whose school health insurance plan carries the **minimum essential coverage** designation, or MEC, is not eligible for tax credits through Covered California while covered by that plan. The training named two plans that already carry it:

- the Student Health Insurance Plan offered by the University of California
- the University of California Voluntary Dependent Plan

**What to tell the student.** Check with the college to find out whether the plan it offers has been designated as MEC. And when the cover options are compared, weigh where the clinics and doctors are and what the services cost to reach, not only the premium.

**The practical test the training used.** If the school plan covers all ten essential health benefits, the student should keep the student cover. If it does not cover all ten, the student should come to Covered California, where financial assistance is available.

**The out-of-state student.** A student who lives out of state for the year but is still claimed as a tax dependent is a real case. It is a personal choice between the school plan and California cover, and the question to ask the consumer is where the student spends most of the year. What the consumer is really weighing is what happens when the student visits and needs care.

## Step 4: Veterans

> ★ **L20-3 — Every Veterans Affairs health care programme meets minimum essential coverage.** The training stated that a consumer enrolled in any VA health insurance programme does not have to make any change to their insurance because of the Affordable Care Act.

**What that lets a veteran do.** A consumer enrolled in a VA health programme may terminate their VA cover and enrol in a Covered California plan with financial assistance, if eligible, during the open enrolment period.

**And what it does not.** A voluntary termination, or the consumer deciding to cancel, does not on its own count as a qualifying life event that opens a special enrolment period. A veteran who simply decides to leave VA cover does not thereby open a special enrolment window.

**Veterans who are not in the VA programme.** If a veteran, or a member of their family, is not eligible for VA health benefits, they may be able to buy health insurance through Covered California or get cover through Medi-Cal, and depending on household size and income they may qualify for financial assistance.

**How you handle it on a call.** Veterans and VA coverage appear as a question on the application, among the other health programmes the consumer may be enrolled in. Ask the consumer and enter the answer. The training's instruction was blunt about this: do not decide from the fact that someone is a veteran, and do not tell a consumer in advance what they are eligible for.

**Supplemental cover.** Combinations such as VA with Medicare, or VA with Medi-Cal, are supplemental arrangements, and those come from the consumer's county office rather than from Covered California.

## Step 5: People with Medicare

Medicare is the federal health insurance programme for people aged 65 and over, for certain younger people with disabilities, and for people whose kidneys have failed, a condition often called end stage renal disease.

> ★ **L20-4 — Part A or Part C meets minimum essential coverage, and Part B alone does not.** A consumer who has Medicare Part A, or who has chosen a Medicare Advantage plan, which is Medicare Part C, already meets the requirement to have health insurance. A consumer with Part B and nothing else does not. The training's point was that the part of Medicare matters, not the word Medicare.

**What minimum essential coverage means for the money.** A consumer who has it is not eligible for financial assistance through Covered California while they have it. The training drew the comparison directly with other coverage: it is the same as having employer cover and choosing not to take it.

**The general rule.** The training's wording was that a consumer who has Medicare is barred from purchasing a Covered California health plan and using subsidies. The official record puts the same rule more precisely: people who are eligible for Medicare are generally **not eligible for financial help** to lower the premium of a Covered California plan, **even if they do not enrol in Medicare**, and a consumer who keeps a Covered California plan while Medicare-eligible may have to pay back financial help and may have their Medicare start date delayed.

**The exception.** A consumer who is eligible for a premium Part A but has not enrolled in it may enrol in a health plan through Covered California instead, and may also qualify for financial assistance. Eligible for and enrolled in are not interchangeable here.

**What the official record adds.** Report Medicare eligibility to Covered California within **30 days**. A Covered California plan is **not automatically cancelled** when a consumer becomes Medicare-eligible, even if they join a Medicare plan with the same insurer, so it has to be cancelled with at least **14 days** notice. Medicare enrolment is not automatic unless the consumer receives Social Security benefits. The initial enrolment period runs for **seven months**, from three months before the birthday month to three months after. Not signing up for Part B during that window can trigger a lifetime penalty of **10%** or more on the monthly premium, and the general enrolment period runs from **1 January to 31 March** with cover starting the following July.

## Step 6: Where the groups meet

Every group in this lesson is answering the same question from a different direction: **does this consumer already have cover that satisfies the requirement?** The answer decides whether financial assistance through Covered California is available.

| Group                                  | What it changes                                                                                                                                                | Where it comes from          |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Young adults under 26                  | May stay on a parent's plan; reaching 26 ends it at the end of the benefit year, not on the birthday                                                           | training                     |
| Members of federally recognized tribes | Zero cost sharing between 100% and 300% of the poverty line; limited cost sharing above 300% with a referral; enrol at any time; exempt from the state penalty | training and official record |
| Students                               | A school plan designated as minimum essential coverage blocks tax credits while it applies                                                                     | training                     |
| Veterans                               | All VA programmes meet minimum essential coverage; voluntarily ending VA cover is not a qualifying life event                                                  | training                     |
| Medicare                               | Part A or Part C meets minimum essential coverage, Part B alone does not; eligible-but-not-enrolled premium Part A is the exception                            | training and official record |

Two threads run through all of it.

**Cover and money are separate questions.** Having other cover does not stop someone applying. The official record says it plainly: having other health insurance does not disqualify you from applying, but it may affect whether you qualify for financial help. A consumer with VA cover, a student plan, or Medicare can still apply, and can still buy cover at full price.

**Special status changes the cost sharing, not the right to apply.** The tribal cost-sharing plans and the young adult rules change what a person pays and how long they may stay. They do not create a separate door, and the training was consistent about the method every time: ask the questions on the application, enter what the consumer says, submit, and read the result back.
