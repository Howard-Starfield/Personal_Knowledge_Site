# One Application, Many Doors

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

Points marked ★ carry the instructor's own emphasis in the training.

## What you will be able to do

- Explain the no wrong door approach and what a mixed household is.
- List the six ways a consumer can apply, and give the service centre number.
- Name the four kinds of enroller, say who each one works for, and how each one is paid.
- Explain how a licensed insurance agent differs from an enrolment counsellor in access and authority.

## Step 1: One application, two programmes

The Affordable Care Act requires a consumer to complete **one application** to be evaluated for eligibility to enrol in a Covered California plan **or** in **Medi-Cal**. Consumers do not have to work out which programme they belong to before they apply. This is the **no wrong door** approach.

Think of a single form, filled in at a single desk, with two doors behind it. The consumer does not choose the door. The form decides which one opens, and the consumer is walked through whichever one it turns out to be.

The training put the value of this plainly: the consumer does not have to know which programme they qualify for in order to apply, and the application works that out for them. That is a single application with no wrong door, and it is the reason a consumer who came in asking about a marketplace plan does not have to be sent away to start again with Medi-Cal.

**Worked example.** A consumer calls asking about a marketplace plan. Partway through the call it becomes clear they may be eligible for Medi-Cal instead. Nothing is lost and nothing is restarted: it is the same application, and it evaluates for both.

## Step 2: Mixed households

The application determines eligibility for both programmes, and it can come back **split across the household**. In one household, one member may be eligible for Medi-Cal and another for a Covered California plan. The training called this a **mixed household**.

The official record uses a different name for the same thing and describes it as ordinary: different people in the same household can qualify for different programmes, and the official term is a **mixed-program family**. So if a consumer says their partner is on Medi-Cal and they are not, that is not a contradiction and not a mistake on their part. It is the normal outcome of one application applied to more than one person.

**Worked example.** A couple call together. One of them qualifies for Medi-Cal; the other qualifies for a Covered California plan with financial help. The representative works through the application once and the household comes out with two different routes, each person on the right one.

## Step 3: Six ways to apply

The training listed six ways a consumer can apply.

| Route                                                                                        | Notes                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Self-enrol online at coveredca.com                                                           | The consumer does this themselves                     |
| Call a Covered California service representative                                             | **1-800-300-1506**                                    |
| Get free in-person or on-the-phone assistance from a enroller approved by Covered California | Free to the consumer                                  |
| Apply in person through a licensed insurance agent                                           |                                                       |
| Apply through the local county office                                                        |                                                       |
| Apply by mail                                                                                | Using the paper application, available on the website |

The official record fills in the practical details around that list. The service centre is open **Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m.** and closed at weekends. There is a **TTY line, (888) 889-4500**, and phone support in **14 languages** in addition to English. Help is always **free and confidential**, and the site carries two further routes the training did not list: live chat with a representative, and a free callback from a enroller approved by Covered California for first-time applicants. The Help On Demand callback is advertised as coming back within fifteen minutes, for first-time applicants only.

## Step 4: The four kinds of enroller

The training identified four kinds of enroller relevant to service-centre work.

| Kind                                                                                                                | Who they are                                                                                                                                                          | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Licensed insurance agent also approved by Covered California**                                                    | Licensed by the California Department of Insurance, and also approved by Covered California to sell Covered California health and dental plans                        | Earns commission on **effectuated enrolments**. Covered California pays commissions on individual and family plans; Covered California for Small Business pays on its own enrolments. May not charge consumers for enrolment assistance. |
| **Enrolment counsellor who works for a funded organisation**                                                        | Individuals at organisations holding grants under the **Navigator Grant Program**                                                                                     | The organisations are approved enrolment entities; the individuals are enrolment counsellor who works for a funded organisations. The grants fund outreach, education and post-enrolment services.                                       |
| **County eligibility worker**                                                                                       | The **Department of Health Care Services** oversees medical eligibility services at the county level, and county eligibility workers provide those enrolment services | The county listings are on the Covered California site.                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Application counsellor approved by Covered California, Medi-Cal managed care plan enroller, plan-based enroller** | Three further approved roles                                                                                                                                          | The training said these three are **not relevant** to service-centre work.                                                                                                                                                               |

Two details in that table repay a second look.

**How agents are paid.** Commission attaches to **effectuated enrolments** — enrolments that actually took effect — not to applications filed. The official record splits this into two streams, individual enrolments and Covered California for Small Business enrolments, and the training described the same split: Covered California pays on individual and family plans, and Covered California for Small Business pays on the rest. The recording is unclear on the exact wording of that second group, so the safe statement is that the payer follows the type of enrolment.

**What agents may not do.** An agent may not charge a consumer for enrolment assistance. The official record is blunter about the business side: agents working for an agency sign a non-monetary agreement, and no information is collected for commission-payment purposes.

## Step 5: Agent and counsellor are not the same

The training's own comparison: a licensed insurance agent also approved by Covered California has **somewhat more access to the consumer's information** and **more authority to perform certain actions** than a enrolment counsellor who works for a funded organisation. Both provide in-person assistance with Covered California, and the training said they operate in much the same way. The difference is not that one is a salesperson and the other is a helper. It is a difference in reach and in authority.

That difference shows up in the official record too. The glossary describes an enrolment counsellor as a trained assister who can help consumers apply for insurance and enrol but **cannot recommend which plan is best** for them. The site's enroller-finder tool lists licensed agents and counsellors as separate provider types, and only these two kinds appear there — the three tracks the training set aside for service-centre work do not appear as consumer-facing helpers.

The official record also adds something worth knowing when a consumer accepts help: record the enroller's name, their number, their address, their website and their approval number. If something goes wrong later, the consumer is the one who has to raise it, and that record is what they will be asked for.

## Review

Close the lesson and write down, from memory:

1. What the single application evaluates the consumer for, and why that is called the no wrong door approach.
2. What a family split across two programmes is, and the name the official record uses for it.
3. The six ways a consumer can apply, the service centre number and its hours.
4. The four kinds of enroller, who each one works for, and what each one is paid by.
5. The one difference between an agent and an enrolment counsellor, and which of the two may not recommend a plan.

Then check the two things people miss: that commission follows an enrolment taking effect rather than an application being filed, and that a counsellor who helps someone enrol may not tell them which plan is best.
