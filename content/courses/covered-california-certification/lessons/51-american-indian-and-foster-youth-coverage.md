# American Indian and Alaska Native Coverage, and Former Foster Youth

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

These two presentations ran back to back in one session: an American Indian and Alaska Native group, then a former foster youth group. What follows is what they add on top of the eligibility material in lesson 20.

## What you will be able to do

- List the five bases on which a consumer comes into American Indian and Alaska Native coverage, and name the first step of the enrolment route.
- Work through the screen steps for a household that contains tribal members, including what happens when the system cannot verify a consumer electronically.
- Decide whether a consumer is a former foster youth, and name the things that do not disqualify them.
- Say what full scope medical gives a former foster youth, and the two things it does not cover.
- Hand a former foster youth caller to their county with the right number and the right request.

## Step 1: Five bases for coming into AI/AN coverage

The training's abbreviation for this group was AI/AN, and its slide listed five ways a consumer can come into coverage `[00:45:24]`:

1. Membership of a tribe that the federal government recognises.
2. Holding ANCSA shareholder status.
3. Being eligible for care through the Indian Health Service, the federal agency, abbreviated IHS.
4. Being eligible for a tribal health program.
5. Being eligible for an urban Indian health program.

The last three are **service eligibility, not membership**. A consumer who is not a tribal member at all can still come in on one of them. The official record draws the same line: some of these benefits belong only to members of federally recognised tribes, while others reach anyone eligible for services through the Indian Health Service, a tribal program or an urban Indian health program.

Which of the two cost-sharing options a consumer can take turns on household income measured against the federal poverty level, and the two options and their bands are set out in lesson 20. What this lesson adds is the route in: the five bases above, the steps that follow them, and what the screen does with them. The training also noted on the same slide that income does not have to be verified in order to enrol on this route, which lesson 20 also carries.

## Step 2: The five steps to enrol

The training walked this list twice, once on the group's slide and again when they took questions afterwards `[00:48:52]`, `[01:00:23]`.

1. **Confirm the tribe.** The slide pointed at the Bureau of Indian Affairs directory as where the confirmation happens.
2. **Apply at Covered California and select the plan type** — zero cost sharing or limited cost sharing.
3. **Pick the tier.** The slide offered bronze, described as often carrying the smallest premium, or silver, which it tied to a network that suits the consumer.
4. **A referral, and only for limited cost sharing,** when the consumer uses a provider outside the Indian Health Service.
5. **The sources** the information came from.

Two cautions about this list. Step three is the point where the slide did not explain itself: it did not say whether the bronze-or-silver choice belongs to the zero cost sharing option or to the limited one, so treat that wording as the presentation's rather than as a rule you can quote to a consumer. And step four is the referral rule lesson 20 already sets out — it is what keeps cost sharing away at an outside provider, not a condition for getting on a tribal option at all.

## Step 3: What the screen does for an AI/AN household

This is the part that is new, because it is about the service centre's own screen rather than about eligibility `[01:01:05]`.

1. Select **Report a Change** from the consumer's home page.
2. Select the **yes radio buttons** for the AI/AN household members.
3. On the review page, a **yellow banner** appears for you to check.
4. The AI/AN members are grouped automatically into one plan, and the members who are not AI/AN go into a different plan.
5. **Pick the AI/AN members' plans on their own.**

The verification side is just as short. The system is built to check tribal status electronically, so the consumer only has to answer the tribal-status question in the application with yes or no. When the system can confirm the answer that way, nothing more is needed. When it cannot, the consumer is offered the chance to upload documents `[01:05:05]`.

**A tribe that is not fully recognised.** A consumer whose tribe is not on the Bureau of Indian Affairs list can still qualify for other services — Indian Health Service, tribal program and urban Indian health program services in California `[01:04:19]`. What a tribe's recognition status does not do is close those services off.

## Step 4: Who counts as a former foster youth

In California, a former foster youth — the training's abbreviation was FFY — is somebody **under 26** who **entered care on or after their 18th birthday**, in **any state or tribe**, and who **now lives in California**. Someone who meets those conditions qualifies for free full scope medical **regardless of income and tax filing status** `[00:51:39]`.

Nothing in the surrounding circumstances takes a consumer out of the group `[00:52:07]`:

- Being in foster care in another state.
- Having children of their own.
- Being married.
- Living with a relative.
- Living in an institution, a group home, a residential placement or a transitional living program.
- Already having health insurance through an employer, a parent or a spouse.
- Having income of any kind.

**Hold the definition, not the check list.** The definition is under 26. The training's own wording when it walked the checks was looser about a consumer who is exactly 26, and the definition is the part to rely on.

## Step 5: What full scope medical gives a former foster youth

Full scope medical here is broader than a single benefit `[00:52:31]`:

- Medical services.
- Vision care.
- Substance abuse treatment.
- Mental health services, including counselling.
- Dental care.

Two things are **not** covered, and both have an age attached to them `[00:52:42]`: contact lenses and sunglasses for anyone 21 or older, and braces for anyone 21 or older.

## Step 6: What CalHEERS does with a former foster youth application

When an applicant says they are a former foster youth, three things change on the screen `[00:53:05]`.

- Their own application does not show the **tax information, health care and income** pages.
- Other household members on the same application still do: that information, health care and income shows for them.
- The **eligibility summary page shows that no action is required** — they are not required to submit documents. If they choose to, there is an option for submitting documents on the document and correspondence page.

So a former foster youth consumer has nothing to send to get their application through the screen. The county is a separate step, and that is covered below.

## Step 7: The order of checks and the handover to the county

The training gave an order for the checks `[00:53:53]`.

1. Check whether the consumer **already has cover through a medical plan**.
2. Check that the consumer **lives in California**.
3. Check the consumer's **age**. Over 26 and the route is a quick sort. Under 26 and you check whether they are a former foster youth.
4. If they are **not** a former foster youth, go back to the quick sort.
5. If they **are**, tell them that California covers former foster youth up to age 26 — free health care for somebody who was in care in any state on or after their 18th birthday, who lives in California now, and who is under 26, and eligibility for California's medical plan whatever their income.

Then the handover tips `[00:54:49]`:

- Find out **which county the consumer lives in**, and look the county's number up in the **Medi-Cal County contact list**.
- **Never hand out a quick sort number** on these calls. Those lines are for staff only, and the set listed in the Medi-Cal County contact information is a different one.
- Give the consumer the **direct dial number** for that county.
- Staff there are usually on the line **from 8 in the morning until 5 in the evening, Monday to Friday**.
- Explain that the consumer must **say they are a former foster youth** and that they want to **hand their county the one-page DHCS application**.
- Ask whether they have any other questions or need any other help.

**Two different things, easy to merge.** In the screen itself a former foster youth has nothing to submit. The county, on the other hand, is told to expect a one-page application from the consumer. Both statements are the training's, and they are about different parties.
