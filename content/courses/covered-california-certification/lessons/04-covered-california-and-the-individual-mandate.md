# Covered California, the Individual Mandate, and the Obamacare Call

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

## What you will be able to do

- Explain how a state can run its own marketplace, what California chose, and when it launched.
- Keep the three layers of the individual mandate apart and say where each one stands.
- Give the California penalty figure with its year attached, and explain what kind of number it is.
- Explain why people still buy coverage even though there is no federal penalty.
- Handle a consumer who will not engage with the name Obamacare, politely and without getting political.

## Step 1: The marketplace California chose to run

A state could choose to run a marketplace of its own as a state-based exchange, or leave the job to the federal government. California elected to create its own. It is called Covered California, and it opened its first open enrollment period in October 2013. The site is `coveredca.com`, and the federal government's website for the law is `healthcare.gov`.

One dating note, because this gets quoted both ways. The training recorded the October 2013 launch of the first open enrollment period. The official site's own About page dates the marketplace from 2014, which is the year coverage under that first enrollment period began. Both statements describe the same event from opposite ends of it.

It is also worth being clear about what Covered California is. The official site says plainly that it is not an insurance company, and that it does not sell insurance directly or pay claims. It is an independent part of state government whose work is to connect residents to plans offered by the insurance companies and to sort out what financial help they qualify for. That is why a consumer buys a plan from an insurer and never sees Covered California when a claim is processed.

## Step 2: Three layers of the mandate

The mandate is three separate things, and the commonest error is to collapse them into one.

| Layer                       | Where it stands                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The federal requirement** | The law requires most US citizens and legal residents to carry health insurance. This is what people mean by the individual mandate, and it remains enforceable. |
| **The federal penalty**     | The federal monetary penalty was removed. A person who fails to have minimum essential coverage is not penalised at the federal level.                           |
| **The California penalty**  | California implemented its own individual mandate with an enforceable monetary penalty, in effect since 1 January 2020, administered by the Franchise Tax Board. |

The sentence to hold on to: **the requirement is still there, the federal money penalty is gone, and California runs a penalty of its own.** The wrong belief to kill is the one that says the individual mandate was repealed. What was removed is the federal penalty attached to it. The requirement itself still stands, and the state added a penalty on top of it.

## Step 3: The California penalty and the figures

This is where a number needs its year attached to it.

**What the training gave.** The California penalty was described as at least $2,700 for a family of four, with 2024 attached to it. Two things travel with that figure.

The first is its nature: it is a floor, not a fixed amount. The Franchise Tax Board computes each household's own penalty, so $2,700 is where a family of four starts from rather than what every family of four pays.

The second is the year, and here the training and the official record do not match. A Covered California release from **December 2025** publishes that same family-of-four figure of $2,700 alongside at least **$900 per adult** and **$450 per dependent child**, which is how the $2,700 is built up. So it is a **2026-cycle** number, not a 2024 one — and it has already been superseded. The amount is right, the year attached to it in the training is not, and the figure itself is out of date. Say so when you meet it.

**What the official record gives now.** The Covered California penalty page publishes a minimum of at least **$950 per adult** and **$450 per child under 18** for a full year without insurance, and an affordability threshold of **8.05% of projected annual household income** — the threshold is the part it labels **tax year 2026**, and the amounts themselves carry no year on the page.

**Who applies it.** The Franchise Tax Board, not Covered California. The official record puts that in plain terms and adds that Covered California cannot give tax advice. If a consumer asks what they personally owe, the answer is that the Franchise Tax Board has an estimator for a household-specific figure. And the working rule for any dollar figure you meet: confirm the year before you quote it.

## Step 4: Why people still buy coverage without a federal penalty

There are two reasons, and the second one is the one that matters.

The first is arithmetic. For some families the penalty is cheaper than the premium, and they will take the penalty. The training was candid about this and called it unfortunate: sometimes a family goes without coverage and simply pays the penalty because paying the penalty is cheaper than paying the premium.

The second is protection. One serious accident or one illness while uninsured is the risk being taken. The training was clear that the mandate is not the only reason to get coverage, and that the point of coverage is that you and your family are not exposed when something unfortunate happens.

**Exemptions.** Some people are not penalised at all. The training named three routes: someone who simply cannot afford coverage, religious conscience, and an appeal. It deferred the detail to the eligibility course. The official record adds the machinery: a general hardship exemption, an affordability hardship exemption, and a religious conscience exemption, claimed either through Covered California during the year or through the Franchise Tax Board after the year ends, with 90 days from the exemption notice to request an appeal.

## Step 5: Handling the Obamacare call

Some consumers will not engage with the name. They say they want nothing to do with the Affordable Care Act, or nothing to do with Obamacare, and that they want Covered California instead.

The training's instruction was to say politely and professionally that the Affordable Care Act, Obamacare and Covered California all mean the same thing, and to keep it out of politics. The goal of the call is simply to make health insurance affordable for the consumer, and the framing that gets them there is reassurance rather than argument.

So this is not a row about the name. If you argue the point you lose the consumer and you have not helped them. And setting up a false choice between the Affordable Care Act and Covered California contradicts the very thing you are trying to say, because they are the same thing.

Two things worth knowing before the call starts. The federal government's website for the law is `healthcare.gov`, and California's marketplace is at `coveredca.com`. Two sites, one law. And Covered California is a state marketplace, not an insurer, so it is not where a consumer's claims or providers live.
