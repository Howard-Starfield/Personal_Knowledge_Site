# Enroller Duties, Prohibitions, and Non-Discrimination

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

## What you will be able to do

- List the duties an enroller performs, including the first-payment duty and the referral duty.
- Name all eight things an enroller must not do, and keep the three charging rules apart from one another.
- Handle a consumer whose agent is charging them a fee, including the limit on what the service center can do.
- State the bases on which an enroller must never discriminate.
- Set out the accessibility duties, including the one that is a duty to know rather than a duty to do.

## Stage 1: The duties

The training listed what an enroller performs. Here is the full list.

| #   | The duty                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Give consumers information about qualified health plans and about the financial assistance that may be available                                                                                           |
| 2   | Walk consumers through the enrolment options that are available on coveredca.com                                                                                                                           |
| 3   | Tell consumers what premiums cost and what health and dental coverage buys them, and answer the questions they ask                                                                                         |
| 4   | Give consumers access to the tools and resources that let them compare cost, benefits and coverage options                                                                                                 |
| 5   | Help consumers who may qualify to enrol in Covered California health plans and in other programmes that make insurance affordable, including Medi-Cal                                                      |
| 6   | Work in the coveredca.com web-based portal to help a consumer complete and submit their application                                                                                                        |
| 7   | Make sure consumers understand the first payment they must make to enrol in a Covered California health plan                                                                                               |
| 8   | Refer the consumer, where appropriate, to consumer assistance hotlines, the Ombuds Office, the Office of the Patient Advocate, the Department of Social Services, or the Covered California Service Center |
| 9   | Help consumers with renewals during open enrolment periods, and with new special-enrolment applications, using the coveredca.com web-based enrolment programme                                             |

Two of the nine carry more weight than the rest, because they are the ones people drop.

**The first-payment duty (7).** This one connects straight back to the previous lesson. The first payment is a condition of enrolment, it goes to the carrier, and a consumer who does not understand that will end up with a gap in coverage. Telling them about it is not a courtesy; it is the job.

**The referral duty (8).** This is the duty that keeps you inside your lane. An encounter that is not yours — a benefits problem, a complaint about how a claim was handled, a disability support question — goes to the right body. Naming the wrong destination is worse than naming none, so the list of five destinations is worth keeping in your head as a list.

Look at the shape of duty 9 as well: renewals during open enrolment, and new special-enrolment applications, both carried out through the web-based enrolment programme. It is one of the two ways the role is used after the initial enrolment.

One more name from the same part of the training, so you recognise it: CalHEERS, the California Health Care Eligibility, Enrollment and Retention System, is the online portal used to enrol individuals and families into health and dental coverage, or to connect them with Medi-Cal. The training covered it in a separate course, so here it is only a name to recognise.

## Stage 2: The prohibitions

And here is the other half. An enroller must not do any of these eight things.

| #   | The prohibition                                                                                                                  |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Charge consumers a fee for the enrolment assistance you give them                                                                |
| 2   | Fail to disclose a conflict of interest                                                                                          |
| 3   | Steer a consumer into giving inaccurate information on the application                                                           |
| 4   | Steer a consumer toward particular plans on the strength of the commission schedule alone                                        |
| 5   | Take payment of the premium from the consumer, or pay any part of it, or any other consideration, to or on the consumer's behalf |
| 6   | Induce or accept from the consumer any direct or indirect remuneration at all                                                    |
| 7   | Intentionally put more than one application in the system for a single household                                                 |
| 8   | Invite, influence or otherwise arrange for an individual to leave an employer-sponsored plan                                     |

**The three that get collapsed into one.** Rules 1, 5 and 6 are three different rules, and in ordinary conversation they become a single vague idea called taking money. Kept apart, they are precise.

| Rule | What it is actually about           | Typical confusion                                                                      |
| ---- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | The enroller's own fee for the help | That disclosure makes a fee acceptable                                                 |
| 5    | The premium money itself            | That paying part of the premium out of kindness is different from handling the premium |
| 6    | Anything else the consumer gives    | That a thank-you gift is not payment                                                   |

Rule 1 is about the enroller's own fee, and it is flatly forbidden — not a monthly fee, not a one-off fee, not a fee disclosed in advance. Rule 5 is about the premium, and it bites in both directions: you may not take the consumer's premium money, and you may not pay any part of that premium, or any other consideration, to or on their behalf. So a generous agent cannot offer to cover the first month either, because that is paying part of the premium on the consumer's behalf. Rule 6 covers everything else the consumer might give, and it bars both inducing it and accepting it once offered — a thank-you gift, a referral fee from a friend, cash handed over in an envelope.

**The two steering rules.** Rules 3 and 4 are also a pair, and they have different objects. Rule 3 is about the application: do not steer a consumer into giving inaccurate information, which means do not coach them into putting a number in the income box that is not their income. Rule 4 is about the plan list: do not steer a consumer toward particular plans solely because of the commission schedule. One is about what the consumer writes; the other is about which plans they are pushed toward.

**Rule 7 sounds narrow and is not.** Putting more than one application into the system for a household on purpose is prohibited, whatever the purpose — comparing two subsidy amounts included. A second application is not a shopping tool.

**Rule 8 protects employer coverage.** An enroller may not invite, influence or otherwise arrange for an individual to leave an employer-sponsored plan. If a consumer has employer coverage, the honest position is to help them understand what they have.

The official Agent Code of Conduct puts two of these in its own words, and it is worth knowing because it is enforceable in its own way. It says agencies and agents may not charge any fee to consumers for performing any service under the agreement, and that recommendations must rest on the consumer's best interests rather than being influenced by financial compensation and incentives from health issuers. The same code bars charging consumers translation fees. Breaching the code is a breach of the agency agreement, which can end the agreement and cost the agent the right to sell Covered California plans.

## Stage 3: When an agent gets it wrong

The training had taken calls from consumers in exactly this situation. A consumer says their insurance agent is charging them a monthly fee for assistance they were not supposed to charge for — not supposed to charge for anything at all, in fact, including taking the consumer's payment details. Sometimes the agent's phone number is no longer in service.

The response has four parts, and the order matters.

1. **Apologise** on the agent's behalf.
2. **Show empathy** — say the kind of thing that lets the consumer feel heard.
3. **Point to the complaint route.** The consumer can file a complaint, and can escalate it to the fraud department if they want to.
4. **Be honest about the limit.** The service center does not control that agent and cannot act on the agent directly.

The fourth part is the one that decides your tone. Saying plainly that you cannot discipline the agent is not a brush-off — it is what lets the consumer understand what will actually happen next, and it stops you promising something the service center has no power to deliver. What the service center can do is route the consumer to the right door.

The official record names two of those doors: the Covered California fraud line for fraud complaints, and the California Department of Insurance consumer hotline for agent or broker fraud.

**The analogy.** A consumer in this situation is holding a bill from a contractor and asking the housing office to sort it out. The housing office cannot overrule the contractor's invoice, but it can tell the consumer which regulator to call and what to put in the complaint. That is exactly the shape of this call.

## Stage 4: Non-discrimination

An enroller must never discriminate against a consumer on the basis of any characteristic in the table below.

| Characteristic                                | What you must not do                                                                        |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Race, ethnicity, culture                      | Treat the consumer differently because of their background or heritage                      |
| Gender and age                                | Treat the consumer differently because of their gender or their years                       |
| Sexual orientation and gender identity        | Treat the consumer differently because of how they describe themselves                      |
| National origin and religion                  | Treat the consumer differently because of where their family came from or what they believe |
| Mental or physical disability                 | Treat the consumer differently because of what they can or cannot do                        |
| Higher or lower income area                   | Treat the consumer differently because of the area they live in                             |
| Health status                                 | Treat the consumer differently because of the consumer's health                             |
| Eligibility status or claims experience       | Treat the consumer differently because of what has happened to them in the market before    |
| Genetic information, evidence of insurability | Treat the consumer differently because of what might happen to the consumer medically       |

Read the list as a set of things a consumer might differ on, and notice the three that are easy to skip over. Eligibility status and claims experience are about what has already happened to a consumer in the insurance market. Genetic information and evidence of insurability are about what might happen to them medically in the future. A consumer's genetic information is not a reason to treat them differently, and neither is a claims history that looks expensive.

One word in the list deserves attention. It says income **area**, not income — so the area a consumer lives in is protected, not only the consumer's own circumstances.

## Stage 5: Accessibility

Four duties, and they are about how you provide help rather than what you say.

- Consumer assistance must be accessible to people with disabilities.
- Enrolment resources, websites and tools must be accessible to individuals with disabilities.
- Assistance has to be given in a way that is physically and otherwise accessible, and in an accessible location.
- Maintain enough knowledge to make a referral, when appropriate, to long-term services and support programmes for people with disabilities.

The reference point for that last one is the California Commission on Disability Access.

That last duty is the easiest to get wrong, because it is a duty to know something rather than a duty to do something. You are not expected to run a long-term services and support programme. You are expected to know enough to point someone to one, and the Commission on Disability Access is where that knowledge comes from.

The official record adds the practical side. As of July 2026 the site is documented as accessible under the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.1 at Level AA, there is a TTY line, and reasonable modifications and free aids and services — sign language interpreters, auxiliary aids, and written information in other formats such as large print and audio — are provided for people with disabilities.

### Check yourself

Before you close the lesson, answer these in writing, then check them against the stages above.

1. Name the five destinations an enroller refers to.
2. Write out all eight prohibitions in your own words, without merging any two.
3. Say what is wrong with an agent who offers to pay the consumer's first month.
4. Say what is wrong with an agent who suggests a consumer enter a higher income to make the premium look affordable.
5. Say what an agent who steers a consumer toward the plan with the best commission schedule has done, even if the plan is also the cheapest one.
6. Outline the four steps for a consumer whose agent is charging a monthly fee.
7. Name three characteristics on the non-discrimination list that a new enroller is most likely to forget.

## Review

Close this lesson and write down, from memory:

1. The nine duties, with particular attention to the first-payment duty and the five referral destinations.
2. All eight prohibitions, kept as eight separate rules.
3. The difference between prohibition 1, prohibition 5 and prohibition 6.
4. The difference between prohibition 3 and prohibition 4.
5. The four steps to take when a consumer says their agent is charging them a fee, and the limit on what the service center can do.
6. The bases on which you must never discriminate, and the four accessibility duties.

Then check the areas people consistently miss: that the charging rules are three and not one, that steering on commissions is prohibited while telling a consumer about premium costs is required, and that the accessibility duty about long-term services and support is a duty to know, not a duty to run the programme.
