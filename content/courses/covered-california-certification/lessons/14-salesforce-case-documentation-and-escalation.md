# Salesforce: Case Documentation, Quick Sort, and Escalation

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

## What you will be able to do

- Set up a case so that the record tells the next person what happened, without a rewrite.
- Say what belongs in the description, what belongs in the closing notes, and how to put right a case you closed early.
- Find your way round the knowledge library, search it, and use the daily review time.
- Run a quick sort from Salesforce, collect its six answers, and act on whichever of the two results comes back.
- Escalate a case correctly, in the right order, and know what stays your responsibility afterwards.

## Stage 1: The case and its fields

Salesforce is the system the service centre works in. A consumer has a **contact record**, and the conversations and the work sit on **cases** attached to it. A case carries a **subject**, a **description**, your **notes**, a **status**, a **case reason** with a sub-reason, and a **resolution reason**.

Two of those fields behave differently, and the difference runs through the whole lesson:

- The **subject** says what the call is about — report a change, online assistance, a termination date change, termination or disenrollment cancellation (00:13:21–00:13:28; 00:17:42–00:18:12). It can be edited.
- There is also an optional **secondary case reason** for the narrower thing that was done on the call — resetting a password, for instance (00:13:06–00:13:16). Leaving it empty is acceptable, as long as your notes record what you actually did (00:12:46–00:13:06).
- The **description** is the one field you cannot edit once the case has been created (00:12:32–00:13:21).

> **The habit this creates.** Decide your description late, and put the facts
> somewhere you can still change.

## Stage 2: Description, closing notes, and the record

**The description** is a brief one or two sentence account of what is happening (00:15:15–00:15:21). It is a summary, not the record.

**The closing notes** are where the detail goes. The shape of a good one is visible in the recording's worked example: the request as completed, the CalHEERS case number, the primary call and what you did about it, the effect — an income change from $30,000 to $40,000 with the application resubmitted and a new premium confirmed — the effective date, the plan the consumer is on, and anything else you did, such as reminding them they will receive tax forms, verifying their communication preference, or advising them to ask the carrier to remove auto-pay (00:13:43–00:15:03; 00:18:12–00:18:40).

The point of putting the detail in the notes rather than the description is that the description is fixed. A consumer who says "actually, I do need a password reset" two minutes before the end of the call has changed the subject of the call after the description was written, and the only place left for it is the closing notes (00:12:19–00:12:36; 00:15:21–00:15:41).

**Take the notes as you go**, in whatever you work in — the recording shows a representative copying and pasting from a quick sort into OneNote — and bring them across when the case is saved (01:02:21–01:02:40).

**If you have already closed the case before you finished documenting**, the fix is in the Feed tab: open **Post** and add the extra notes there (00:15:51–00:16:12). Those notes do not appear in the case view, but they are visible in the feed and they are part of what the quality review looks at (00:16:51–00:17:08).

There is also a knowledge article holding examples of documented cases, and templates for documented escalating cases (00:17:26–00:18:54).

> **What "documented" means in practice.** Everything that was done during the
> interaction, recorded. A reviewer reading only your notes should be able to
> reconstruct what happened without listening to the call.

## Stage 3: The knowledge library

The knowledge library is a database of approved information that exists to help process and resolve consumer inquiries. It is updated frequently, and it is searchable so that answers can be found quickly (01:24:39–01:25:00).

**Three ways in** (01:33:17–01:34:14):

1. From a **CRM case**, the search-knowledge filter surfaces suggested articles based on the case's subject.
2. The **global search bar** — choose Knowledge in the dropdown, then enter a keyword.
3. From the primary tab, switch to the **Knowledge** tab.

Inside an article, the browser's own find function is what you use to search within the document, so you can go straight to a section such as reinstatement, termination or online access (01:26:16–01:26:42).

**What the library holds.** The content is divided into topic articles, and there are several kinds of article: **job aids**, which walk a procedure step by step; **task guides**; **policy and procedures** for complicated tasks; a **general summary** of basic health-care information; and a category of short, pinpoint articles that the recording calls BAM articles — for example on what the documentation must contain, on handling appeals, or on the service centre's own steps for verifying global presence (01:34:30–01:35:01). Inside a topic article sit task guides and job aids, which are expanded overviews and link onwards to sibling and related articles (01:35:10–01:36:44).

**Fifteen minutes at the start of the day.** Every morning, before the first call of a shift in production, there is 15 minutes of review time, spent in a status called **Daily Review**. In it you can review new articles and newly updated ones — from Knowledge you can open new articles, published articles, or recently viewed (01:36:56–01:38:15).

**And the rule that comes with it.** The expectation is that you do your own due diligence — research the articles first — before asking the lead queue for assistance. The first step is always to try to self-service (01:24:52–01:25:13; 01:26:52–01:27:13).

> **Why the rule exists.** The library is the first place an answer is looked
> for, and a lead who gets an escalation with no research behind it is being
> asked to do the looking for you.

## Stage 4: When a quick sort is required

A quick sort is the one thing the service centre does that reaches out to the county — it is the only time the county is contacted (00:32:19–00:32:26).

It is **required on every call about a new application for coverage where the consumer has no CalHEERS case yet and nobody in their household is already on Medi-Cal** (00:32:26–00:32:39). Whatever the consumer's projected income or circumstances, a quick sort can be run from Salesforce (00:32:39–00:32:46).

**Two routes.** You can run it from a **CRM contact** — the drop-down menu in the corner of the contact, then _Run quick sort_ — or from a **CRM case**. Either works; the recording's preference was the case, because the case already has its subject and description in place (00:32:55–00:33:13; 01:00:15–01:02:10).

**The six questions** (00:33:18–00:33:49):

1. How many people are in your family?
2. How many children in the family are under 19?
3. Are any of your family members pregnant?
4. Is anyone in the family 65 or more?
5. Are any of your family members disabled?
6. What is your annual income?

Then you click next, and one of two results comes back.

## Stage 5: The two results

### Transfer

The result reads: _Transfer. Contact should be transferred to county._ The steps are to pick the consumer's county and their language in the drop-down, click next, and the county's phone number and the tracer then appear. Click finish, and the CRM case is created — with these fields filled in automatically: status **Closed**, case reason **Medi-Cal**, a sub-reason recording a quick sort that transferred to the county, along with the subject, description and resolution reason (00:33:49–00:34:53).

**The warm transfer itself.** The automated system asks for the two-digit county code, then the two-digit language code, and finally the tracer ID. With all three, you are connected to a county worker (00:42:14–00:42:41). The opening given to the county worker says that a quick sort has just been run for a household member who could qualify for Medi-Cal today (00:42:48–00:42:59).

**None of those three numbers goes to the consumer.** The county phone number is an internal number for the transfer; the county only assists the service centre with warm transfers, and a consumer ringing it directly is a red flag that the number has been handed out. The tracer ID is described in the recording as for your eyes only (00:41:22–00:41:38; 00:42:21–00:42:31).

### No transfer

The second result is _No transfer_, and the wording beside it on screen says that a case has been set up for the request. Click finish, and a CRM case is created and **stays in progress**, with case reason **New customer** and subject **New customer quick sort** (00:51:32–00:52:17).

On a no-transfer result the county is **not** called — the household is most likely not eligible for Medi-Cal on the answers given — and the case remains open (00:52:44–00:52:58). What you do instead is walk the consumer through submitting an application in CalHEERS: _Apply for individual_, which starts a new case, then _Apply Now_, and then you help them submit (00:53:24–00:53:36; 00:54:02–00:54:19).

Once the application has been submitted, the ten-digit CalHEERS case number, which starts with a 5, attaches to the case (00:54:19–00:54:30).

**You do not have to give the consumer that number.** A letter is generated and sent to their preferred method of contact, carrying the 10-digit number in the corner beside their name and address. A consumer with a registered online account can get the number from that account too; if they have no account, you help them create one (00:54:41–00:55:28).

> **The difference in one line.** A transfer sends the consumer to a county
> worker inside the call. A no-transfer sends the consumer down the CalHEERS
> application path with you.

## Stage 6: The auto-closed case still needs documenting

On a transfer, the case closes itself, and the training was explicit that the system does this for you the moment the result is a transfer, whereas a no-transfer leaves the case in progress (00:52:58–00:53:24).

The documentation is still yours. Asked whether a transfer still has to be documented, the answer was yes, and the reason given was the review: a 30-minute call with nothing in it but a note that the consumer rang is not acceptable documentation (00:55:36–00:56:29).

So on a transfer you post your notes in the **Feed**. What belongs in them: that the consumer rang to start a new application and to find out where they stand, that they are a brand new applicant and a quick sort was performed, the six answers, that a transfer result came back, and that a county eligibility worker took over the call (00:57:03–00:58:20).

The notes do not have to repeat every answer word for word — a reviewer would be content with the quick sort and the transfer being recorded (01:03:20–01:03:47). But the practical trap is timing: paste the county phone number, the language code and the tracer ID out of the quick sort into your notes **before** you click Finish, because after Finish they are gone (01:04:06–01:04:31).

> **The habit.** The auto-close is not an excuse. The close was the system's
> doing; the notes are yours.

## Stage 7: Escalation

**What escalation is.** Some CRM cases cannot be handled at the service centre or at team-lead level and need to go to the priority support unit or out to external resolution — an access-to-care issue, or a back-dated termination, are the examples given (00:31:13–00:32:01).

**How it is done, in order** (00:31:32–00:32:06; 01:28:46–01:30:03):

1. Use the **Change Owner** button to put the case into the queue it needs.
2. Go to the **Feed** tab and add a **post** to tag the person the case is escalated to.
3. To tag them, type the **@** symbol, begin typing their name, and **pick their name from the list that pops up**. It then shows as a hyperlink with their name. A tag that is typed but never picked raises no alert at all.
4. On a live call: **post your notes first**, put the consumer on hold, then in Amazon Connect click **Lead line**. The next level of support picks the call up straight away and can see the notes. The consumer can be held for no more than four minutes (00:51:10–00:51:22).

Before any of that, do the research yourself — the knowledge articles first, the lead queue second (01:24:52–01:25:13).

**What happens next, and what stays yours.** When the escalation is valid, the person you escalated to takes over the call. Your responsibility is assisting the consumer: you identify a valid escalation, you escalate it, and you move on. You are **not** responsible for following up with the consumer, for giving them the escalation results, or for emailing them — there is a whole department for that (01:32:41–01:33:05).

> **The line.** Escalating is not handing the consumer over and disappearing. It
> is handing the case to someone better placed, having first done the work the
> case needs.

## Review

1. A consumer asks for one thing, and two minutes before the end of the call adds
   a second. Where does each of those go, and which of the two fields can you no
   longer change?
2. You close a case and only then realise you left out half of what you did.
   What do you do, and where does that end up?
3. A quick sort comes back with a transfer result. Which three items of
   information do you need for the automated system, and which of them must not
   reach the consumer?
4. A quick sort comes back with no transfer. What is the case status, and what
   do you do with the consumer next?
5. Name the six questions a quick sort asks.
6. A consumer cannot be helped from what is on the case. What do you do before
   you escalate, and what do you do when you do escalate?
7. You have escalated a case and the person you escalated to has taken over the
   call. What are you still responsible for, and what are you not?
8. A consumer wants to know their CalHEERS case number. Do you have to give it
   to them, and how do they get it if you do not?

Then, for each of the traps below, write the **trigger** — the wording that
would have pulled you into the wrong answer — next to the correction. A trap
