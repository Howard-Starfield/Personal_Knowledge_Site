# Appeals and Medi-Cal Boundaries

> [!tip] How to use this lesson
> The source for this lesson is training session 2026-10-09 12-36-27. Every stage ends with something you have to be able to say out loud, because the boundary rules only matter when a consumer is on the line waiting.

Points marked with the star carry the training's own emphasis.

## What you will be able to do

- Say what an appeal is, who submits it, and by when it has to be submitted.
- Recognise an active appeal on a case, and leave that case alone.
- Send a consumer to the right place for an appeal, and to the right place for everything that is not an appeal.
- Explain the line between what Covered California decides and what the county decides on a Medi-Cal case.
- Use wording that does not promise a consumer something the system will not give.

## Stage 1: What an appeal is and who files it

An appeal is a request to review a coverage decision. The consumer submits it.

The training was blunt about the boundary here. You can explain how the process works, and you can help the consumer fill the form out over the phone. What you do not do is file it for them. Staff do not file appeals or complaints on a consumer's behalf, and the consumer is the one who ultimately completes the form and sends it. Give them the case number, give them the route, and let them send it.

The ways a consumer can file, as the training described them:

- Online, through the Covered California website, by searching for the page on filing an appeal.
- By email, by fax, or by upload, following the instructions on the form.
- By phone, with the Health Consumer Alliance.
- Directly with the local county office, because an appeal can concern Medi-Cal as well as Covered California, and the county is part of the route.

The official site adds one detail the training did not spell out. It separates the two documents. An appeal is filed by filling out the appeal form and mailing it, while a complaint is filed online. On the official page about filing an appeal, the two routes are different documents for different problems.

### Is this an appeal at all?

The official site carries a routing table, and it is worth learning, because most of what arrives on your phone is not an appeal.

| What the consumer's problem is                                                            | Where it goes                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| A decision about eligibility, financial help, or enrolment                                | An appeal, to Covered California   |
| The service, the website, the enrolment process, or how a representative handled the call | A complaint, to Covered California |
| The insurance company itself, such as a claim, a provider, or a refusal of service        | The insurance company              |
| A complaint that has already been through the Service Centre and is unresolved            | The Ombuds Office                  |

Two things follow from that table. A consumer who is angry about a claim or a doctor has not brought you an appeal, and neither a quick sort nor a transfer will help them. And a consumer whose complaint has already been through the Service Centre may need the Ombuds Office, which only opens after the Service Centre has had its turn.

An appeal can be filed for the consumer or on behalf of any household member listed on the application, and the official site asks that each name be written on the form so it is clear whose decision is being appealed. The form is available in several languages, and in Braille and large print, and help filling it out is available by telephone.

### The deadlines

The official page states the deadlines, and they were confirmed live in the package's verification audit:

- An appeal generally has to be filed within 90 days of the date Covered California mailed the eligibility decision.
- The hearing process and the decision take up to 90 days from the date Covered California receives a valid appeal request.
- A complaint is answered within 30 business days.

The training put the first of those this way: the consumer has 90 calendar days from the notice date of a Covered California or Medi-Cal determination to submit an appeal. During that period Covered California works with the consumer to try to settle it informally. If it cannot, a hearing is scheduled and held, and then a decision is issued.

There is one further step the training covered that the official page for filing an appeal does not carry. If a consumer is not satisfied with an appeal decision about enrolment in a Covered California plan, premium assistance, or cost-sharing reductions, the training said the consumer can file an appeal directly with the federal Department of Health and Human Services within 30 days of the date of that decision. Treat that as the training's statement. It is useful to know, and it is not the number to quote for the first appeal.

One caution. The training also said that a consumer can file an appeal any time they like. Read that as reassurance that the process is open to them, not as a deadline. The 90-day window on the official page is the working rule, so do not tell a consumer that time does not matter.

## Stage 2: The freeze on an active appeal

An active appeal shows on the case as a green check mark next to the contact record, and the subject line may mention an informal resolution.

★ The training's instruction is the one to memorise: when there is an active appeal, do not touch the consumer's case. That means no reported change, no added coverage, and no terminated coverage. The case is frozen while the appeal runs.

What you do instead:

- Refer the consumer to their appeals analyst.
- Give them the analyst's number.
- Do not transfer them, whatever they ask for.

The official site says the same thing in its own words. On the page about filing an appeal, under the heading for consumers who already have an active appeal, it says not to take additional action on the case without guidance, and to contact Covered California for help.

You still authenticate the consumer as you always would, and you still document the call as you always would. The freeze is on the case, not on the call.

And if the appeals analyst cannot reach an agreement with the consumer, the analyst escalates it to court and schedules a hearing for the consumer and a judge. That escalation is the analyst's job, not yours.

## Stage 3: Two numbers with two different jobs

Two numbers come up constantly on appeals calls, and they are not interchangeable.

The State Hearings Division number is for appeals. Strictly appeals. If a consumer's problem is an appeal and nothing else, that is the number.

The Health Consumer Alliance number covers appeals and everything else that surrounds them: billing problems, medical debt, legal advice, and immigration questions. The training described it as free assistance, and the official site confirms the free legal help: the Health Consumer Alliance offers free legal advice and representation, by telephone or in person, and it is a network of ten consumer assistance programmes open Monday to Friday from nine in the morning to five in the afternoon. The number published on the official pages is (888) 804-3536.

The Medi-Cal Helpline, (800) 541-5555, and the DHCS Ombuds Office, (888) 452-8609, are published on the official site for Medi-Cal problems.

Two limits worth knowing, both from the official site: Covered California cannot give legal advice, and a complaint reaches the Ombuds Office only after the Service Centre has had its turn.

## Stage 4: Where the Medi-Cal line sits

Medi-Cal is California's Medicaid programme, run by the Department of Health Care Services rather than by Covered California. It is financed with federal and state funding, and it offers low or no cost health coverage to California residents who meet the eligibility requirements.

### One application, two programmes

The official site's own words: one application covers both Covered California and Medi-Cal. Said more fully, one application checks your eligibility for both Covered California and Medi-Cal, and shows you your options. The consumer does not choose between the two. Their application determines which one they qualify for.

★ The training made the same point from the caller's side. Every consumer who completes a subsidised Covered California application is automatically reviewed for Medi-Cal eligibility. It is not one or the other. On the screen, the question is whether the consumer would like to see if they qualify for free or low cost coverage. A yes sends the application down the Medi-Cal screening path. A no means the consumer is looking at unsubsidised coverage.

### Who decides what

Here is the boundary, and it is the reason this lesson exists.

Covered California's role is to determine whether an applicant may be Medi-Cal eligible, and then to pass that information to the county of the applicant's residence through the online application. The county makes the determination.

So on a case that turns out to be a Medi-Cal matter, the consumer goes to their local county office. What you do not do is report a change on a Medi-Cal case, add a plan, cancel a plan, or reset the consumer's password. None of that is yours to do. What you do do is authenticate the consumer as usual and document the call as usual.

This is why the training framed it as a transfer after a quick sort. When a quick sort comes back as a transfer, the service centre contacts the county. When it comes back with no transfer, the consumer is given the county's details and goes to the county themselves. Either way the county is the decider of Medi-Cal eligibility, and either way you authenticate and document as usual.

### When Medi-Cal can be applied for

The official site states the difference plainly. Medi-Cal can be applied for at any time of year. Covered California requires open enrolment, or a special enrolment period after a major life change.

The training added the income shape of it. Expanded Medi-Cal under the Affordable Care Act is open to every household with an annual income below 138 percent of the federal poverty level, and there are many programmes within Medi-Cal, and programmes specific to certain counties, that a consumer may qualify for beyond the standard path through Covered California. The official site gives the same figures: most adults qualify with a household income up to 138 percent of the federal poverty level, and children up to 266 percent. If the tax household's income is above the Medi-Cal limits, the applicant is determined eligible for Covered California, with or without financial assistance.

One more thing the training said, and it is useful when a consumer is anxious about a gap: if a consumer is eligible for Medi-Cal, they may be eligible for coverage for the whole month in which they were found eligible, and in some cases for coverage right away.

## Stage 5: The words you use with the consumer

★ The training's wording rule: do not tell a consumer that Medi-Cal is free. Say low or no cost health coverage.

The reason is not politeness. Medi-Cal can be free, and for many people it is. But some people still pay out of pocket, for premiums and for other costs, so a promise of free coverage becomes a complaint later when a bill arrives. The official site's own wording carries the same hedge, describing Medi-Cal as covering health, dental and vision care often at little or no cost. The word often is doing real work.

Three more sentences to have ready:

- After a quick sort, the consumer may qualify for Medi-Cal. You are quoting what the screen found, not a decision, because the county decides.
- Covered California has not approved the consumer for Medi-Cal and cannot do so. The county makes that decision once the application reaches them.
- The consumer applies for Medi-Cal through their county office, and they can apply at any time of year.

What you never say: that Medi-Cal is free and always will be, that the consumer has been approved for Medi-Cal, or that Covered California will make the Medi-Cal change for them.

## Stage 6: Two households and two definitions

A case can sit on the wrong side of the Medi-Cal line because of who is counted in the household, so this is the last piece of the boundary.

The Covered California household is the tax household. It is the tax filers and all their tax dependents.

The Medi-Cal household is not always the same group of people. The training gave three reasons it differs:

- Married couples who live together are always counted in the same household, whether they file their taxes jointly or separately.
- Children are always counted as part of the household in which they live with their parents and siblings, regardless of who claims them as tax dependents.
- Medi-Cal has its own rules for counting a pregnant individual, and those rules take the number of children expected into account.

★ The training's own example: a pregnant individual expecting twins is counted as one person by Covered California, and as three people under Medi-Cal rules. That is the clearest single illustration of where the two programmes part company, and it is why an answer built on the tax household alone can be wrong for Medi-Cal.

The official site confirms the effect from the other side. Medi-Cal uses a household's income, but the limit is different for each person depending on age or situation, and that is what produces mixed programme families. Same family, two programmes, two different household definitions, two different answers.
