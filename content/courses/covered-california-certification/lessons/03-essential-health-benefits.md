# Essential Health Benefits and Preventive Care

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

## What you will be able to do

- Name all ten essential health benefit categories, in order, from memory.
- Explain which plans the requirement to cover them reaches, and which two markets it applies to.
- List the preventive services that carry no copayment, coinsurance or deductible.
- Explain why a required benefit category does not mean the services inside it are free.

## Step 1: What the law requires, and of which plans

The law requires a standard set of essential health benefits to be carried by every qualifying plan. Two qualifiers sit inside that sentence, and both are easy to drop when you are talking at speed.

The first qualifier is **non-grandfathered**. The requirement does not reach every plan that was ever sold. The training did not go into how grandfathered status is decided, so if a consumer asks how the line is drawn, the honest answer is that the determination belongs to the plan and the marketplace rather than to a representative on a phone call. Do not improvise it.

The second qualifier is the pair of markets: **individual and small-group**. Those are the two markets the standardisation covers, and how a consumer's coverage is arranged decides which rules apply to them. How employer coverage works is a separate subject.

What the requirement does is set a floor on what a plan has to offer. It does not say anything about what the member pays. Those are two different things, and holding them apart is the whole of the next two steps.

## Step 2: The ten benefit categories

In both the individual and the small-group market, every qualifying plan has to cover these ten categories:

1. Ambulatory patient services
2. Emergency services
3. Hospitalization
4. Maternity and newborn care
5. Mental health and substance use disorder services, including behavioural health treatment
6. Prescription drugs
7. Rehabilitative and habilitative services and devices
8. Laboratory services
9. Preventive and wellness services, and chronic disease management
10. Paediatric services, including oral and vision care

That is the complete list of ten, not a sample of them. The training was explicit that these benefits are offered across all of the current California plans, and the official site puts it the same way: no matter which plan you choose, they all include the same essential health benefits.

So when you describe them, give all ten and say that there are ten. A partial list leaves a consumer wondering what the rest are, and it misstates a rule that is a closed set.

Three details in that list get lost more often than the rest. Behavioural health treatment is named inside the mental health and substance use disorder category, not as a category of its own. The rehabilitative and habilitative category covers services **and devices**. And the paediatric category explicitly includes oral and vision care.

## Step 3: Preventive care carries no cost sharing

Inside all of this there is one carve-out, and it is the part a consumer notices first, because it is the part where money does not change hands.

**Preventive care is not subject to copayments, coinsurance or deductibles.** Vaccinations and medical screenings sit outside the cost-sharing system entirely.

The training named these services:

- Mammograms
- Colonoscopies
- Wellness visits
- Gestational diabetes screening

It also noted that women have access to further preventive care and screenings, including approved contraceptive methods.

The official site adds two points that make the carve-out easier to explain. First, preventive care is free and covered from the first day of coverage, and it is free in every coverage level, so a member's choice of plan never changes what a screening costs. Second, there is a boundary worth knowing: if the consumer brings up a new health problem during a preventive visit, the plan's normal costs may apply to that part of the care. The preventive services themselves are still free. The free part is the preventive service, not the whole appointment.

## Step 4: A required category is not the same as a free service

Here is the trap, and it is the one that catches people who have understood the rule but not reached the end of it.

**A required category does not mean every service inside it is free.** The requirement is that the category must be covered. Cost sharing still applies to the services in it. What removes cost sharing is the separate preventive carve-out, and it removes it only for the preventive services it names.

So a Bronze member's emergency room visit is covered and is still paid for out of pocket. A specialist consultation is covered and still carries cost sharing. A hospital admission is covered and still carries cost sharing. Preventive care is free, and everything else in the category is subject to the plan's deductibles, copayments and out-of-pocket maximum.

The official site makes the same point from its side: its list of covered services is a general overview, and the detail of what a member pays comes from the plan's own benefits.

**The analogy.** Think of the ten categories as the ten departments of a store every shopper is entitled to walk into. The sign on the door says you may enter any of them. It does not say everything on the shelves is free. The entrance is covered. The goods are not.

The one thing that is genuinely free of charge, in every plan, is the preventive care: the vaccinations, the screenings and the named preventive services.

## Review

Close the lesson and write down, from memory:

1. All ten benefit categories, in order, and the two qualifiers on the requirement.
2. The preventive services that carry no copayment, coinsurance or deductible, and the extra ones named for women.
3. The boundary on a preventive visit when a new health problem comes up.
4. Why a covered category does not make the services inside it free.

Then check the two things people consistently get wrong: describing the ten categories as a sample rather than a closed list, and treating the whole of a required category as free.
