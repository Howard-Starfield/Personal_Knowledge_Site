# How a Household Is Grouped in CalHEERS

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

Points marked ★ carry the emphasis the training placed on them.

## What you will be able to do

- Say what an enrollment group is, and how it differs from the household the application decides.
- Name the situations in which CalHEERS separates a household without being asked.
- Walk a consumer through Manage Groups, Edit groups and Add a group.
- Say what happens to the premium help, and to the cost sharing, when members are separated.
- Regroup a household that is already enrolled, in the order the steps have to happen.
- Know when to raise regrouping, and when to say nothing at all.

## Step 1: The household and the enrollment group

Two different things get called a household, and keeping them apart is the whole lesson.

**The household** is the set of people the application decides together. Who is in it, who counts as a dependent and what income is counted are all settled by the application rather than by you, and that is covered in the application lesson.

**The enrollment group** is a shopping group inside the household. A group is what carries a plan, a premium and, once enrolled, its own deductible and its own out-of-pocket maximum. Members in one group buy the same plan; members in different groups can buy different plans.

★ **Same application, different groups.** Everyone on one application still shares one household. Splitting them into groups does not split the household, it splits the plan choice.

**Why the distinction matters.** It is the reason a household can be eligible together and still need to be enrolled apart, and it is the reason two members of one household can end up with two deductibles instead of one family deductible.

## Step 2: What CalHEERS does on its own

On the enrollment dashboard, CalHEERS sorts household members into groups before anyone touches anything. The rule behind it is the eligibility result.

**Same eligibility, same group.** Members who come out of the application with the same eligibility are placed together automatically. The training's example was a household of four who all qualified for financial assistance and all qualified for Covered California: one group, sitting on a recommended group type.

**Three things split a household on its own.**

1. **American Indian or Alaska Native status.** A member with federally recognised AI/AN status is placed apart from the rest, because only AI/AN members have access to the tribal plans. In the training's example the three members who were not AI/AN stayed together because they shared the same eligibility, and the AI/AN member was grouped separately.
2. **Subsidised and unsubsidised.** When some members' eligibility is subsidised and some is not, those members are grouped separately as well.
3. **Federally recognised and non-federally recognised AI/AN.** A household that mixes members with federally recognised AI/AN status and members without it is grouped separately.

**A note on the training's own knowledge check.** The session tested this with a multiple-answer item whose options were financial help, cost-sharing reductions and American Indian or Alaska Native status, and the answer given was all three. The training was openly unsure about that item, saying the underlying rule is that members with the same eligibility are grouped together and members with different eligibility are not, and that it had never seen a household in which everyone was AI/AN. Treat the same-eligibility rule as the safe spine of your answer.

## Step 3: Changing the grouping yourself

CalHEERS lets you regroup a household so that members with different coverage needs can buy different plans. The training's slide put it this way: different coverage needs may come from a member needing doctors who do not take part in the same plan, or from a member needing a higher-tier plan to bring service or prescription costs down.

The path, starting at the enrollment dashboard:

1. Select **Manage Groups**.
2. On the manage your groups page, select **Edit groups**.
3. On the edit your groups page, select **Add a group**. This creates Group 2.
4. Move members across. You can drag and drop a member from Group 1 into Group 2, or use the **Move** link on that member and confirm the move.
5. There is a second route to the same place: the **Change group type** link, which arranges the groups for you.

★ **The training's preference.** The training said it rarely used the automatic group-type option and preferred to set the groups by hand, dragging members across one at a time, because that keeps the result clear.

## Step 4: The four group types

The **Change group type** link offers four ways of arranging a household:

| Group type        | What it does                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Recommended**   | The arrangement CalHEERS suggests, which is the one the dashboard opens on.                                                                                                   |
| **All together**  | Everybody in one group, on one plan.                                                                                                                                          |
| **Each separate** | Every member in their own group. The training noted that the costs are often highest in this arrangement.                                                                     |
| **Custom**        | The consumer builds the groups. The training noted that costs may be higher, and that the option can help when some household members see a different doctor from the others. |

## Step 5: What separating does to the money

**The premium help is split.** When members who are eligible for financial assistance are moved into separate groups, CalHEERS divides the household's Advance Premium Tax Credit and the California Premium Credit among the groups.

**The worked example.** A household of four, all with the same eligibility, all in one group:

- One plan for all four: a Kaiser Silver 73 HMO at a net monthly premium of $557.38.
- The parents separated out onto a Kaiser Bronze 60 HMO: $147.25 a month for the two of them.
- The children kept the Silver 73 HMO: $210.29 a month.
- The household's total monthly net premium falls by $199.84.

The training's reading of that: separating the parents from the children saved almost $200 a month, and this kind of grouping can make a real difference in a four- or five-member household where one member really needs a Platinum plan that the whole household could not afford on premiums alone.

**The cost-sharing consequence.** Separated groups do not share a family deductible. Each group carries its own deductible and its own out-of-pocket maximum, so a household split across two groups is looking at two sets of limits rather than one. The plan-benefit session made the same point from the other side: if everyone is happy with the same plan, everyone should be in the same group, and the family deductible is shared.

**The trap inside a cheap premium.** In the training's live demonstration, one of the options was a high deductible health plan HMO at $0 a month against a regular Bronze 60 PPO at $281.44. The $0 premium is real, and so is the catch: the consumer would pay 100 percent of their out-of-pocket costs until the deductible was met, which the training put at $7,200.

## Step 6: Regrouping a household that is already enrolled

A consumer who is already enrolled cannot simply be moved into a new group. The training's demonstration showed the order the steps have to happen in:

1. **Cancel the existing plans first.** In the demonstration the representative tried to separate the members and was stopped, because the plan had to be cancelled before the household could be regrouped.
2. **Regroup everyone.**
3. **Re-enroll everyone.**

★ **Cancel, then regroup, then re-enroll.** Skipping the cancellation does not work.

## Step 7: When to raise it, and when not to

**The trigger.** You will know a consumer wants to be regrouped as soon as they tell you that one member wants one plan and another member wants a different one.

**What you say.** The training's line was: let you know that I will have to regroup the household, I will have to group you separately from your spouse, is that okay. And if the consumer says no, that it is not okay, the answer is that you have to do it, because they cannot hold different plans inside one group.

**When to say nothing.** If the consumer is happy with the plan the household is already on, do not offer regrouping and do not mention it. The training was blunt about that: no need to even mention it.
