# New Applicant or Existing Consumer

> [!tip] How to use this lesson
> The source for this lesson is training session 2026-10-09 14-56-43. It is a worked call from beginning to end, so the order of the stages is the order of the call. Read them in sequence and the call takes care of itself.

Points marked with the star carry the training's own emphasis.

## What you will be able to do

- Decide whether a caller is a new applicant or an existing consumer, and say what makes the answer definite.
- Set up the record and the case in the right order, before the screen rather than after it.
- Run the first eligibility screen and say what its result does and does not decide.
- Read the eligibility result, find the specific reason, and explain it to the consumer.
- Write a case note that a reviewer can act on, and correct one that is already closed.

## Stage 1: The search decides who you are talking to

The first thing on the call is not a question, it is a search. You search before you decide anything, because the decision about what kind of call this is falls out of the search result.

The sequence the training walked through:

1. Search by name and date of birth.
2. If nothing comes back, ask the consumer for their full social security number, and search again.
3. Read the result, and then confirm it with the consumer.

★ The reasoning behind the second step is the part worth keeping: asking for the full social security number is what guarantees the consumer is a new applicant. It is not an identification ritual. You ask once, maybe twice, and you do not keep asking after that, because the consumer is already on the call and being asked to spell their number repeatedly is how a friendly call turns into a hostile one.

Once the search confirms it, you say it out loud and get a yes or no: are you comfortable with this being a new application. The training wanted that confirmation, not an assumption.

### The rule that catches people

Here is the rule the training stopped to make sure everybody had: if the search finds a consumer whose eligibility has already been determined, you do not run a quick sort.

There are two versions of this and both matter.

If the search returns an existing consumer with active eligibility, no quick sort. There is nothing to screen for, because eligibility has already been decided.

If the search returns an existing consumer whose eligibility was determined and came back ineligible, there is still no quick sort. This is the counterexample the training used, and it is the one people get wrong. In the recorded call the consumer was ineligible at that moment and was about to get married, which is a qualifying life event, so a quick sort would have been wrong on the facts. An ineligible result is not a new applicant. New-applicant status comes from the search and the confirmation, never from what the eligibility determination said.

## Stage 2: Confirm the record and create the case early

Once the consumer is confirmed as new, the setup happens before the screen, not after.

You create the contact in Salesforce, with the consumer record type, and you fill in what you have: the name, the date of birth. Then you create the case. The subject for a new application is new application, and the description carries the detail.

★ The training was explicit about why this is early. On a call with a quick sort in it, the case has to be created right away, because the screen runs from the case. You cannot do what you normally do and save the case for the end of the call. The training put it as being forced to create the case early rather than waiting until the end of the call as usual.

The reason the consumer does not notice is the hold. You say something like asking whether the consumer minds a brief hold for a minute, and you use that minute to create the contact and the case without narrating your way through a screen of navigation. The training gave the reason plainly: a consumer talking while you are working out the navigation is pressure you do not need, and it is the kind of pressure that makes new agents slow down.

The case fields you will see, and what to put in them:

- Subject: new application.
- Description: the detail of the call.
- Status: already closed automatically, so there is nothing to set.
- Resolution reason: completed request most of the time, and call disconnected if the call gets cut off.

One artefact of the training environment will confuse you if you meet it. The CalHEERS case link can show as none, because the training environment does not carry the connection. In a live environment the CalHEERS case appears on the case.

## Stage 3: The first eligibility screen

The screen for a confirmed new applicant is the quick sort, run inside CalHEERS, and the training was clear that this is where the call starts for this kind of consumer.

The lead-in matters as much as the questions. The training's wording was that the application determines eligibility for Medi-Cal and Covered California, and that the questions are being asked to get an idea of what the consumer might qualify for. Get an idea of what you might qualify for. That is the level of certainty you are entitled to at this point in the call, and it is the same level of certainty you are entitled to when you read the result back to the consumer later.

Two things about the way the questions are asked:

- Say household, not family members. The training was deliberate about this. Family members is a phrase consumers read as a question about their relationships, while household is the filing term, because what the screen is doing is asking about household size.
- The questions are about the household, not about the consumer. How many people are in the household, how many children under 19, is anyone pregnant, is anyone 65 or older, is anyone disabled, and what the annual income is.

The result of the screen is a routing decision, not an eligibility decision. It says whether this consumer most likely belongs with the county or with Covered California, and it never says that anyone is eligible. If the result points to the county, what you say is that based on those answers the consumer will most likely be eligible for Covered California, and then you ask whether they are ready to start an application today.

## Stage 4: Reading the result and giving the reason

The screen gets the consumer into the application. The eligibility result is what tells you whether they came out of it covered.

The result lives in the account. Go to eligibility results under manage your application, and it lists every household member and what each of them is eligible for. The headline status can read not eligible right away, and the detail is behind see full details.

★ The point of the full details is that the headline is not the reason. The screen tells you what happened, and the detail tells you why, and the consumer needs the why. In the recorded call the detail gave two separate reasons: the consumer was ineligible for Medi-Cal because they were about the income limit, and they were ineligible for Covered California because based on the information provided they did not meet the requirements to enrol in a plan outside of open enrolment.

That second reason carries its own instruction. The consumer can reapply if their situation changes, or during open enrolment. A qualifying life event is the thing that changes the situation, so in practice the advice you give is that the consumer can come back during open enrolment or when they have a valid qualifying life event.

Give that advice in those words, because it is the advice that matches the reason on the screen. Telling a consumer they are ineligible and stopping there leaves them with nothing to do.

## Stage 5: What the note has to say

★ The training asked the room for the two things that absolutely have to be documented, and the answer was the CalHEERS case number and the member's name. The recording is partly garbled at that point, so treat the wording as a paraphrase of what was said: the CalHEERS case number, and the name of the member the call was about.

Beyond those two, the shape of a good note is that it describes what happened rather than reproducing everything that was typed.

The training's worked note, written on the call, ran something like this:

- The consumer called to begin a new application.
- The search returned no results, and a quick sort was performed and returned no transfer.
- The application continued in CalHEERS.
- Eligibility was determined for a household of one, with an income of sixty thousand dollars.
- The consumer was deemed ineligible due to a lack of qualifying life event, and was advised that they can return during open enrolment or when they have a valid qualifying life event.

★ The instruction behind the note is the one people fight hardest with. The training said there is no need to spill everything else about the application, because you would be typing for days. Put in what is important, and leave the rest.

### The acronym rule

The note above writes qualifying life event and then adds the letters, and uses the letters from then on. The training asked the room to notice that the acronym appears at least once, spelled out, and then can be used freely afterwards, because it is used for quality assurance purposes.

The rule has a limit. Defining an acronym once is good practice. Stripping every noun out of the note so that it reads as a list of initials is not. A note is read by someone who was not on the call, so it has to stay understandable on its own. Spell the term out the first time, then shorten it.

## Stage 6: Documenting in the time you have, and fixing it after

The timing of the note is as important as its content.

★ The training's habit is to use the hold time for the documentation rather than saving it all for afterwards. You put the consumer on a brief hold while you finish notating the account, and while you are there you get the reference number for the call. The consumer hears the reason, and it is a good one: it shows them the interaction is being documented, and if they ever have to call back, they have a reference number and possibly your name.

The training allowed up to four minutes on a hold, and said what to do if you are not finished at that point: go back and tell the consumer you are almost done notating the case and need a few more minutes. Then there are another three to four minutes after the call, and finishing most of the note before the after-call work means those minutes are yours rather than the next call's.

### When the case is already closed

You will get it wrong at some point. A typo, a missing detail, a mistake in what you wrote.

There is a window. A closed case can still be edited for about fifteen minutes, maybe twenty. Catch it inside that window and you can go back and fix it yourself. Miss the window and the case locks, and the fix is a post job: extra notes, or a reflection if you made an error, added on the case as a record of what happened.

So the practical order is: capture it while you can still edit it, and use the post job when you cannot.

### The boxes on the right

The case screen has checkboxes down the right-hand side for the required case review. The training was clear that these are a mental checklist for you rather than a form to be completed on every call, and that they were put there because people were worried about the required case review. The formal review is taught separately. So use the boxes to find the work you might have missed, and do not treat ticking them as proof that the review was done.

## Review

Close the lesson and write down, from memory:

1. The three steps that decide whether the caller is a new applicant, and why the social security number is asked for.
2. The rule that stops you running a quick sort, and the ineligible counterexample that makes the rule stick.
3. Why the contact and the case are created before the screen rather than after it.
4. The words you use for the screen's lead-in, and the level of certainty they commit you to.
5. Where the eligibility result lives, and what see full details adds to it.
6. The two reasons the recorded consumer was ineligible, and the advice each one produces.
7. The two things that absolutely have to be in the note, and what the instruction is about everything else.
8. The acronym rule, and the limit on it.
9. The window for editing a closed case, and what you do once it has locked.
10. What the checkboxes on the right of the case screen are for.

Then check the two things people miss: that an ineligible result is not a new applicant, and that a note which strips every noun out of it has obeyed the acronym rule and broken everything else.
