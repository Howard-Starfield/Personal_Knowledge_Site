# The Final Assessment: How It Works, and Twenty-Five Questions with Their Answers

> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's Covered California training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation.

## What you will be able to do

- Answer each of the twenty-five review questions the way a representative would answer it on the phone.
- Name the document the answer lives in: the lesson that teaches it, the knowledge article the training uses, the official page, or the session and the minute.
- Keep apart the pairs of questions that look alike - the two authentication questions, and the two document-deadline questions - because the difference between them is the answer.
- Say where the official record and the training do not agree, instead of quietly picking a side.

## The assessment, and how to use this lesson

**What the training says the assessment is.** The review that precedes the final is twenty questions, and they are not multiple choice: you have to locate each answer using your approved resources. The final is longer - about fifty questions, all multiple choice, drawn from the review and from more besides - and it takes something between three quarters of an hour and an hour. The part that catches people is that the review and the final ask about the same material in different words, so memorising the review wording is not enough (session 2026-10-15 mi-term-exam, 00:03:04, 00:03:31, 00:04:04, 00:04:34, 00:04:36, 00:05:13). The standard the review sets is the one that carries over: **locate the answer, and say where you found it.**

Two halves, and the second is the one people drop. The first half is the answer. The second half is the document: which lesson, which knowledge article, which page on coveredca.com, and where the training is the only place it is written, which session and which minute. A representative who can answer from memory and cannot say where the answer lives has half the skill, because on a call the second half is what lets a colleague check the answer and lets a consumer be told where to read for themselves.

**What these questions are.** The recording of the review on 23 October is almost entirely silent - forty-five seconds of speech over a screen - so it is not the source of anything here. The twenty-five questions below are **this package's own revision set**, written against the lessons and the official record, and every one of them names where its answer lives. Where two records differ, the lesson says so rather than choosing for you.

Four things to hold on to while you work through the stages:

- **Two of the questions are not the same question.** Question 1 asks what pieces of information exist to authenticate with; question 12 asks what you ask when the caller has already given the name. Question 6 asks for the base period; question 7 asks where you extend the due date on the ninetieth day.
- **Some answers are the training's own rather than published policy.** The reasonable opportunity period is the clearest case: the training gives a number of days, and the official record publishes no number at all.
- **One question here offers an "all of the above" option.** It is reproduced below as this question puts it, and the lesson says which of the reasons qualify. The practice question in the bank is worded so that no option says it.
- **Some questions have no answer in the package.** Where that is the case, the lesson says so and tells you what you would say instead.

## Stage 1: Authenticating the caller, and finding the case

### Question 1 - the pieces of information you authenticate with

> Father called in this morning; wife is the primary. What pieces of information, after the first and last name, could you use to successfully authenticate the caller? List all options you have.

**The answer.** You authenticate the primary, not the caller, so everything you ask about is the primary's. There are six approved items you can use after the first and last name: the date of birth, the full mailing or physical address, the phone number, the final four digits of the social security number, the email address, and the names of every household member recorded on the case. Any three of them answered correctly, with the name, is the standard - four items in all. Date of birth, address and phone number are the ones the training prefers; an email address only counts if the case shows one; the household member names are the last resort.

Then, because the caller is the primary's husband and not the primary, a second check follows. You look at the Review Application page and then the Individual Information tab, where the primary's marital status and the household relationship both have to agree with what the caller says about himself. If the case reads single or never married, he is not the spouse and the call is handled at the lower level of rights.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 17, _Authenticating a Caller_ (`check\17-authenticating-a-caller\lesson.md`, stages 4 and 5); the training sessions 2026-10-08 09-01-46 and 2026-10-08 09-31-51.

> **The trap.** The list of six is the answer to question 1. It is not the whole of question 12, and answering the two with one list is the easiest way to get the question wrong.

### Question 12 - what you ask once the name is in hand

> Rainbow Scott stated her first and last name immediately after you greeted her. To fully authenticate her, what are the additional standard questions that you would ask to successfully authenticate her?

**The answer.** Three more answers, and you are looking for three correct ones rather than a confession. The name she has already given is one item, so you need three correct answers from the same six-item list, four in total. The order to work in is phone number, then mailing address, then email address, then the final four digits of the social security number, then the household member names; the date of birth sits inside the three and is one of the preferred ones.

Two habits matter as much as the list. If an answer comes back wrong you do not say so and you do not end the attempt - you carry on and ask the next item, and you correct the record once the caller is authenticated. And you check the answers in the Case Overview Hub, not on the application PDF, which may not show the latest information.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 17, _Authenticating a Caller_ (`check\17-authenticating-a-caller\lesson.md`, stage 4); the training sessions 2026-10-08 09-01-46 and 2026-10-08 09-31-51.

> **The trap.** "Three" is not the whole answer. Three is the number of correct items; the name is a fourth thing the caller has to produce, and the date of birth only counts once, inside the three.

### Question 3 - the two places you can run a quick sort from

> Donna called in today. You pull up Salesforce to begin the Quick Sort. After being unable to locate her in CalHEERS, what two areas can you click on to get to the Quick Sort Calculator?

**The answer.** Two routes, both in Salesforce: a CRM contact and a CRM case. From the contact you use the drop-down menu in the corner of the record and choose the quick sort; from the case you run it on the case itself. The training's preference was the case, because the case already carries the subject and the description, so there is less to write out twice.

A quick sort is required on every call about a new application for cover where the consumer has no CalHEERS case yet and nobody in the household is already on Medi-Cal. It is the one thing the service centre does that reaches out to the county, and it asks six questions: how many people are in the family, how many of them are children under 19, whether anyone is pregnant, whether anyone is 65 or more, whether anyone is disabled, and the annual income.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 14, _Salesforce Case Documentation and Escalation_ (`check\14-salesforce-case-documentation-and-escalation\lesson.md`, stage 4), and lesson 18, _Outbound Calls and the Quick Sort_ (`check\18-outbound-calls-and-quick-sort\lesson.md`); the training session 2026-10-07 12-31-54, 00:32:55-00:33:13.

## Stage 2: Households, and the changes that go with them

### Question 2 - a change reported on a household split across two programmes

> Adora called in today to report a change. She has twin girls (Angel & Amaze) who are on Medi-Cal. How would you assist her?

**The answer.** First the search, because she is an existing consumer: find her case, confirm it with her, and do not run a quick sort or start a new application. Then look at the eligibility results summary on the account homepage, which shows a result for each person in the household. A Medi-Cal result against any member, alongside Covered California members, is what the official record calls a mixed-program family - the training's own phrase for the same thing was a mixed household, so use the official term when you are speaking to a consumer.

On a mixed-program case the one thing you may not do is process a reported change. So you do not make the change yourself. You refer Adora to her county's Medi-Cal office and give her the public phone number for that county's Medi-Cal office; if she makes the change in her own account, she should ring the county after five days to check that it arrived. The training's warning is the reason for the care it takes: a representative making changes that affect Medi-Cal recipients can damage a case.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 22, _New Applicant versus Existing_ (`check\22-new-applicant-versus-existing\lesson.md`) and lesson 36, _Mixed Households: Two Programmes, One Family_ (`check\36-mixed-households\lesson.md`, steps 6 and 7); the coveredca.com page _Medi-Cal for Mixed Program Families_ (`mixed-program-families.md`); the training session 2026-10-15 08-22-38.

### Question 16 - a divorce, a court order and a child still on the tax return

> Macy is calling you. Her divorce is final, and her ex-husband is required to provide health and dental coverage for their child. She is still claiming the child on her taxes; however, the child no longer needs coverage through her account. What would you do?

**The answer.** Start from the tax return, not from the divorce. The household is the tax filer, the spouse, and everyone claimed as a tax dependent - and a dependent counts even when they are not applying for cover, with their income still coming into the household. Because Macy still claims the child, the child is still in her household and still on her application. The court order changes who pays for the child's care; it does not move the child out of her tax household.

So you do not remove the child from the household. What you work is the change that ends the child's cover on her account, and the two events on the record are qualifying life changes that carry a 60-day window: a divorce, separation or dissolution, and a court order requiring cover for a child. If she stops claiming the child on her tax return, that is the change that moves the household, and it has to be reported like any other change of circumstances.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 36, _Mixed Households: Two Programmes, One Family_ (`check\36-mixed-households\lesson.md`, step 2); the coveredca.com pages _Information for Immigrants_ (`immigrants.md`) and _Managing Your Account_ (`account.md`); the training session 2026-10-15 08-22-38.

### Question 17 - a twenty-sixth birthday in the middle of the year

> Jason called in today because he wants to make sure he is covered for the rest of the year. Today is his birthday, and he turned 26. How would you educate him concerning his coverage for the remainder of the year?

**The answer.** His cover does not stop on his birthday - not on the day and not in that month. A dependent whose twenty-sixth birthday falls during the year stays on the plan to the end of that year; the training's worked example was a twenty-sixth birthday on 9 October 2026, with the cover running to 31 December 2026 and a new arrangement needed from 1 January 2027.

So the education is: he is covered for the rest of this year, and from the first of January he needs his own arrangement - his own application in the open enrolment period, or a qualifying life event. The official record lists the loss of cover under a parent's plan as a qualifying life change, and a birthday is not one of them, so the two halves of the answer are the ones people lose: the cover does not end today, and it does not run for ever.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 20, _Special Eligibility Groups_ (`check\20-special-eligibility-groups\lesson.md`, step 1) and lesson 51, _American Indian and Foster Youth Coverage_ (`check\51-american-indian-and-foster-youth-coverage\lesson.md`); the coveredca.com page _Managing Your Account_ (`account.md`); the training session 2026-10-09 10-46-31.

## Stage 3: Documents, deadlines and the drug list

### Question 6 - how long the consumer has to send the documents

> How many days do consumers have to submit their documents when they are requested? (ROP - Reasonable Opportunity Period)

**The answer.** Ninety-five days for most documents. The training's other figure is 155 days for proof of income, so the answer depends on the document: 95 days for most of them, 155 for income.

Two things to say alongside the number, because they change how much it is worth. First, these are the training's own figures and not published policy - the official record publishes no number of days at all for the period, so read the deadline off the case rather than from memory. Second, the two training sessions do not agree: on 15 October the period was given as 95 days for everything with no carve-out for income, and the split is the later statement and the one to carry. On one pass the speaker said 145 before settling on 155 in the same breath; the figure to carry is 155.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 53, _The Reasonable Opportunity Period_ (`check\53-the-reasonable-opportunity-period\lesson.md`, stage 2); the knowledge article the training names, ROP - Reasonable Opportunity Period; the training session 2026-10-22 07-56-48, 01:36:12-01:36:31, and session 2026-10-15 10-56-52, 00:52:43-00:53:10.

> **The trap.** The official record publishes no day count for this period at all. If a question asks how many days, the honest answer carries the training's number and says whose number it is.

### Question 7 - extending the due date on the ninetieth day

> Jaila called on the 90th day to say she is unable to submit her requested proof of income and needs more time. Where would you extend the due date?

**The answer.** Through manual verifications. You go to manual verifications and you give her 60 days. That is the route, and it is the answer to this question - not a longer base period, and not a second letter.

The extension is a judgement call and not an entitlement. The training's guidance is to offer one when the consumer asks for one or when the deadline is close, and not to assume the consumer has had the letter in front of them. Two things close the door: once the item shows as overdue the route is shut, and an extension that is worked is recorded for every applicable person, one at a time. On the ninetieth day she is still inside the window, so the extension is available to be offered - offered, not owed.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 53, _The Reasonable Opportunity Period_ (`check\53-the-reasonable-opportunity-period\lesson.md`, stage 2); the knowledge article ROP - Reasonable Opportunity Period; the training session 2026-10-22 07-56-48, 01:38:42-01:38:52, 01:41:09-01:41:26 and 01:41:35-01:42:04.

### Question 8 - a child's dental cover

> Hi, I am calling to confirm up to what age my child's pediatric dental coverage lasts.

**The answer.** Under 19. Every Covered California health plan includes dental insurance for children under 19, so you confirm that the child is covered for dental until their nineteenth birthday and the family pays nothing extra for it. If the person asking is an adult, the answer changes: dental for adults is a separate family dental plan bought from the insurer, and it is not part of the health plan.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 28, _Vision, Dental and Other Coverage_ (`check\28-vision-dental-and-other-coverage\lesson.md`); the coveredca.com page _Dental Insurance_ (`dental.md`); the training session 2026-10-13 10-42-49.

### Question 22 - what a formulary is

> Define what a formulary is.

**The answer.** A formulary is a health plan's list of the prescription drugs it covers - the glossary calls it a drug list. To find out whether a prescription is covered at all, the consumer asks their health insurance company for their drug list; extra formulary information is available through the shopping tool. The training's reading was that the service centre cannot pull a formulary up itself, though it hedged that it would need to go back and check, so it is not something to promise on a call.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 28, _Vision, Dental and Other Coverage_ (`check\28-vision-dental-and-other-coverage\lesson.md`, stage 6); the coveredca.com page _Glossary_ (`glossary.md`); the training session 2026-10-13 10-42-49, 00:38:16-00:38:41.

## Stage 4: Money: what makes one premium different from another

### Question 21 - the two kinds of financial help

> Name two types of financial help that Covered California offers consumers.

**The answer.** Two, and a third that is easy to leave out. **Advance premium tax credits** lower the monthly bill, and they are available across the coverage levels for households between 100% and 400% of the federal poverty level. **Cost-sharing reductions** lower what a member pays for care - the deductible, the copays and the coinsurance - and they are available at 250% of the poverty level or less, alongside an Enhanced Silver plan. The third is the **California Premium Subsidy**, the state's own contribution.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 1, _What the ACA Is and What It Created_ (`check\01-what-the-aca-is-and-what-it-created\lesson.md`) and lesson 31, _Explaining Plan Benefits_ (`check\31-explaining-plan-benefits\lesson.md`); the coveredca.com page _Financial Help_ (`financial-help.md`); the training sessions 2026-10-06 08-19-24 and 2026-10-14 08-03-23.

### Question 23 - when the out-of-pocket costs stop

> When do out-of-pocket costs stop for a consumer's medical care during the benefit year?

**The answer.** When the consumer reaches the plan's out-of-pocket maximum. That is the ceiling on what they pay for covered services in one plan year, and once it is met the carrier pays the covered costs in full for the rest of the benefit year. It is not the end of the year, and it is not a fixed number for everybody: each plan sets its own.

Two things it does not reach. The ceiling does not apply to premiums, and it does not apply to services the plan does not cover - and on a plan where out-of-network care is allowed there are usually separate limits for out-of-network services and providers, which the consumer's own insurer is the place to get. Everything the consumer pays out of pocket counts toward both the deductible and the ceiling, with the monthly premium the one exception.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 2, _The Four Metal Tiers_ (`check\02-the-four-metal-tiers\lesson.md`) and lesson 31, _Explaining Plan Benefits_ (`check\31-explaining-plan-benefits\lesson.md`, steps 1 and 5); the coveredca.com page _Costs and Financial Help_ (`costs.md`); the training session 2026-10-14 08-03-23.

### Question 24 - the three things insurers price on

> Insurance companies use which three pieces of information to determine premiums?

**The answer.** Age, family composition, and the geographic pricing region. The rate climbs with age and the climb stops at 64. California is cut into 19 pricing regions, and the rate follows what health care costs inside the region, so two consumers of the same age pay different amounts when they live in different cities. Family composition moves the rate by how many children under 21 the household has, and a plan may charge for no more than three of them however many there are.

**The wrinkle, and it is not resolved.** The training's own knowledge check asked which criteria set rates and offered five candidates, with smoking habits and family history among them; the answer it gave was family size and geographic location, and the other two were ruled out. The filed rate data does not line up with that. The federal rate file breaks the approved monthly premium down by plan, then by rating area, by age, by tobacco and by tier, across 19 rating areas, and the individual rate in it is the filed monthly premium for that age, tier and rating area. So tobacco is priced in the filed rates while the training's criteria leave it out.

Give the training's three when the question is the training's own knowledge check, and let the knowledge article settle the difference. Do not tell a consumer that smoking never affects the price, and do not present the training's answer as the filed method.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 13, _The Shopping Tool and Plan Types_ (`check\13-the-shopping-tool-and-plan-types\lesson.md`, stage 7, What makes one premium different from another), which teaches this in full; the training session 2026-10-13 10-42-49, 00:40:41-00:41:30, 00:43:26-00:43:44 and 00:56:05-00:57:08; the coveredca.com pages _Glossary_ (`glossary.md`) and _CMS Marketplace Public Use Files_ (`cms-marketplace-public-use-files.md`).

### Question 25 - a reason there is no penalty

> What would be a reason that a consumer would not face a penalty for not having coverage throughout the year?

**The answer.** An exemption. The official record publishes three: a general hardship exemption, an affordability hardship exemption, and a religious conscience exemption. The training named three routes as well - the consumer cannot afford cover, religious conscience, and an appeal - and you tell the consumer to use the route that fits and to appeal if the answer comes back wrong.

Two more answers to have ready, because they are the ones consumers ask about. The penalty is worked out month by month, so a consumer who had qualifying cover for part of the year pays only for the months they did not have it. And a member of a federally recognised tribe does not pay the individual penalty at all.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 4, _Covered California and the Individual Mandate_ (`check\04-covered-california-and-the-individual-mandate\lesson.md`) and lesson 12, _Traps and Edge Cases_ (`check\12-traps-and-edge-cases\lesson.md`); the coveredca.com page _Penalty Details and Exemptions_ (`tax-penalty-details-and-exemptions.md`); the training session 2026-10-06 08-19-24.

## Stage 5: Coverage, plans and the law

### Question 5 - the three counties and the children's programme

> San Francisco, San Mateo, and Santa Clara Counties offer what program to children?

**The answer.** CCHIP - the County Children's Health Insurance Program. In those three counties, and in no others, a child may be placed on that scheme instead of Medi-Cal, and it follows rules that are close to Medi-Cal's. It is a county scheme, not a Covered California plan, so the consumer's route in is their county's C-CHIP office.

The conditions the training listed are worth having ready: the child lives in one of those three counties, is from birth up to the nineteenth birthday, the household income sits above 266% of the federal poverty level and no more than 322% of it, the child is a citizen, a national or lawfully present, and the child has a social security number unless it is still pending. Eligibility lasts 12 months.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 52, _C-CHIP and MCAP_ (`check\52-c-chip-and-mcap\lesson.md`); the coveredca.com pages _Medi-Cal for Children_ (`children.md`) and _Medi-Cal for Mixed Program Families_ (`mixed-program-families.md`); the training session 2026-10-21 11-48-01, 00:00:33-00:01:55.

### Question 9 - a tribal member below 300% of the poverty level

> Shelly qualifies as a federally recognized AI/AN and has income below 300% FPL. How would you educate her based on the FPL chart? What AI/AN plan would she be eligible for?

**The answer.** Between 100% and 300% of the federal poverty level, a member of a federally recognised tribe can take a **zero cost-sharing plan**: no deductibles, no copayments and no coinsurance for the covered services. Below that band the route is Medi-Cal, where the income qualifies. The training puts the limited cost-sharing plan above 300% of the poverty line, and the official record publishes it as available at any household income: it removes the cost of care received through an Indian health care provider, or on a referral from one, and it can be taken at any metal tier and even on an unsubsidised application.

The conditions on the zero cost-sharing plan are the ones to say out loud: she is not eligible for full-scope Medi-Cal, does not have free Medicare, and does not have affordable minimum-value employer cover. There is one zero cost-sharing plan per carrier and product, so there is no metal tier to choose and no comparison to make. Two further points for a consumer: a member of a federally recognised tribe does not pay the individual penalty, and she may enrol or change plan at any time rather than waiting for the annual window.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 51, _American Indian and Foster Youth Coverage_ (`check\51-american-indian-and-foster-youth-coverage\lesson.md`) and lesson 20, _Special Eligibility Groups_ (`check\20-special-eligibility-groups\lesson.md`, step 2); the coveredca.com page _Coverage for American Indians and Alaska Natives_ (`american-indians-alaska-natives.md`); the training sessions 2026-10-21 10-40-29 and 2026-10-09 10-46-31.

> **The trap.** The training did not explain the bronze and silver metal tiers in this session, and said so. Do not describe the plan she qualifies for by its tier.

### Question 18 - the act

> There is an act that offers subsidies such as APTC, provides coverage for people with pre-existing conditions, and expanded the Medicaid program. What is the name of the act?

**The answer.** The Affordable Care Act - the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act - signed into law in March 2010. It is the law that created the marketplaces and the subsidies that become advance premium tax credits, that guarantees cover for people with pre-existing conditions, and that expanded the Medicaid program to reach further down the income scale.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 1, _What the ACA Is and What It Created_ (`check\01-what-the-aca-is-and-what-it-created\lesson.md`); the coveredca.com page _Glossary_ (`glossary.md`); the training session 2026-10-06 08-19-24.

### Question 20 - seeing a specialist without a referral

> What health plan types allow you to see an in-network specialist without a referral from a PCP?

**The answer.** The PPO and the EPO. A PPO lets a member use doctors inside the network or outside it, and a referral is not required to see a specialist. An EPO covers in-network care only, apart from emergencies, and it also needs no referral. The HMO is the one that needs it: it covers in-network care only, and a referral from the primary care doctor is needed to see a specialist. The part that separates an EPO from an HMO is the referral, not the network.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 13, _The Shopping Tool and Plan Types_ (`check\13-the-shopping-tool-and-plan-types\lesson.md`, stage 8); the coveredca.com page _Health Plan Coverage Levels_ (`coverage-levels.md`); the training session 2026-10-06 10-24-58.

## Stage 6: The service centre's own procedures

### Question 4 - what a consumer has after a termination for non-payment

> What options does the caller have if they were terminated for non-payment?

**The answer.** Reinstatement, and the carrier is the one who decides it. The steps are the same every time: confirm that the carrier terminated the consumer for non-payment, tell the consumer to ring their carrier and pay the past-due premiums to them, and tell them the carrier will then contact Covered California to get them reinstated. The carrier can refuse, and a consumer who has been terminated for non-payment may not be able to get cover back until the open enrolment period or a qualifying life event.

Two things a representative should not do. You do not reinstate it yourself, and where the carrier is already on the call with authority to approve a reinstatement, that call is escalated rather than taken by the service centre. And where the consumer is also disputing the tax form, the dispute does not restore cover: where the carrier reinstates, it gives 30 days to pay the balance and the reviewer then approves the dispute and a corrected form is issued; where the carrier refuses, the consumer uses the form as it stands and settles the financial help at tax time.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 46, _Escalation Handling_ (`check\46-escalation-handling\lesson.md`, step 8), lesson 29, _Renewal and Payments_ (`check\29-renewal-and-payments\lesson.md`) and lesson 44, _Disputing a Tax Form_ (`check\44-disputing-a-tax-form\lesson.md`, step 5); the coveredca.com page _Managing Your Account_ (`account.md`); the training sessions 2026-10-20 08-06-17 and 2026-10-19 10-56-08.

### Question 10 - an abusive or insulting caller

> How do we handle abusive or insulting callers here at CCA?

**The answer, and the honest limit of it.** The package does not carry a script for this one. The training session that raised it said the section on abusive and threatening callers would come later, and it did not come, so the closest material is the conduct training and the conduct code the official record publishes.

What the conduct training gives you: empathy is the floor, so you stay calm and you do not take it personally; you keep the call going rather than hanging up on a caller who is angry; you do not match the caller's tone; and you document what happened. Where there is a threat rather than abuse, that is a different matter and the supervisor's rule applies.

What the official record gives you, and it cuts the other way as well: neither staff nor agents may engage in abusive, intimidating, threatening or detrimental behaviour toward a consumer. So the answer is not to absorb abuse in silence, and it is not to answer abuse with abuse. It is de-escalation, and the conduct code is the document to point at.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 50, _Service Centre Conduct_ (`check\50-service-center-conduct\lesson.md`, steps 3, 4 and 6); the coveredca.com page _CCSB Agent Code of Conduct_ (`ccsb-code-of-conduct.md`); the training session 2026-10-21 08-06-22.

### Question 11 - an escalation that has passed the initial contact period

> If an escalation has passed the initial contact period, what steps would you take to further assist the consumer?

**The answer.** First the working out. That is an overdue escalation: a Salesforce case that has passed the initial contact period set in the Escalation and Consultation - Timeframe article, and where the escalation team has made no contact. If they have made initial contact by email or phone, it is not overdue, and a consumer who has been called is being handled.

Then the two documents. For more information you follow the Escalation and Consultation Task Guide. And if the case has turned into a question of access to care, you follow the Overdue Escalation to ER section of the Escalation and Consultation - Steps knowledge article. Those are the two places the answer is written down, and they are the places to send a colleague who asks.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 46, _Escalation Handling_ (`check\46-escalation-handling\lesson.md`, step 6); the training session 2026-10-20 08-06-17, 00:29:31-00:30:23.

### Question 13 - what to tell the consumer after an escalation is submitted

> After an escalation is submitted, how would you educate the consumer about the follow-up call? (Hint: Include the timeframe for receiving a callback.)

**The answer.** Once the escalation has been transferred to the escalation queue, that team contacts the consumer by email or by phone. The initial contact is one to ten business days from the date the case was transferred to the queue, and the resolution is one to thirty business days from the day the first contact is made. The consumer is contacted again when more information is needed and when the escalation has been closed.

The words to use are therefore plain: someone from the escalation team will be in touch within ten business days, and the whole thing is normally resolved within thirty business days of that first contact. And the one thing you do not say is that you will ring them yourself - once the escalation is valid the hand-over has happened, the case is theirs, chasing the consumer is no longer your duty, and you keep your own case open rather than closing it.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 46, _Escalation Handling_ (`check\46-escalation-handling\lesson.md`, step 6); the knowledge articles the training names, Escalation and Consultation - Timeframe, Escalation and Consultation Task Guide and Escalation and Consultation - Steps; the training session 2026-10-20 08-06-17, 00:28:00-00:30:23.

### Question 14 - who can remove a soft pause or a carry forward

> Who can remove a Soft Pause or Carry Forward status?

**The answer.** A county eligibility worker. A case in soft pause or in carry-forward is on the list of things a representative may not handle, because the rule behind that list is that you can pass a document but you cannot decide the eligibility again: passing the document is the first step, the redetermination is the second, and the second belongs to the county. The Department of Health Care Services can remove a negative action, and only that one. A representative at the service centre can remove neither.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 23, _Case Review Statuses_ (`check\23-case-review-statuses\lesson.md`, stages 2, 3 and 4) and lesson 36, _Mixed Households_ (`check\36-mixed-households\lesson.md`, step 6); the training sessions 2026-10-12 08-36-54 and 2026-10-15 08-22-38.

### Question 15 - a negative action on the account

> Sunshine has a Negative Action on her account. Will this affect her CCA plan?

**The answer.** No. A negative action is the county stopping or denying Medi-Cal, and it has no bearing on whether she can be in a subsidised Covered California plan if she is otherwise qualified. It is not a Covered California function at all, and it is applied to individual enrollees rather than to the whole household, so one person's flag does not touch anybody else's.

What it does change is the money. If the consumer does not take up Medi-Cal, the financial assistance stops, so she pays the full premium and the full cost sharing from the month the assistance ends - the training's example had the county place the flag in September, so the assistance stopped from 1 October and she paid full price for October. Going forward you can work with her to get the financial help restored from the first of the following month; for the month that has already passed, she has to go to the county. The training's phrase for the flag is a temporary hold, and that is the wording to reach for.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 23, _Case Review Statuses_ (`check\23-case-review-statuses\lesson.md`, stage 4); the training sessions 2026-10-12 08-36-54 and 2026-10-12 14-13-17.

### Question 19 - the reasons that belong to the escalation queue

> Which of the following reasons might you reach out to the Escalation queue for? (Escalation & Consultation Resource)

The options as this question puts them: Fraud; Request for Supervisor; Reporting a death and updating the primary person on the account; Questions about making changes in CalHEERS; All of the above.

**The answer.** One of those reasons belongs to the escalation queue, and it is the third: a report that the consumer has died. A death is one of the two grounds on which an end date is escalated - the other is a provable error at our end that the consumer can prove - and the update to the case that follows the death rides on the same escalation. The service centre does not change an end date itself, on any ground.

The other three do not belong there, and it is worth knowing why each one does not, because a representative who reaches for the wrong one burns the one thing the process depends on, which is the lead line coming first.

- **Fraud.** Fraud is worked with the fraud team, which the escalation team supports on escalated requests; it is not a reason to call the queue.
- **Request for a supervisor.** That is a warm transfer, and a warm transfer is how a caller is handed to the next person, not an escalation. The lead line comes first on every escalation, whatever the circumstances, and the lead approves or rejects it.
- **Questions about making changes in CalHEERS.** A question is a question. Where the change is a Medi-Cal one, the county owns it and the consumer is referred there; a representative may not process a reported change on a mixed-program case.

**Where it lives.** Lesson 46, _Escalation Handling_ (`check\46-escalation-handling\lesson.md`, steps 1, 2 and 7); the knowledge article the training names, Escalation & Consultation Resource, which the training calls Escalation and Consultation - Timeframe, Escalation and Consultation Task Guide and Escalation and Consultation - Steps; the training session 2026-10-20 08-06-17, 00:22:19-00:23:14 and 00:29:31-00:30:23.

> **The trap.** The question's own last option is "All of the above", and it is wrong. The practice question in the bank is worded so that no option says it.
