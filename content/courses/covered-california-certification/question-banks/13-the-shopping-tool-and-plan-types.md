    prompt: "Explain what eligibility for coverage means and who makes the decision, and say which facts about a household it turns on."
    sampleAnswer: "Eligibility is not something a representative decides and passes on. It is worked out when the application is processed, from facts about the household: the household itself and how many people are in it, income, anybody the consumer claims as a tax dependent, where they live, which for this marketplace means being a California resident, citizenship or immigration status, and whether they already have health insurance. The official eligibility page lists income, place of residence, citizenship or immigration status and current health insurance among the factors, and adds age and disability as other factors. One application screens the household for Covered California and for Medi-Cal at the same time and returns a result for each person, so different people in one household can qualify for different programmes - the official name for that is a mixed-program family. Only the people applying have to share Social Security numbers or immigration information; the rest of the household can still be listed."
    explanation: "A correct answer says eligibility is determined by the application rather than by a representative, gives at least four of the factors, and keeps the two programmes apart in a way that shows one application can return different results for different people. An answer that lists only income has missed most of the factors, and an answer that treats the result as the representative's judgement has the wrong decision-maker.     prompt: "Define the household the shopping tool and the application use, and explain what happens to a claimed tax dependent who lives with another parent and is insured through that parent."
    sampleAnswer: "The household is the tax household, not the people sharing an address: you, your spouse or domestic partner, and anybody you claim as a tax dependency. A claimed dependent counts even when they do not live with you and even when they are not applying for coverage, so a child living with their other parent and insured through that parent is still in the consumer's household. The person who claims the dependent is the head of the household and the primary on the application; if the other parent claims the child instead, the consumer is a household of one. Where parents take turns claiming a child from year to year, each of them updates their own application each year to add or remove that dependent, through Covered California."
    explanation: "A complete answer ties the household to the tax return, gives the two exceptions that catch people out - the dependent need not live with the consumer and need not be applying - and says who is the primary applicant. An answer that defines the household as everyone in the home has missed the tax rule, and an answer that leaves out the primary applicant has missed the second half. Lesson section: Stage 3: The tax household"

    prompt: "Say what the shopping comparison tool produces, what produces the actual eligibility results instead, and why a consumer can see different figures at the two stages."
    sampleAnswer: "The tool produces an estimate. When it is opened it asks for the coverage year, the ZIP code, the household's income, the household's size and the ages of its members, and which of them need coverage, and it then shows a warning that this is not an application for coverage but a tool that gives an estimate. The application is what returns the actual eligibility results, so a consumer can see a plan priced at nothing in the tool and see different numbers once the application is processed, because the application's figures are the ones that count. The official site calls the tool Shop and Compare, describes it as the fastest way to see what you might pay, and notes that it can also check whether the household would qualify for Medi-Cal instead."
    explanation: "A good answer names the estimate and the application as two different things, gives the inputs the tool collects, and explains why the two sets of numbers can differ. An answer that says the tool decides eligibility has dropped the warning entirely, and an answer that lists the inputs without saying what the output is has not answered the question. Lesson section: Stage 4: The shopping tool gives an estimate, not an application"
