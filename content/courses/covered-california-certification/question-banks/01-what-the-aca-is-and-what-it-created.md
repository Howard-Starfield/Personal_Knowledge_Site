    prompt: "A consumer tells you they have diabetes and no insurer will sell them a plan. Explain what the law now says about that, and what it says about a planholder who later becomes seriously ill."

Both of these protections were the point of the reform: before it, the people who most needed insurance were the people who struggled to get it."
    explanation: "Look for both halves — guaranteed issue on the way in, and no cancellation on the way through. An answer that gives only one of them is half right. Lesson section: Step 2: The access protections."

    prompt: "Explain the two separate rules about age and premiums, and why keeping them apart matters."
    sampleAnswer: "The first rule is that everyone of a given age pays the same premium, whatever their health history — nobody is charged more because of a pre-existing condition. The second rule is that premiums may vary by age, but a 64-year-old's premium may not exceed three times a 21-year-old adult's premium. The first rule controls what may vary within an age band, and the second controls how much the bands themselves may differ, so confusing them produces nonsense in either direction."
    explanation: "The key move is separating the rule about equal prices at the same age from the rule about age bands. The first is about health history, the second about how much the bands themselves may differ, so confusing them produces nonsense in either direction. Lesson section: Step 3: The cost rules."
