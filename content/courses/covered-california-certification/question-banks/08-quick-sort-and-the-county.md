    prompt: "What is a quick sort, who gets one, how many questions does it use, and what does the result decide?"
    sampleAnswer: "A quick sort is a short screening run for a brand-new applicant, meaning a consumer with no application on file and no eligibility determined yet. It uses six questions: how many people are in the family, how many children are under 19, whether anyone in the family is pregnant, whether anyone is 65 or older, whether anyone is disabled, and what the annual income is. The result is either a transfer, meaning the consumer should be transferred to the county, or no transfer, meaning a case has been created for this request. The result decides the routing of the call, and it never decides eligibility, because six answers are enough to say a consumer might qualify and no more."
    explanation: "Look for the six questions, the new-applicant condition and the two possible results. An answer that treats the screening as a decision about eligibility has over-read it, and an answer that gives no number for the questions has left the defining detail out. Lesson section: Step 1: What a quick sort is."
