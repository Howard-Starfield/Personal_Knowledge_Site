    prompt: "Name the three sections of the Consumer Relations and Resolution branch, say what each one is there to do, and explain why the priority support unit is not one of them."
    sampleAnswer: "The branch is split into three sections. The appeals section works and settles consumer appeals, trying an informal fix before anything heavier is needed. Escalations Resolution, abbreviated to ER, resolves the hardest eligibility and enrolment problems and works directly with carriers and counties. The Special Projects Unit runs the individual projects, including duplicate enrolment and financial discrepancies. The priority support unit is not one of the three: it is the queue in the middle of the escalation ladder that a service centre call reaches, whereas the special projects unit sits inside the branch. The two acronyms sound alike and the training session itself mixed them up at first."
    explanation: "Look for three named sections — appeals, escalations resolution and the special projects unit — and for the priority support unit being placed outside the branch as an escalation queue rather than inside it as a section. An answer that lists the priority support unit as one of the three has made exactly the confusion the training warned about. Lesson section: Step 2: Three sections, three different jobs."

    prompt: "Take an escalated case through the seven stages it passes inside Escalations Resolution, and say where the case sits while each stage is happening."
    sampleAnswer: "The process runs in seven steps. First comes intake of the escalated issue. Second, research on the consumer's problem begins. Third, the documentation is reviewed, both what is recorded in CalHEERS and what is recorded in the customer relationship management system, which is Salesforce. Fourth, the issue is analysed and the team decides which kind of resolution is needed. Fifth, that resolution is implemented. Sixth, the implementation is confirmed, so the file shows the change actually happened. Seventh, the consumer is informed and the case can be closed."
    explanation: "Look for all seven stages in order: intake, research, documentation review in CalHEERS and the customer relationship management system, analysis and the choice of resolution, implementation, confirmation, and closure after the consumer has been told. An answer that stops at implementation has missed the confirmation step, and an answer that merges research with the documentation review has lost one of the seven. Lesson section: Step 4: Escalations Resolution, the last rung."
  - id: "5f950616-b49c-4ecb-8159-ac44120dd3a0"
    revision: 1
    status: active
    type: free-recall
    prompt: "Describe what the Special Projects Unit does about duplicate enrolment: how the periodic cycle works, how the overlap is resolved for a consumer who answers, and what happens to one who does not."
    sampleAnswer: "The unit runs a periodic data matching cycle three times a year. Covered California posts a 30-day notice to consumers who hold subsidised duplicate enrolment, and after the 30 days the consumers who have not acted are identified and put into a separate workflow. For a consumer who answers, an overlapping enrolment review looks at the case variables that decide which plan is real: different addresses, claims utilisation, primary contact and overage dependence, and household size with projected income. The fix then takes one of three shapes — cancel the overlapping enrolment, end the first plan before the second one starts so cover is continuous, or retroactively remove a dependent still listed on a parent's case. For a consumer who cannot be reached or will not authenticate, the unit confirms the notices are on file and the 30 days have passed, then removes the financial assistance from one of the two cases so that only one stays subsidised, starting the following month."
    explanation: "Look for the three-times-a-year cycle and the 30-day notice, the three resolution shapes for a reachable consumer — cancel one, end one before the other starts, or remove the dependent retroactively — and the separate treatment of a consumer who cannot be reached, where the financial assistance comes off one case rather than the plan being cancelled. An answer that says the extra plan is cancelled for an unreachable consumer has mixed the two workflows together. Lesson section: Step 5: The Special Projects Unit and duplicate enrolment."
