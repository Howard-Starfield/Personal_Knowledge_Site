    prompt: "Which income documents do you ask a consumer for, and when is the attestation form the right answer?"
    sampleAnswer: "For employer wages the documents are a pay stub, the most recently filed Federal Income Tax Form 1040 with any appropriate schedules, a wage or income tax statement covering the W-2 and the 1099 family, and an employer statement when no other formal document is available. In most cases one document is enough. The attestation form, the Attestation of Income, No Documentation Available, is the last resort: it is for a consumer who cannot provide any of the others, it is accepted case by case, it is signed under a penalty of perjury, it is valid only for the benefit year it was asked for, and it has to be renewed every benefit year. An employer statement has its own conditions, being on company letterhead, signed by the employer and dated within 45 days of submission."
    explanation: "Look for the ordinary documents, the one-document rule, and the attestation form as the fallback for a consumer who has nothing else. An answer that offers the attestation form as the quickest route has inverted its purpose, and an answer that leaves out the benefit-year limit and the renewal has missed what makes the form conditional. Lesson section: Step 2: Which income documents to ask for."

    prompt: "Who has to upload the income documents, and how long has the consumer got?"
    sampleAnswer: "The consumer uploads them. The training was flat about it: a representative cannot upload on a consumer's behalf, and doing it is the consumer's own responsibility. What the representative can do is show the consumer where the account says something is still outstanding, and tell them the window. The training's figure was 30 days from the application date, and the training hedged the number when it first raised it, so it is best carried as the training's figure rather than as a fixed rule. The consumer can sign in and upload at any point, and the account tells them what is missing. The reason a case shows the income as not verified is that the system could not check the figure by itself."
    explanation: "Look for the consumer being the one who uploads, the representative's two jobs of showing where and saying how long, and the 30-day figure marked as the training's own and hedged. An answer that lets the representative upload the document during the call has swapped the responsibility, and an answer that quotes the 30 days as an unqualified rule has dropped the hedge the training put around it. Lesson section: Step 3: The consumer uploads, and the clock is running."

    prompt: "Set out the household questions the application asks, and say what the household's income figure then decides."
    sampleAnswer: "The screening asks how big the family is, how many of the children are under 19, whether anybody in the family is 65 or older, whether anybody in the family is disabled, and what the annual income is. The application then wants the household: a spouse or registered domestic partner and anyone else living in the home, children under 21 in the home including stepchildren, parents or stepparents in the home with a child under 21, anybody named on the family's income tax return, the rest of the household that files the tax return, and any family member living with you whom you claim as a dependent on somebody else's return. The filing status is asked too, along with whether anybody claims the consumer as a dependent. The figure those answers produce decides which programme the household qualifies for and how much financial help it gets, so a person who does not want coverage still goes on the application."
    explanation: "Look for the screening questions, the household the application asks for, and the income figure being the thing that decides the programme and the amount of financial help. An answer that leaves out the household entirely has lost the point of the question, and an answer that treats the household list as a list of people who must want coverage has missed why somebody who is not applying is still on the form. Lesson section: Step 4: The household questions the money depends on."
