    prompt: "A consumer on a Covered California plan with financial help reports an income change, and their case now shows a carry forward status. Explain what that status means, what happens to their coverage and their monthly premium while the county decides, and who is allowed to end the review."
    sampleAnswer: "Carry forward means the consumer holds a Covered California plan with financial help and has reported a change that could make them eligible for Medi-Cal, so the case is now pending a Medi-Cal determination and the county has to make the decision. While the county works, the consumer's coverage is uninterrupted: the plan continues, their monthly premium goes unchanged rather than up or down, they should keep paying it, and they can keep using their doctor and their prescriptions. A redetermination may take the county 45 days at most, and sometimes longer than that, so the representative tells the consumer to contact the county while their Covered California eligibility stays the same and unchanged. Only the county can lift the status. If the county finds the consumer eligible for Medi-Cal they have to take it, and if they refuse, or simply ignore the county, the financial assistance is discontinued and a negative action is put on the account."
    explanation: "A complete answer names the trigger for the status, states that both the coverage and the premium carry on unchanged, gives the 45 days the county may take, and says that the county alone ends the review. It also covers what happens if the consumer refuses Medi-Cal or ignores the county, because that is where the negative action comes from. An answer that stops at the county having to decide leaves the consumer with no idea what they are paying in the meantime. Explain what the soft pause is, who is allowed to lift it, what happens to the consumer's Medi-Cal coverage while the review runs, and what you should tell the consumer to do if they report another change during the month in which the soft pause came off."
    sampleAnswer: "A soft pause is what a Medi-Cal case gets when the consumer reports a change that might make them eligible for Covered California, for example an increase in income, or a change that means they no longer qualify for Medi-Cal or might qualify for another Medi-Cal programme. The flag stays on the account while the county works out the consumer's eligibility, and Medi-Cal coverage continues uninterrupted throughout. Only a county eligibility worker can lift it, so the consumer is sent to their county office for anything to do with their current Medi-Cal coverage, because Covered California cannot make that determination; a representative should verify the consumer's programme eligibility first. If another change is reported during the month in which the soft pause came off, the redetermination can put a soft pause back on the case, so the consumer should either report the change directly to the county or wait until the first of the following month and report it then."
    explanation: "A complete answer defines the soft pause as the mirror image of a carry forward, says the county alone lifts it, says Medi-Cal coverage continues while it runs, and gives the same-month advice with its reason. The reason is the part usually missing: a change reported during the month in which the pause came off can trigger another pause, so the choice is between reporting to the county and waiting for the first of the next month.     prompt: "A consumer's Medi-Cal has been stopped and their case carries a negative action. Explain what a negative action is, what usually causes one, what it does and does not affect, and what a representative is able to do about it."
    sampleAnswer: "A negative action is the status the county applies when somebody who was previously eligible is stopped from Medi-Cal, or when an application that is still pending is refused Medi-Cal; the flag means the consumer does not qualify for Medi-Cal, it is applied to individual enrollees rather than to the whole household, and only a county eligibility worker or the Department of Health Care Services can remove it. It is usually caused by non-compliance: the county mails a packet to the household asking them to fill it out and send it back, and if it is not returned the county places the flag, whether the household ignored it on purpose or by accident. A household that returns the packet and is found ineligible on the merits is not a negative action. The flag has no bearing on whether the consumer can enrol in a subsidised Covered California plan if they are otherwise eligible. A representative can help try to lift it, usually by reporting a change, which can restore the financial help from the first of the following month, while financial help for the months already passed has to be returned by the county."
    explanation: "A complete answer gives the definition, names non-compliance and the unreturned packet as the usual cause, draws the line against a denial on the merits, and states that the flag does not block a subsidised Covered California plan. The last part is what a consumer is most likely to hear as bad news, so an answer that leaves it out is the one that fails. Lesson section: Stage 4: Negative action is the county stopping or denying Medi-Cal"

    sampleAnswer: "The end date is worked out from the month in which the county reached its decision, not from the month the Medi-Cal was backdated to, because backdating the Medi-Cal effective date does not move the Covered California end date. The 14-day rule decides which month the system schedules automatically: if the county finalises in the first half of the month, the notice period still fits inside that month and the termination is scheduled for the last day of that same month, and if the county finalises closer to the month's end, the 14-day window falls in the following month and the termination is scheduled for the last day of that following month. In that second case the consumer is still entitled to an end date of the last day of the month in which the county decided, and they have to call and ask for it so that the date can be corrected."
    explanation: "A complete answer separates the month of the decision from the month of the backdating, gives both halves of the 14-day rule, and says the consumer may still ask for the last day of the month in which the county decided. The training's own worked example is a change reported on 12 October with the county deciding on 12 November and backdating Medi-Cal to 1 October, which gives a Covered California end date of 30 November.
