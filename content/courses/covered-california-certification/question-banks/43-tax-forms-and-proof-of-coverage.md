    prompt: "Which tax form does Covered California issue, which two related forms can you not get for a consumer, and where does each of those come from?"
    sampleAnswer: "Covered California issues IRS Form 1095-A, by January 31 each year, to people with individual-market Covered California cover. It lists the months a person carried insurance and the federal financial help they received during the year. A 1095-B comes out of the Small Business programme, out of Medicare, or out of Medi-Cal, and a 1095-C comes out of the employer. Neither of those two can be opened, reprinted or corrected by service centre staff, so a question about one goes to the body that issued it: the county, the carrier or the Small Business programme for a 1095-B, and the employer for a 1095-C. People covered by Medi-Cal, people on a minimum coverage plan, and people whose employer bought cover through Covered California for Small Business receive no 1095-A at all."
    explanation: "Look for the January 31 issue date, the three groups who get no 1095-A, and the rule that staff cannot open the 1095-B or the 1095-C. An answer that treats every enrollee as getting a 1095-A has missed the exclusions, and an answer that says staff can pull up a 1095-C has crossed the access boundary. Lesson section: Step 1: Which form belongs to which coverage."

    prompt: "What do the three columns on Form 1095-A report, and what does Column A minus Column C give you?"
    sampleAnswer: "Column A reports the whole price of the plan for the month before any assistance is subtracted, Column B reports the price of the second-cheapest silver plan on offer to the household, quoted whether or not anyone chose it, and Column C reports the assistance that was paid. Column A less Column C gives what the household actually paid that month. Column B is a comparison figure rather than the plan the consumer picked, so you can define it in a sentence, while the question of what it does in the tax calculation belongs to a tax professional."
    explanation: "Look for all three columns, the subtraction, and the note that Column B is a comparison amount rather than the plan that was bought. An answer that leaves Column B out, or that treats it as the consumer's own plan, has not read the form. Lesson section: Step 2: Reading the three columns of Form 1095-A."

The email is a signpost rather than the document itself. Staff must not email these documents to a consumer, because the training said that doing so can get an enroller terminated. The approved routes are the account itself, a CalHEERS reprint, or a manual reprint when the paper has to go somewhere the normal flow cannot reach."

Lesson section: Step 3: How the form reaches the consumer."

    prompt: "You need to get a consumer a paper copy of their 1095-A. Describe the two reprint routes, when each is used, how long each can take, and the two rules that keep a reprint from going wrong."
    sampleAnswer: "The CalHEERS reprint is the ordinary route: it covers a consumer who wants another copy, who wants paper although they prefer email contact, or who simply wants the document, and the usual wait is about ten business days. The manual Salesforce reprint is for paper that has to go somewhere the normal flow cannot reach, such as an out-of-state address or the tax professional's own address, and it can take up to sixty days, cannot be expedited, and is not moved along by a lead or a supervisor. The two rules are not to repeat a CalHEERS reprint inside fourteen calendar days, because that window is calendar days and not business days, and not to use the manual route when the ordinary one will do, because an unnecessary manual reprint puts the case on a list and can bring coaching or disciplinary action."
    explanation: "Look for both routes, the two waiting times, the calendar-day window, and the reason the manual route is not the default. An answer that quotes only one route, or that treats the fourteen-day window as business days, would leave a consumer waiting the wrong amount of time. Lesson section: Step 4: Choosing a reprint path."
