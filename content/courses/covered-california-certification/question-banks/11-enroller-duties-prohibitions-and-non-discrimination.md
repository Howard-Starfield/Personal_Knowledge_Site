    prompt: "List the duties an enroller performs."
    sampleAnswer: "An enroller gives consumers information about qualified health plans and about the financial assistance that may be available; walks them through the enrolment options available on coveredca.com; tells them what premiums cost and what health and dental coverage buys them, and answers their questions; gives them access to the tools and resources that let them compare cost, benefits and coverage options; helps consumers who may qualify enrol in Covered California health plans and in other programmes that make insurance affordable, including Medi-Cal; works in the coveredca.com web-based portal to help a consumer complete and submit their application; makes sure consumers understand the first payment they must make to enrol in a Covered California health plan; refers the consumer, where appropriate, to consumer assistance hotlines, the Ombuds Office, the Office of the Patient Advocate, the Department of Social Services, or the Covered California Service Center; and helps consumers with renewals during open enrolment periods, and with new special-enrolment applications, using the web-based enrolment programme."

    prompt: "Name the eight things an enroller is prohibited from doing, and explain why the three charging rules and the two steering rules are not the same rules as one another."
    sampleAnswer: "The prohibitions are: charging consumers a fee for enrolment assistance; failing to disclose a conflict of interest; steering a consumer into giving inaccurate information on the application; steering a consumer toward particular plans on the strength of the commission schedule alone; taking payment of the premium from the consumer, or paying any part of it, or any other consideration, to or on the consumer's behalf; inducing or accepting from the consumer any direct or indirect remuneration at all; intentionally putting more than one application in the system for a single household; and inviting, influencing or otherwise arranging for an individual to leave an employer-sponsored plan. They are not the same rules because they target different objects. The three charging rules cover the enroller's own fee, the premium money itself, and any other remuneration the consumer gives. The two steering rules cover the information entered on the application and the choice of plan. The duplicate-application rule is about creating more than one application for a household on purpose."
    explanation: "A correct answer gives all eight and keeps the three families apart: three charging rules, two steering rules, and the duplicate-application rule. The most common error is to merge the three charging rules into one, or to name only five or six of the eight. Lesson section: Stage 2: The prohibitions."

    prompt: "A consumer says their insurance agent is charging them a monthly fee for help, and that the agent's phone number is no longer in service. What do you do, and what can you honestly say about what happens next?"
    sampleAnswer: "Apologise on the agent's behalf and show the consumer empathy first. Then explain that they can file a complaint, and escalate it to the fraud department if they want to. Be honest that the service center does not control that agent and cannot act on the agent directly, but that it can route the consumer to the right door. The official record names two of those doors: the Covered California fraud line for fraud complaints, and the California Department of Insurance consumer hotline for agent or broker fraud."
    explanation: "A correct answer has the four parts in order — apologise, empathise, point to the complaint route, and be honest about the limit of what the service center can do. An answer that promises the agent will be disciplined, or that leaves out the complaint route, is wrong on one side or the other. Lesson section: Stage 3: When an agent gets it wrong."
