    prompt: "Who can bring a consumer's coverage to an end, and what does each of them decide?"
    sampleAnswer: "Four parties, each with its own decision. The consumer decides for themselves: they hold a legal right to bring their own health and dental cover to an end at any time and for any reason, and you process that without escalation. The county decides anything to do with Medi-Cal enrolment and with who is in the household, and the service centre has no access to end a Medi-Cal enrolment. The carrier decides the things the marketplace cannot decide for the policy, such as whether to approve a mid-month termination and whether to return part of the premium. The escalation queue decides the four things that cannot be handled in the call: a mid-month termination, a change to or removal of the primary, a retroactive end date, and a request to reinstate cover."
    explanation: "Look for all four parties with the decision attached to each, and for the point that the service centre cannot end a Medi-Cal enrolment. An answer that names only the consumer and the carrier has left out the two that decide for other people, and an answer that gives the escalation queue the last word on everything has lost the boundary. Lesson section: Step 1: Who can end cover."

    prompt: "A household's status decides who may process a termination request. What are the three statuses, and what may you do in each of them?"
    sampleAnswer: "There are three: a household where every member is on Medi-Cal, a mixed-program family where some members are on Medi-Cal and some on Covered California, and a household of Covered California members only. You find the status on the enrollment dashboard, by going to the eligibility results listed further down that screen. Where everyone is on Medi-Cal you end nothing and pass the consumer on to their local county office, with its telephone number. In a mixed-program family you end the Covered California members, as one process if they want the same date and as a separate one for each if they want different dates, while the Medi-Cal members and any change to household membership belong to the county. Where everyone is on Covered California you may end any member's cover."

    prompt: "What is the difference between cancelling a plan and reporting a change, and how do you know which one the consumer needs?"
    sampleAnswer: "Cancelling ends the member's cover and leaves them on the application, still a tax household member, and the cancellation is then documented in Salesforce. Reporting a change is how a member comes out of the household altogether, and that has to be done with the county, because membership of the household is not yours to change. The question that tells them apart is whether the member is still a tax dependent of the primary. A consumer who has moved away and is no longer a tax dependent is asking to come out of the household, so it is a change to report, not a plan to cancel."
    explanation: "Look for the effect on tax household membership, the county's role in a change, and the test that separates the two actions. An answer that describes cancelling as removing somebody from the household has swapped the two, and an answer that leaves out the test leaves the representative to guess. Lesson section: Step 3: Cancel the plan, or report a change."

    type: free-recall
    prompt: "A consumer says they ended their cover by mistake and want it back. What can you actually do for them?"
    sampleAnswer: "Because the cancellation was voluntary, that cover cannot be reinstated. The request still goes to the escalation queue, and the expected outcome there is a refusal, so you escalate rather than turning the consumer away yourself, because denying a reinstatement without escalating is the mistake that is penalised. What you can offer instead is a fresh enrolment if the consumer has a qualifying life event, and otherwise they wait for the open enrolment period. A voluntary cancellation is not itself a qualifying life event, so ending one cover does not open a special enrolment period for another."
    explanation: "Look for the rule that a voluntary cancellation cannot be reinstated, the requirement to escalate rather than refuse, and the two routes back in, which are a qualifying life event or the open enrolment period. An answer that says the cover can simply be restored has skipped the escalation, and an answer that turns the consumer away at the desk has missed the rule about not denying it yourself. Lesson section: Step 4: The voluntary way."
