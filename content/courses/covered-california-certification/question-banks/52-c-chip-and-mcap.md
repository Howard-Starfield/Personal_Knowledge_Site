    prompt: "What is the children's programme, where does it operate, and what has to be true for a child to qualify?"
    sampleAnswer: "It is a scheme giving children affordable and comprehensive medical, mental and vision cover, and it runs only in the counties of San Mateo, San Francisco and Santa Clara. A child qualifies when they live in one of those three counties, when they are from birth up to the 19th birthday, when the household income is in the band above 266% and no more than 322% of the federal poverty level, when the child is a U.S. citizen, a U.S. national, or lawfully present, and when they have a social security number, with two exceptions to that last one for newborns and for children whose number is still pending. A child does not qualify if they already have minimum essential coverage in place, or if they are incarcerated or deceased."
    explanation: "Look for the three counties, the age, the income band, the citizenship or lawful presence condition, the social security number rule and its two exceptions, and the three exclusions for existing cover, incarceration and death. An answer that names the counties but leaves out the income band has left out the test that decides the programme, and an answer that treats a pending social security number as a disqualification has it backwards. Lesson section: Step 2: The children's programme: who qualifies."

    prompt: "A consumer declines the children's programme for their child. What happens to the child's cover and to the family's financial help, and what has to happen if the family wants to come back later?"
    sampleAnswer: "The child may enrol in a Covered California health plan, but with no subsidy at all, and the decline does not change the eligibility result and does not create premium assistance for the child. If the family's percentage of the poverty level later goes down, the child may qualify for Medi-Cal instead. If the family stops the enrolment and comes back at a later date, the original eligibility may still be in effect for the child, provided the child still qualifies. Separately, where a consumer wants to move a child out of an unsubsidised Covered California plan and into the programme, you have to tell them they need to get in touch with their county's C-CHIP office, and a change to the household's application is something the service centre does itself, without passing the case to the county."
    explanation: "Look for the plan without a subsidy, the unchanged eligibility result, the absence of premium assistance, the fall in the poverty level percentage opening Medi-Cal instead, and the original eligibility still standing if the family returns. An answer that says the decline makes the child eligible for premium assistance has the wrong sign, and an answer that leaves out the county's C-CHIP office has left out the route the consumer has to take. Lesson section: Step 3: Declining the children's programme, and changes."

    prompt: "What is the pregnancy programme, and how is the household size worked out for a pregnant consumer?"
    sampleAnswer: "MCAP is the Medical Access Program, and it provides health coverage for pregnant individuals who are eligible and who meet the programme's requirements. The household rule is the one to hold on to: for Medi-Cal for pregnant individuals, the household size is worked out by counting the pregnant consumer as one and then adding the number of children expected. So a consumer who is pregnant with twins counts as three."
    explanation: "Look for the name of the programme, who it covers, and the counting rule of one plus the number of expected children, worked through for twins. An answer that counts only the pregnant consumer has missed the expected children, and an answer that counts one plus the number of pregnancies rather than the number of expected children has confused the rule with the birth. Lesson section: Step 4: The pregnancy programme, and the household count."

    prompt: "How long does the pregnancy programme's cover run, and which changes do not bring it to an end?"
    sampleAnswer: "Consumers stay covered throughout their pregnancy and through the 12-month extended postpartum coverage period, and it goes on however their income, citizenship or immigration status changes. It applies to individuals enrolled in Medi-Cal for pregnant individuals and in MCAP, and the official record puts the same period the same way. A consumer is free to bring their postpartum Medi-Cal cover to an end at any time. Bringing minimum essential coverage to an end of your own accord is not a qualifying life event, so it cannot open a special enrolment period."
    explanation: "Look for the pregnancy plus the 12-month extended postpartum coverage period, the three changes that do not stop it, and the two facts about the end of it: that a consumer may cancel at any time, and that a voluntary ending of cover is not a qualifying life event. An answer that says the cover ends when the income rises has turned a continuation rule into a cut-off, and an answer that leaves out the cancellation right has left the consumer without their choice. Lesson section: Step 5: How long the pregnancy cover runs."
