    type: free-recall
    prompt: "Who is the primary enrollee on a case, and what may the primary do?"
    sampleAnswer: "The primary enrollee is the person the case is built around, the account holder that the rest of the household is attached to. The training set out the role as a set of rights rather than a definition: the primary may act on the case without restriction. Every other person on the case is measured against that, so knowing who the primary is decides how much a caller may do and how much you may tell them. A spouse has the primary's powers apart from one carve-out, and a non-primary adult enrollee has a much shorter list and no access at all to the primary's personal details."
    explanation: "Look for the primary as the person the case is built around, and for the statement that the primary may act on the case without restriction. An answer that supplies a technical test for who counts as primary has substituted a rule the training never stated, and an answer that stops at the definition without saying what the role permits has left out the half of the question that decides the call. Lesson section: Step 1: One primary on every case."

    prompt: "A spouse calls about a case whose primary is their partner. Set out what the spouse may do on the case, and the one thing they may not do."
    sampleAnswer: "A spouse holds the same powers as the primary, with a single exception: the spouse cannot give a one-time verbal authorisation for a third party to speak on the primary's behalf. The training's list of what a spouse may do covers resetting the account, making changes to the case, cancelling the coverage, adding an authorized representative and adding a plan. The exception shows up when the telephone is passed around: the primary may hand the call to somebody else, because the primary is the one giving the permission, but the spouse cannot hand on the primary's authority."
    explanation: "Look for the spouse holding the primary's powers, the single exception about one-time authorisation for a third party, and at least three of the actions the training listed. An answer that limits the spouse to a token role such as being allowed only to listen has dropped the rule, and an answer that names the exception as resetting the account or cancelling coverage has picked the wrong thing. Lesson section: Step 2: What a spouse on the same case may do."

    prompt: "A non-primary adult enrollee on the case calls. Set out what they may do, what they may not do, and what you must not tell them."
    sampleAnswer: "A non-primary adult enrollee, meaning someone on the application the way a dependent of the children on the application is on it, has three powers: ending their own coverage and only their own, providing documents, and getting their own 1095-A. They cannot end anybody else's coverage, so a 22-year-old cannot ask for her parents' coverage to be stopped. And you cannot give them personal information about the primary: the training's example was the parents' social security number, which is not yours to read out to a caller. The account password and login details sit in the same list, though that clause sits inside the garbled part of the recording, so treat it as the likely reading rather than a settled rule."
    explanation: "Look for the three permitted actions, the refusal to end anybody else's coverage, and the refusal to disclose the primary's personal details. An answer that lets a non-primary adult enrollee end the whole household's coverage has given away the point of the rule, and an answer that treats a family relationship as good enough reason to read a social security number down the phone has missed the limit. Lesson section: Step 3: The narrow rights of a non-primary adult enrollee."
