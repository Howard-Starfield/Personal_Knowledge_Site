    prompt: "A spouse calls asking you to add health and dental cover for the primary. Set out the order you work in, and what each step depends on."
    sampleAnswer: "The order the training set was: work the change on the application first, covering the household size, the income, the tax filing status and the spouse's own status; record the event that triggered the change, which in the scenario was a new domestic partnership, and resubmit the application; add the health plan from the enrollment dashboard rather than the choose-plan option; add the dental plan, which can only be opened once the medical plan is finished; and finally document the case and close it. Each step depends on the one before it. The quotes you compare plans on come out of the corrected household, so the application work comes first, and the dental option does not appear at all until the medical enrolment is complete."
    explanation: "Look for the five steps in order, and for the two dependencies. An answer that starts with the plan selection has skipped the application work the price depends on, and an answer that treats health and dental as one step has missed the order between them. Lesson section: Stage 1: The order of work on a plan call."
  - id: "8cf1f10d-84b1-419a-a1e4-964a0b00a6d6"
    revision: 1

    type: free-recall
    prompt: "Why does the household have to be right before a price is shown, and what changes when it is not?"
    sampleAnswer: "Every figure the system quotes is read out of the application, so the price is only as good as the household the application describes. A spouse who has employer-sponsored cover is not applying for marketplace cover and has to be recorded as having not applied, or as not eligible. Leave that as an applicant state and the household is counted with more people than it really is, the financial help is worked out on the wrong basis, and the quote is wrong. The worked example made it visible: the same $80,000 with no income change at all showed around $600 for a household of one and around $340 for a household of two, because that income sat outside 400% of the federal poverty level for one person and inside it for two. Correct the household, resubmit the application, and only then start reading premiums to the consumer."
    explanation: "Look for the link between the recorded household and the quote, and for the worked numbers showing the same income producing two different prices. An answer that says the price depends on income alone has missed household size, and an answer that leaves out the employer-cover reason for the correction has left out the fix. Lesson section: Stage 2: Get the household right before you price anything."
  - id: "b7c79bc2-c3e4-4814-a42b-ccbe837176ea"
    revision: 1
    status: active
    type: free-recall
    prompt: "What goes in the note you leave on a plan-selection call, and what stops you editing it?"
    sampleAnswer: "The subject names what the call was about in two to three words, maybe four, and the description is the short account of what the consumer asked for and what you did. The note carries the detail: that this was a primary call and what you did on it, the household size you set, the income, the tax filing status and any other relevant application information, why the spouse is not asking for cover, because the cover they hold comes from an employer, the qualifying life event written out in full once and then abbreviated with its date, the eligibility result you gave the consumer, and what happens next. It also records that the household has been asked for proof of income and the date, which is usually around 95 days from the call, or 155 days for proof of income in a later session - the training's own figures, since the official record publishes no number of days at all, so the date to read to the consumer is the one on the record. Two things can spoil that: clicking completed request before the notes are written deletes the temporary notes, so they have to be copied out first, and once the case is posted it stays editable for roughly 15 to 20 minutes before it locks, after which a later note is added instead."
    explanation: "Look for the two short fields, the list of facts the note has to carry, the document request with its date, and both limits: the temporary notes that are deleted if you close too early, and the edit window that closes shortly after the case is posted. An answer that leaves out the document request has left the next person unable to tell that the household is waiting on something. Lesson section: Stage 6: The record you leave behind."
