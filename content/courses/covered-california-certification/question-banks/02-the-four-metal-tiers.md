These are averages across all members of a category, not a promise about what any one member will pay."

Lesson section: Step 1: Four categories, one set of benefits."

    prompt: "Explain what the percentage for each category actually measures, and what it does not promise."
    sampleAnswer: "It measures the average percentage of health-care costs the plan is expected to cover, as opposed to what the member pays out of pocket. So a Bronze plan picks up roughly 60 of every 100 dollars of covered care and the member picks up about 40, while a Platinum plan splits nearer 90 and 10. It does not promise a particular amount for a particular member, because what someone actually pays depends on the care they use, the plan's deductible, and whether the provider is in network."
    explanation: "Look for the phrase average share of health-care costs the plan is expected to cover, and for the three things that decide an individual member's real spending. An answer that treats the percentage as a promise about a single bill has missed the point of the word average. Lesson section: Step 2: What the percentage actually means."

    prompt: "Why does Silver have to be described as a range rather than as a single figure? Say what the training stated and what the official record publishes."
    sampleAnswer: "The training stated Silver as a flat 70%. The official record publishes Silver as a range from 70% to 94%, because enhanced Silver plans exist alongside Silver 70: the official site lists Silver 73, Silver 87 and Silver 94, each of which lowers cost sharing for the households that qualify for it. The mismatch with the training is worth naming explicitly rather than smoothing over: the training gave a flat 70%, and the official record gives a range. Lesson section: Step 3: Silver is the category that moves."
