    type: free-recall
    prompt: "What is a required case review, and what are the three things it checks?"
    sampleAnswer: "A required case review is a look at the case you already have open, carried out as part of a service call. It checks three things. First, the contact information that authentication did not already cover, which means the email address on file and the preferred method of contact, because the home address and the telephone number have both been used to authenticate. Second, the consent for verification, and whether it is about to run out, in which case you offer to update it. Third, the eligibility for every person in the household, read from the eligibility summary for each person rather than only for the primary. The review also includes a look at the personal verifications, which is the document step rather than one of the three checks."
    explanation: "Look for the three checks named in order, with the contact information narrowed to what authentication left out. An answer that counts the document step as one of the three has confused it with the eligibility check, and an answer that omits the per-person eligibility summary has covered only the primary. Lesson section: Step 2: The three things the review checks."

    prompt: "In which situations does a required case review not apply, and what should you do when you are not sure?"
    sampleAnswer: "It does not apply to a household where every member is on Medi-Cal, to an inactive case or a brand-new applicant, to a non-primary adult enrollee or a divorced member, to someone calling to end the whole household's Covered California cover, to a household with no active enrolment in any medical cover, to a consumer who has an exemption, and to an outbound call. When you are not sure, do it. Doing the review where it was not required earns credit, while not doing it where it was not required is recorded as not applicable, which is neither a penalty nor a credit."
    explanation: "Look for the list of exceptions and the instruction to do the review when unsure, together with the fact that extra work is credited rather than penalised. An answer that stops at the Medi-Cal-only case has left out the other exceptions, and an answer that says to skip the review when unsure has inverted the instruction. Lesson section: Step 3: When the review does not apply."

    prompt: "A case shows documents still due. What does the review require you to tell the consumer, and what may you do for them?"
    sampleAnswer: "You have to tell them what is due and when it is due. A pending status means the application could not check the information electronically, and the consumer then has a window of days to send documentation: the training gives 95 days for most documents and 155 days for proof of income, and because the official record publishes no number of days at all, the date on the record is the one to read off and quote. You may guide them to their own online account and talk them through adding the document, but you do not upload it for them, even if they offer to email it to you. If what they uploaded will not meet the requirement, tell them so and ask whether they want to send something else. If you cannot tell either way, call the lead line so that the document can be checked and passed if it is acceptable."
    explanation: "Look for the obligation to state the document and its deadline, the guide-but-do-not-upload rule, and the escalation to the lead line when acceptability is unclear. An answer that leaves out the deadline has dropped the part the consumer actually needs, and an answer that says you can upload a document they send you has reversed the rule. The deadline is not one number: it is 95 days for most documents and 155 for proof of income, and both of those are the training's own figures rather than published policy. Lesson section: Step 4: The document step."

    prompt: "A case carries minimum essential coverage verification items. Which of them can you close on the consumer's own signed attestation, which one is settled on a form from the website, and which one can you never pass yourself?"
    sampleAnswer: "Two of them can be closed on the consumer's own signed attestation: the item for cover through the consumer's job, and the item for cover that does not come from a job. The consumer writes the statement out by hand or types it up, puts their CalHEERS case number on it, dates it and signs it, and says in it that they have no cover through an employer and no offer of one, and separately that they have no cover that does not come from an employer; when that document is uploaded the item can be passed, by you or by the lead. MEC Medicare is settled on the attestation form carried on the website, which the consumer ticks, completes, signs and dates. MEC Medi-Cal is never passed by the front line: the county controls it, and neither a denial letter nor a personal attestation changes that. If you pass it by mistake the county fails it later, and the result is no financial assistance and a negative action."
    explanation: "Look for the split between the two employer items, which a consumer attestation closes, the Medicare item, which has its own form on the website, and the Medi-Cal item, which belongs to the county and is never passed by the front line however convincing the consumer's paper is. An answer that says a personal attestation settles every item has missed the boundary, and an answer that leaves out the consequence of passing the Medi-Cal item has missed why the boundary matters. Lesson section: Step 6: The four minimum essential coverage items."
