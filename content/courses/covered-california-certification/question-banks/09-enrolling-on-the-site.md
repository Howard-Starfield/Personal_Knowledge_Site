    type: free-recall
    prompt: "Walk through the steps that turn a plan a consumer has chosen into a bound enrolment on the portal, and say who performs each one."
    sampleAnswer: "Nothing binds anybody to anything until a plan is actually selected. After that the representative selects the plan, the portal plays two disclaimers, and the consumer has to agree to proceed. The representative then enters their four-digit PIN and signs on the consumer's behalf. The last step belongs to the consumer: they make a first payment to the carrier, and by that point the enrollee is bound by the plan. Comparing premiums and benefits on the same site before all of that is harmless, because browsing never binds anyone."
    explanation: "A correct answer has the order right — plan selected, disclaimers played, agreement given, PIN entered, signature applied, first payment made — and keeps the three actors apart, so the consumer agrees and pays while the representative signs. An answer that has the representative collecting the premium, or that lets the consumer sign in the representative's place, has a step wrong. Lesson section: Stage 1: From a chosen plan to a bound enrolment."

    prompt: "Why does the portal require a four-digit PIN before an enrolment can be completed, and what does that tell you about the design of the site?"
    sampleAnswer: "The PIN is the control that stops the site from letting a consumer drift into a bound enrolment by accident. The training described the portal as deliberately built so consumers cannot accidentally enrol themselves into a plan: browsing and comparing is fine, but a plan only becomes bound once somebody has gone through the agreement, entered the PIN and signed electronically. Representatives hold that PIN and consumers do not, so the step a representative performs is the step that keeps accidental enrolment out of the system."
    explanation: "A correct answer ties the PIN to the deliberate design of the site and to accidental self-enrolment, and identifies the PIN as the representative-side control in that sequence. An answer that treats the PIN as a security formality, or that says consumers are blocked from enrolling at all, has missed the point. Lesson section: Stage 2: Why the site is built this way."

    prompt: "What is the consent standard for the disclaimers on the portal, and why is it deliberately narrower than you might expect?"
    sampleAnswer: "The only question that matters is whether the consumer agrees to the disclaimer: yes or no. Nobody can tell whether the consumer is really listening, whether they are understanding it, or how their hearing is working, so no one is expected to try. The portal plays the disclaimer, the consumer says yes, and the work moves on, with no extra step for establishing comprehension. What happens to the application afterwards is the application’s own job, not the representative’s."
    explanation: "A correct answer states the standard as a plain agreement or refusal and explains that comprehension is not being judged. An answer that adds a check of understanding, or that says the representative decides whether the consumer was informed enough, has added a requirement the material does not contain. Lesson section: Stage 3: The consent standard: a yes or a no."
