    prompt: "Put the escalation sequence in order, and say what the lead line actually does before an escalation is approved."
    sampleAnswer: "The order is: post your notes using the standard template, give the consumer the reference number for the call, reach the lead line, and only then call the escalation queue. The lead line is contacted before the escalation queue every time, whatever the case looks like. The lead posts their own notes and does their own research, because they have to be sure the case is going to the escalation queue appropriately. Then the lead decides: either the case is rejected, with the reason named, or the escalation is approved, because everything you have said adds up and it holds up against the knowledge base, and you may now telephone the escalation queue. Only after that do you call the escalation queue, which takes the case over and sends it up to PSU to be assigned."
    explanation: "Look for the four steps in order, and for the lead doing their own research and being able to send the case back. An answer that lets the representative go straight to the escalation queue has dropped the rule that makes the lead line meaningful. Lesson section: Step 2: The sequence, in order."

    prompt: "What goes in the escalation template, where does the template go, and which parts of the case can you still change afterwards?"
    sampleAnswer: "The standard template is posted every time you telephone the lead line, or anybody further up the support chain, for anything at all, and it goes in Post, which is the Feed, rather than in the notes. It carries the primary contact's name and phone number, copied from the CalHEERS case unless the consumer prefers a different number or an email; the other cases you researched, with N/A when you researched none and only the ones that were helpful and relevant; the CalHEERS case number; whether it is a mixed household, which is no for a Medi-Cal or mixed-household consumer; why you are contacting them; and the actions you are requesting. You can update the subject and add the carrier and the escalation reason. The description is the one field you cannot edit, so anything another desk has to read has to be right in it the first time. Your steps to resolve can be edited for roughly the first hour after you close the case, and it can lock at any time, after which anything extra goes in Post."
    explanation: "Look for Post rather than the notes, the mixed-household answer of no, and the two editing windows. An answer that puts the template in the notes, or that assumes the description can be revised later, has missed where the record lives. Lesson section: Step 4: The escalation template."
  - id: "4acd7c70-58eb-43db-bb10-220c02287772"

    status: active
    type: free-recall
    prompt: "You have made a valid escalation and handed the call to the escalation queue. What happens to your case, and what are you still responsible for?"
    sampleAnswer: "Escalation takes the call over. It goes up to PSU, PSU is assigned the case, and from that point your job is to stop. Chasing the consumer is no longer your duty: you do not follow up with them, and you do not telephone them with updates; that is PSU's job, and PSU is the team responsible for closing out the case. You keep your own case open. When you pass the call over to the lead line or the escalation queue you do not close the case, and the training flagged clicking close case as a mistake new representatives make. Afterwards the case's status moves through the PSU statuses of new, in progress, pending carrier, pending customer, pending help desk ticket and closed, on timeframes of one to ten business days for the initial contact and one to thirty business days for the resolution."
    explanation: "Look for the hand-off ending your follow-up duty, and for the instruction to leave your own case open. An answer that makes you responsible for calling the consumer with updates, or that says you close the case once it has gone, has inverted the boundary. Lesson section: Step 6: Where your job stops."

    prompt: "A consumer's coverage was terminated for non-payment and they want it back. What do you do, and what is the one exception?"
    sampleAnswer: "Any reinstatement due to non-payment goes directly to the carrier now, and it used to be that you escalated on the consumer's behalf. So you confirm that the consumer was terminated for non-payment by their carrier, you tell them to call their carrier and make their past-due premiums to them, and you tell them the carrier will then contact Covered California to get them reinstated. The one exception is that you escalate if the carrier is on the line with the consumer and that carrier representative is authorised to approve a reinstatement. There are two tests and both have to pass: is the carrier on the line, and are they authorised to approve reinstatements due to non-payment. If the carrier representative says they are not authorised, then it is done and you are not escalating, and you continue the conversation with the carrier."
    explanation: "Look for the routing to the carrier, the three things you tell the consumer, and the two-test exception. An answer that escalates a non-payment reinstatement without the carrier on the line has missed the change, and one that treats a carrier on the line as sufficient has missed the authorisation test. Lesson section: Step 8: The cases that belong to the carrier."
