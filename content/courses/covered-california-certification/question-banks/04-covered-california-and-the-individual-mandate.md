    prompt: "Explain how a state can run its own health insurance marketplace, what California chose, and when it launched."
    sampleAnswer: "A state could either run a marketplace of its own as a state-based exchange or leave the job to the federal government. California elected to create its own, Covered California, and its first open enrollment period opened in October 2013. The site is coveredca.com, while the federal government's website for the law is healthcare.gov. Covered California is not itself an insurance company: it is an independent part of state government that connects residents to plans and works out what financial help they qualify for."
    explanation: "A correct answer gives the choice a state had, California's decision to run its own marketplace, the October 2013 first open enrollment period, and coveredca.com.     prompt: "Explain the three layers of the individual mandate and where each one stands."
    sampleAnswer: "The federal requirement is that most US citizens and legal residents must have health insurance; it remains enforceable, and it is what people mean by the individual mandate. The federal penalty is gone: the federal monetary penalty was removed, so nobody is penalised at the federal level for failing to have minimum essential coverage. The California penalty is the third layer: California implemented its own individual mandate with an enforceable monetary penalty, in effect since 1 January 2020, administered by the Franchise Tax Board."
    explanation: "A correct answer keeps the three apart and gives the date and the administering agency for the state penalty. The failure this question is built around is collapsing the layers and saying the individual mandate was repealed; what was removed is the federal penalty, not the requirement, and the state added a penalty of its own. Lesson section: Step 2: Three layers of the mandate."
