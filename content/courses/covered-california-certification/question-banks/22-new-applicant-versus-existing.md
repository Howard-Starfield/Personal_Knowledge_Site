  - id: "4649929a-b4cd-4910-b402-c5eebb333b6a"
    revision: 1
    status: active
    type: free-recall
    prompt: "How do you decide whether a caller is a new applicant or an existing consumer, and what makes the answer definite?"
    sampleAnswer: "You search before you decide. You search by name and date of birth, and if nothing comes back you ask for the full social security number and search again. Asking for the full social security number is what guarantees that the consumer is a new applicant, so you ask once or twice and then you stop, because asking a consumer to spell their number repeatedly is what turns a friendly call into a hostile one. Then you read the result back and get a yes or no from the consumer. The answer is definite because it comes from the search and the confirmation, and never from what the eligibility determination said, since an existing consumer whose eligibility came back ineligible is still an existing consumer."
    explanation: "Look for the order of the steps, the reason the full social security number is asked for, and the statement that new-applicant status never comes from the eligibility result. An answer that treats an ineligible consumer as a new applicant has taken the counterexample the training used, and an answer that leaves out the search has skipped the step the decision actually rests on. Lesson section: Stage 1: The search decides who you are talking to."

    prompt: "A consumer applies outside open enrolment and the result comes back ineligible. Where do you find the reason, what does the reason say, and what do you tell the consumer?"
    sampleAnswer: "You go to the account, to eligibility results under manage your application, and then you open see full details, because the headline status is not the reason. In the recorded call the detail gave two reasons: the consumer was ineligible for Medi-Cal because they were about the income limit, and they were ineligible for Covered California because, based on the information provided, they did not meet the requirements to enrol in a plan outside of open enrolment. So you tell the consumer that based on the information provided they do not meet the requirements to enrol in a plan outside of open enrolment, and that they can reapply if their situation changes or during open enrolment. In practice the advice is that they can come back during open enrolment or when they have a valid qualifying life event, because a qualifying life event is what changes the situation."
    explanation: "Look for the location of the result and the full details link, for the reason in the consumer's own terms, and for the advice that follows from it. An answer that stops at the ineligible headline has found the what without the why, and an answer that gives advice which does not match the reason on the screen has left the consumer with a next step that does not exist. Lesson section: Stage 4: Reading the result and giving the reason."
  - id: "ef2dae9a-ca9b-44a7-b271-fcf96fe30a08"
    revision: 1
    status: active
    type: free-recall
    prompt: "What has to be in the case note for a new-applicant call, and what does not?"
    sampleAnswer: "Two things absolutely have to be documented: the CalHEERS case number, and the name of the member the call was about. Beyond those, the note describes what happened rather than reproducing everything that was typed, so it records that the consumer called to begin a new application, that the search returned no results, that a quick sort was performed and returned no transfer, that the application continued in CalHEERS, the household size and the income, and the outcome with the advice that was given. What does not belong is a spill of the rest of the application, because you would be typing for days. An acronym may be spelled out once and then reused, which is how a note stays short while remaining readable, but a note that strips out every noun stops being understandable to the person who reads it later."
    explanation: "Look for the two must-have items, for the instruction to describe rather than to reproduce, and for the difference between defining an acronym once and abbreviating everything. An answer that asks for a field-by-field copy of the application has missed the point of the note, and an answer that treats a fully abbreviated note as good practice has confused the acronym rule with the whole rule. Lesson section: Stage 5: What the note has to say."
