    prompt: "A consumer calls about coverage they think they have twice. In a duplicate-case escalation, what do you do first, and what do you do once you have the cases?"
    sampleAnswer: "The first move is a combo search on the consumer, and you take every case that comes back, including the ones showing as closed and terminated, because a case you think you can ignore may still be one you need, and the cases are worked through by process of elimination. Then you pull up each case in turn and go into the enrolments on it rather than reading the header, because a case can be closed and still carry a plan with coverage dates on the record. Only once the cases are found do you ask the consumer which plan they want to keep, and you take that as the start of the conversation. The order matters: find the cases first, then work out which coverage the consumer is keeping."
    explanation: "Look for the combo search, for every case being taken including the closed and terminated ones, for reading the enrolments on each case, and for the plan question coming second. An answer that starts from the consumer's plan preference has the order wrong, and one that stops at the case in front of the consumer has too narrow a scope. Lesson section: Stage 1: What a duplicate case is, and the first move."

It is complicated because if the consumer used services under all of the policies, the fix is hard: the provider may have to submit the claim to the other health insurance company, and that only works when the doctor is in network with them. There are a lot of things that have to be determined, and the service centre is not the desk that determines them. The recording names this downstream team as ERI&T without expanding the letters, so treat the abbreviation as unverified and work to the process instead."
