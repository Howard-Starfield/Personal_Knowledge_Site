    prompt: "In your own words, what does the training mean by call control, and what is the goal of the call?"
    sampleAnswer: "Call control is guiding the conversation along a deliberate, consistent process of questioning so that the call reaches the right answer, rather than reacting to whatever the caller says next. In practice it means identifying the issue early, asking the kind of question the moment needs, listening properly, using your tools and moving towards a resolution. The goal of the call is a solution to the caller's problem or an answer to the caller's question. Control does not mean taking the conversation away from the caller, and it does not mean being brisk; their problem stays the destination of the call."
    explanation: "A complete answer says that control is about directing the conversation deliberately and consistently towards a solution, that the goal is to solve the caller's issue or answer the caller's question, and that it is not about shutting the caller down. An answer that describes control as talking over the caller, or as keeping the call short, misses the point the training was making. Lesson section: Step 1: What call control is for."

First, get the issue stated as early in the call as possible; when a consumer cannot explain it in specific terms, use probing questions to help them clarify, because knowing the issue early lets you focus on solving it. Second, handle a talkative consumer: wait for a pause in their explanation and then interject with a short statement that names the next step, such as saying you need a couple of pieces of information first and that you will help with those, and then ask for the first one. Third, manage the interaction: when a consumer asks a very open-ended, off-topic question, give a brief and friendly answer and then put a pertinent question back to move the call forward."
