    prompt: "What is the annual redetermination, what does it look at, and what has to be in place before it can run?"
    sampleAnswer: "The annual redetermination is the yearly review of whether a consumer still qualifies for premium assistance and for the Medi-Cal programmes. It works by electronic verification, so the household's income and tax information are checked against federal records instead of being sent in on paper. Covered California has to hold the consumer's authorisation, or consent, before it may verify income and tax information for that purpose, and that consent is captured in the signature section of the application, where the applicant is asked whether the marketplace may run electronic verifications for up to five years."
    explanation: "Look for three things: the yearly review of eligibility, the electronic check against federal records, and the consent that has to be held before either can happen. An answer that treats the redetermination as something the marketplace may do on its own has left out the permission, and an answer that names the permission but not the electronic verification has only half of the picture. Lesson section: Stage 1: The yearly check and the permission behind it."

    prompt: "How long can a consumer authorise electronic verification for, and what does a consumer have to do if they did not authorise far enough ahead?"
    sampleAnswer: "Anywhere between zero and five years, and the consumer can change that figure at any time, in either direction. The training encouraged five years for a consumer who expects to be a long-term member, and accepted a shorter period for someone who is only on the marketplace temporarily, such as while waiting for an employer to offer cover. Whatever the length chosen, if the household wants financial assistance or Medi-Cal and the consent has run out, permission has to be given again. A consumer who authorised only a year or less has to go back online and give the marketplace permission again before the annual renewal process can be completed."
    explanation: "Look for the zero-to-five-year range, the fact that the consumer can change it, and the rule that catches people out: consent given for a year or less does not carry the consumer through the following renewal.     type: free-recall
    prompt: "What is passive renewal, and when does it not happen?"
    sampleAnswer: "Passive renewal is what happens when a consumer does nothing at all: the application and the plan renew themselves for the coming year. The consumer is put back into the same plan unless they cancel the coverage, they pick a different plan, or the plan they hold has gone off the market and they take no action to choose a replacement. The official record adds that the re-enrolment happens no later than 30 days after the renewal deadline, on the strength of the household and income details the file already holds, so a household that has not reported a change carries last year's figures into the new year. During the open enrolment period a renewing consumer can still shop the plan catalogue, and may find that they have become eligible for premium assistance for the first time."
    explanation: "Look for the three exceptions to automatic re-enrolment, and for the two things a renewing consumer can still do. An answer that only says the plan renews by itself has left out the cases where it does not, and an answer that leaves out the information on file has missed the caution the official record attaches to it. Lesson section: Stage 3: Passive renewal, and choosing something different."

    type: free-recall
    prompt: "A consumer is renewing into the same product with the carrier they already have. Do they owe a first payment for the new year, and what do they still have to do?"
    sampleAnswer: "No new first payment. A binder payment is not required where a subsidised enrollee is moving into the very same plan, or into a different plan of the same product, such as moving from a bronze or silver HMO to a gold HMO with the carrier they already have. The plan carries over into the new year, and the Pay Now button is not offered for January because the plan is rolling over rather than starting. The premium itself may have moved, because the carrier may have raised or reduced prices. What the consumer still has to do is pay the January premium by its due date, or the coverage is not effectuated, and they keep the full grace period if they are late."
    explanation: "Look for the exemption and its limits, and for the payment that survives it. An answer that says a renewal always needs a first payment has missed the exemption; an answer that says nothing is owed at all has dropped the January premium, which is the payment that actually has to be made on time. Lesson section: Stage 4: The first payment for the new year."
