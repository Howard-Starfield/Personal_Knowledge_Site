    prompt: "Explain what the no wrong door approach is, and what happens in a household where two members end up in different programmes."
    sampleAnswer: "A consumer fills in one application, and that single application evaluates them for both a Covered California plan and Medi-Cal. Nobody has to work out in advance which programme they belong to, and the consumer who came in asking about a marketplace plan does not have to start again with Medi-Cal. The application can also come back split across the household, with one member eligible for Medi-Cal and another eligible for a Covered California plan. The training called that a mixed household; the official record calls it a mixed-program family and describes it as normal."
    explanation: "Look for both halves: one form, two programmes, and a household that can come back mixed. An answer that describes the consumer choosing a programme before applying has missed the point of the approach, and an answer that treats a mixed household as an error to be corrected has missed the second half. Lesson section: Step 1: One application, two programmes."

    prompt: "Compare the two kinds of enroller who help people in person: what access each has to the consumer's information, and how their authority differs."
    sampleAnswer: "A licensed insurance agent holds a California Department of Insurance licence and is additionally approved by Covered California to sell its health and dental plans. An enrolment counsellor works for an organisation that holds a grant under the Navigator Grant Program, and those grants fund outreach, education and post-enrolment services. The training's distinction is that the agent has somewhat more access to the consumer's information and more authority to perform certain actions than the counsellor, while both provide in-person assistance with Covered California. The official glossary adds the limit on the counsellor: they can help a consumer apply and enrol, but cannot recommend which plan is best for them."
    explanation: "Look for the agent-versus-counsellor distinction and the direction of the difference, since the agent has more access and more authority rather than less. An answer that treats the two as interchangeable, or that claims the counsellor has the wider authority, has the comparison backwards. Lesson section: Step 5: Agent and counsellor are not the same."
