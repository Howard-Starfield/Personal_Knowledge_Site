    type: free-recall
    prompt: "A consumer tells you the California health coverage penalty for a family of four is $2,700. Explain what is wrong with that, and what a representative should say instead."
    sampleAnswer: "The $2,700 figure is real but dated: it is the December 2025 release's household figure, built from $900 per adult plus $450 per child, so it applies to a family of four with two adults and two children and is not a flat amount for every household. It has been superseded, and it does not appear on the live Covered California penalty page at all. That page now publishes a minimum of $950 per adult and $450 per child under 18, plus an affordability threshold of 8.05% of projected annual household income, and it is the threshold that carries the tax year 2026 label. The Franchise Tax Board, not Covered California, computes and collects the penalty, so the representative should name the body and the tax year and point the consumer at the FTB's Penalty Estimator for their own amount."
    explanation: "A complete answer gives four things: where the $2,700 came from and that it is a per-adult-and-per-child figure rather than a flat family amount, that it has been superseded and is not on the live penalty page, what the current published amounts are and which of them carries the 2026 label, and the fact that the Franchise Tax Board computes the penalty. An answer that only gives a new number has not corrected the error, because the consumer's number was not wrong, it was mislabelled and out of date. Lesson section: Stage 1: Numbers that are not what people remember."
