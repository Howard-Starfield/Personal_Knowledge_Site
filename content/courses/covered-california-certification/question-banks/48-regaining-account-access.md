    type: free-recall
    prompt: "What are the four rules that decide whether a consumer's password may be reset, and which of them comes first?"
    sampleAnswer: "First, representatives can help consumers with login errors caused by invalid usernames, passwords or responses to security questions. Second, a password may only be reset or unblocked for the primary member, and only after authentication. Third, only the primary member, their spouse, or a registered domestic partner may have the primary member's password reset. Fourth, an authorised representative has to hold an account of their own for the case they act on. The rule that comes first in practice is authentication, because nothing at all happens to a consumer's username, password or account before the consumer has been authenticated."
    explanation: "Look for all four rules, and for authentication being the gate that comes before the rest rather than a fifth rule beside them. An answer that leaves out the requirement about a registered domestic partner has skipped one of the three people who may have the primary's password reset, and an answer that treats the first rule as a licence to act before authentication has inverted the order. Lesson section: Step 1: Authentication first, and the right account."

    prompt: "How do you tell a locked account from a blocked one, and what can a consumer do for themselves without you?"
    sampleAnswer: "You ask the consumer one question: have they entered their one-time passcode incorrectly. If they have, the account is blocked; if they have not, it is locked. The consumer sees the difference as two different messages, one stating that the account cannot be confirmed and the other that the maximum number of attempts has been reached. On their own, a consumer who has registered an email address or a cell phone for a one-time passcode can reset their own password or PIN, retrieve their own username and answer their own security questions, by going to coveredca.com, choosing sign in, then the forgot password or forgot username link, and entering their username and date of birth. Two things bring them back to the counter: not having registered a one-time passcode, and reaching the maximum number of failed login attempts."
    explanation: "Look for the single diagnostic question and the direction of its answer, since a yes means blocked and a no means locked, and for the self-serve route with the registration of an email address or a cell phone as its precondition. An answer that says a blocked account releases itself, or that a consumer can self-serve without registering a one-time passcode, has got both halves wrong. Lesson section: Step 3: Locked or blocked, and the road the consumer can take alone."

    prompt: "Take me through the service centre procedure for resetting a consumer's password in CalHEERS, from the first screen to the moment the consumer can get back in."
    sampleAnswer: "You log in to CalHEERS, go to the admin home page, hover over management and choose user accounts administration, which opens the search users page. You look for the person you need by username or by first and last name and then press the search button, and the organisation has to be set to exchange external or nothing comes back. You select the edit button on the user's row, and the pop-up offering a password reset or an unblock appears. You then either choose manual change the password, type the new password and its confirmation and select reset, or leave auto-generate selected and let the system produce one, which is unavailable when the account's preferred contact method is mail. Whatever you set is a temporary password: the consumer signs in with it, is made to create a new password before reaching the account home, and is told to change the password straight away."
    explanation: "Look for the route into the tool, the organisation field being set to exchange external, the two ways of supplying a password, and the temporary nature of the result with the consumer changing it at first sign-in. An answer that stops once the reset is done has missed the temporary-password point, and an answer that leaves the organisation field unmentioned has left out the trap that makes the search return nothing. Lesson section: Step 4: The service centre procedure in CalHEERS."
