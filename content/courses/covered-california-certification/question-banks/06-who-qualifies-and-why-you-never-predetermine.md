    prompt: "In your own words, describe what has to be true of a health plan before Covered California will offer it to consumers, and what every such plan provides."
    sampleAnswer: "Four things have to be true. The plan has to be approved by Covered California for sale through the marketplace; it has to provide the essential health benefits; it has to follow the established limits on cost sharing, meaning deductibles, copayments and out-of-pocket maximum amounts; and it has to meet the other requirements of the Affordable Care Act. On top of those four, every qualified health plan meets the law's requirement for having health coverage at all, which is called minimum essential coverage."
    explanation: "A good answer names all four conditions and then the minimum essential coverage point. An answer that stops at the essential health benefits has missed the cost-sharing limits and the remaining requirements, and an answer that gives the four conditions without the minimum essential coverage point has missed the second half of the question. Lesson section: Step 1: What a qualified health plan is."

    prompt: "Name the three criteria a consumer must meet to qualify for a qualified health plan through Covered California, and say which one allows for something that has not happened yet."
    sampleAnswer: "A consumer must be a citizen or national of the United States, or a non-citizen who is lawfully present in the United States; must be a resident of California or intend to become one; and must not be incarcerated, other than incarceration pending the disposition or judgment of charges. The second one is the forward-looking criterion, because intending to become a California resident is enough and the person does not have to have completed the move. The third one also has an exception built into it, for a person awaiting the disposition or judgment of charges."
    explanation: "Three criteria, all three needed. The intention belongs to the California residency criterion and not to the citizenship one, and the exception inside the incarceration criterion is worth naming too. An answer that gives two criteria, or that adds a financial condition such as an income limit, is wrong, because income and financial help are not part of the three. Lesson section: Step 2: The three eligibility criteria."

    prompt: "A consumer's income is low, they have never enrolled before, and they are unsure whether they want to ask for financial help. Do they still have a route into a qualified health plan? Explain."
    sampleAnswer: "Yes. Being eligible for financial help is not a condition of enrolling in a qualified health plan, so a consumer can buy one without any help at all. Help is a discount on the cost, not a ticket to the door. The training put the same point the other way round: a consumer does not need financial help to qualify, and a consumer who does not want it is still entitled to buy coverage on the standard terms."
    explanation: "Look for the separation between qualifying for a plan and qualifying for a discount on it. An answer that says the consumer must take financial help, or must qualify for it, has turned a discount into an entry requirement, and an answer that says the consumer should not bother applying at all has quietly predetermined the outcome. Lesson section: Step 3: Financial help is not a condition of enrolling."
