    prompt: "You have to place an outbound call to a consumer. Explain the order of the first two steps, and why the consent step cannot be skipped."
    sampleAnswer: "The first step is to greet the consumer and get their agreement to the recording, and the second is to authenticate them in CalHEERS according to their relationship to the primary contact, with the decision about whether the authentication succeeded coming after that. Consent comes first because the consumer on an outbound call never passes through the automated greeting that covers an inbound call, so staying on the line is not the agreement it would be otherwise; the representative has to say that the call is being recorded and get an explicit yes. Anyone who joins the call later needs their own consent as well. The same order applies to the outbound calls made to tell a consumer that documentation they uploaded is not acceptable, or to remind them that they still have to upload documentation, where the consumer is consented and then authenticated again."
    explanation: "A complete answer gives the order, the reason, and the rule about people who join later. An answer that puts authentication first has the order backwards, and an answer that says consent is required because of a rule rather than because the consumer never passed through the automated greeting has missed the mechanism behind it. Lesson section: Stage 2: Consent comes before authentication"

    prompt: "Explain how you decide whether an outbound caller has been authenticated, and what you must not do when the answer is that they have not."
    sampleAnswer: "Authentication succeeds when the caller correctly answers three of the authentication questions and meets the extra criteria that go with their relationship to the primary contact, and you then carry on with the case review talking points. It fails when the answers are wrong or the extra criteria are not met, and then you release no information to the consumer, make no account or case changes, provide no line information and reset no account password. The recovery steps are the ones used on an inbound call: ask for the details as they appear on the caller's application or other official documents and check whether a document matches, offer to correct the application if the caller can be authenticated that way, say plainly that you cannot authenticate them with the information provided while still offering help with general questions, point them at the website to update their own information, and suggest that the primary contact call 800-300-1506 to add the caller as an authorised representative."

Lesson section: Stage 4: Deciding the result, and what failure means"

    prompt: "Explain the two results the quick sort calculator can give, what happens to the case in each, and what the consumer gets in each."
    sampleAnswer: "You answer the six quick sort questions and press Submit, and the calculator gives either a Transfer result or a No Transfer result. A No Transfer result opens a Quicksort Complete window and the case stays open for this request, so you go on with the application: the eligibility step, plan selection and enrolment for the appropriate family members, explaining the next steps and asking whether the consumer has any questions. A Transfer result means the consumer, or another member of the household, may potentially be eligible for Medi-Cal; a window opens with drop-downs for county and language, you choose the consumer's county of residence and their language preference, and the consumer is transferred to the county for continued help with the application. On a transfer the case is closed once the consumer has been handed over, with the resolution reason showing a quick sort transfer to the county."
    explanation: "A complete answer gives both results, what stays open and what gets closed, and the fact that the transfer result sends the consumer to the county rather than ending the call. An answer that describes the two results but not their effect on the case is half an answer, and an answer that treats a transfer as a dead end has missed that the county is where the application continues. Lesson section: Stage 6: The two quick sort results"
  - id: "b0f1fc56-7cce-46d3-abe7-e1060a3a2143"
    revision: 1

    type: free-recall
    prompt: "Explain what you may and must not say to a consumer during a quick sort transfer, and what happens to the call."
    sampleAnswer: "You must not tell the consumer that they are eligible for Medi-Cal, because the quick sort does not decide eligibility; you may tell them that the consumer might qualify for Medi-Cal, or appears likely to, and that the county will decide once they are transferred. You must not give them the county telephone number or the tracer ID that appear on the transfer window, because they are for your own use in making the transfer, and a consumer who calls that number instead will be turned away by the county and sent to the number that should have been given to them, the one on the DHCS listing. The call itself is a warm transfer: you stay on the line, introduce the consumer to the county representative, and release the call only once the county representative has said that they are ready to assist and the consumer is confirmed as still on the line. You then close the transfer window and confirm in the system that the resolution reason reads as a quick sort transfer to the county and that the status is closed."
    explanation: "A complete answer gives the two things that must never be said, the wording that is allowed, and the sequence of the handover. An answer that only covers the telephone number leaves out the eligibility wording, which is the easier of the two to get wrong, and an answer that describes a cold transfer has lost the coordinated handover the training is explicit about. Lesson section: Stage 7: The transfer, and the things you never say"
