    prompt: "Who is in the household for a mixed-program family, and who is left out?"
    sampleAnswer: "The household is the tax household: the person who files the return, their spouse, and every person they claim as a tax dependent. A spouse or tax dependent is included even when they are not applying for coverage, and anyone who is not claimed as a tax dependent is left out. Financial help is worked out from the household's total income rather than only from the income of the people applying. Everyone applies together, and then each person is assessed individually."
    explanation: "Look for the tax basis of the household, for the rule that people who are not applying still count, and for the individual assessment of each person. An answer that makes the household everyone living in the house, or only the people who are applying, has got the rule wrong. Lesson section: Step 2: Who counts as the household."

    prompt: "Why does a family most often end up split across the two programmes, and what is the federal poverty level used for?"
    sampleAnswer: "The most common cause is that Medi-Cal applies different income limits to adults and to children. The federal poverty level is the annual income measure that the Department of Health and Human Services sets, and it is what those limits are expressed as a percentage of. Children qualify for Medi-Cal with household income up to 266% of it, while most adults qualify up to 138%. A family at 250% is therefore above the adult limit and below the child limit, which is the usual way a household splits."
    explanation: "Look for the difference between the adult and child limits, for the two percentages, and for the description of the federal poverty level as an annual federal income measure. An answer that puts the split down to the county running two programmes separately has missed the mechanism. Lesson section: Step 3: Why families split across the two programmes."
