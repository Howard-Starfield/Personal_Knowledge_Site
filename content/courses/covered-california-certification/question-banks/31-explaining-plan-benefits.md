    type: free-recall
    prompt: "Define a deductible, a copay, coinsurance and the annual out-of-pocket maximum, and say what each one does to a bill."
    sampleAnswer: "A copay is a flat amount paid for a covered service, whatever else is going on with the rest of the bill. Coinsurance is the consumer's share of a covered service, given as a percentage of the amount the plan allows. The deductible is what a consumer must cover on their own for covered services until the plan begins sharing, and it does not attach to every service. The annual out-of-pocket maximum is the ceiling on what the consumer pays for covered services in a plan year; once it is reached, the plan pays the covered costs in full for the rest of that benefit year."
    explanation: "A correct answer defines all four and keeps them apart. A copay is a fixed amount for one covered service, coinsurance is a percentage of the allowed amount, the deductible is the amount paid before the plan begins to share, and the out-of-pocket maximum is the yearly ceiling on the consumer's own spending for covered services. The wrong turns that fail are collapsing the deductible into the maximum, because the maximum is a ceiling for the whole year while the deductible is a gate in front of the plan paying anything, and treating the copay as a promise that every visit of the same kind costs that figure.
