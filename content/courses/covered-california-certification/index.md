> [!note] Study notes based on the owner's own Covered California enroller training recordings. Original explanations; not affiliated with or endorsed by any organisation. Program rules change — verify anything time-sensitive against the official Covered California site.

This course turns the owner's training recordings into durable study material. It has two halves. The first is organised the way the curriculum is organised: the law that created the marketplace, then the products it sells, then who can buy them, then when, and finally the conduct rules that govern the help you give. The second half is the operational material — how a case is worked, what the portal does, and what to say on a call — and it is where most of the traps live.

Every lesson follows the same shape. A short explanation of the idea in plain language, a worked example, a checkpoint you answer from memory, and a set of questions that make you retrieve rather than reread. The course ends with a page of things that are easy to get wrong, because that is where the marks are.

The two halves are rehearsed differently, and `content-draft/study-guide.md` §21 explains why: the first half is a set of facts to recall, the second half is a set of sequences to perform in the right order.

## How this course works

- Each lesson teaches one unit in small stages: the idea, an analogy, the precise rule, a worked example.
- After each stage you get a retrieval checkpoint — a short quiz that asks you to recall rather than reread.
- Each lesson ends with a quiz covering the questions the checkpoints did not use.
- Every lesson asks you to explain it back in your own words, which is the fastest way to find the gaps.

## A note on how the source was handled

The training recordings are the source of truth for what was taught. Where a number in a recording has since changed, the recording's figure is kept and labelled with the year it came from, and the current figure is given alongside it. Where the official site says something the recording did not cover, that is marked as an addition rather than blended in silently. Every such decision is recorded in the fact-check report for that lesson.
