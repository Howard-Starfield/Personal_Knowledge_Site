Meetings often mix three different jobs: finding facts, comparing options, and
making a commitment. A short decision record separates those jobs before the
conversation starts.

> [!abstract] Example content
> This article is original mock material for testing the public blog. It does
> not come from an imported course or private source.

## Start with the decision

Write one sentence that names the choice and its deadline. "Discuss a new note
system" is a topic. "Choose whether to replace the current note system before
the next course begins" is a decision.

Then record the smallest set of facts needed to make that choice.

| Field         | Question to answer                                 |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Decision      | What choice must be made?                          |
| Options       | Which realistic alternatives are still open?       |
| Criteria      | What matters enough to change the result?          |
| Evidence      | What do we know, and how did we learn it?          |
| Uncertainty   | Which assumption could reverse the recommendation? |
| Reversibility | What would it cost to undo the choice?             |
| Review date   | When will new evidence justify another look?       |

The record does not need to predict every consequence. It needs to reveal the
reasoning that supports the choice now.

## Keep observations separate from preferences

"The import took 40 minutes" is an observation. "The import is too slow" is a
judgment that depends on a threshold. Write both, but do not merge them.

This distinction makes disagreement useful. One person may challenge the
measurement. Another may accept the measurement and disagree about the
threshold. Those are different problems.

## Ask what would change the recommendation

A decision record is weak when every possible result supports the preferred
option. Name at least one observation that would change your recommendation.
If no realistic evidence could change it, write that the choice is a
preference rather than an evidence-led decision.

Before commitment, use [[decision-tools/pre-mortem-before-commitment|Run a pre-mortem before commitment]] to look for failure modes that the record missed.

After commitment, use [[decision-tools/experiments/change-one-variable-at-a-time|Change one variable at a time]] when the decision can be tested in a bounded trial.
